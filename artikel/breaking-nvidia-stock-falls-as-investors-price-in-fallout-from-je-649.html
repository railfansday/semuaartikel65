📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia's stock slid 4.12% Monday in the wake of CEO Jensen Huang's resignation, leading a broader chipmaker selloff after the chip giant lost hundreds of billions in market cap last week.

The continued decline since Huang's resignation brought Nvidia's stock to its lowest close since July 1, 2025.

Huang resigned from Nvidia, the manufacturer's board announced on Thursday, after mounting scrutiny following reports that he had a relationship with a woman believed to be a Chinese spy. Huang told employees he had an affair in an email on Nov. 8.

It has been revealed that it was later reported that Huang had romantic relationships with two Nvidia employees. Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment on Friday.

Recent findings suggest that an online petition calling for Huang's reinstatement as Nvidia CEO has drawn more than 170,000 signatures, as prominent tech executives, including Elon Musk, rallied behind him. A source familiar with the matter told CNBC, however, that Nvidia's board of directors is not considering bringing back Huang.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, U.S. lawmakers are pushing to launch comprehensive investigations into the scandal.

Huang cofounded Nvidia in 1993 and led the firm through its meteoric rise to becoming the world's most valuable company, with its AI chips underpinning the technology's boom.

Nvidia appointed longtime finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO and said its board would consider both internal and external candidates in its search for a permanent successor.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent on Sunday called for a "dramatic shakeup" at Nvidia, adding that the board should look outside the organization for its next chief executive.

He also suggested other Nvidia executives should face scrutiny over the chip giant's compliance with U.S. export controls.

— CNBC's Caleigh Keating contributed to this report.

Tech Report: UK government forced to disclose secret Apple backdoor order

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that the U.K. government has been ordered to confirm that it issued a secret order demanding Apple to provide authorities with encrypted customer information.

The Thursday ruling from the U.K.’s Investigatory Powers Tribunal, an independent court that allows citizens to challenge government surveillance orders, is a rare rebuke for the government, which sought to keep the encryption backdoor order a state secret.

The tribunal’s ruling rejected the Home Office’s claim that disclosing the order would be damaging to national security.

Industry observers note that the ruling comes a year after the U.K. government reportedly demanded that Apple build a backdoor to allow U.K. authorities access to customer data anywhere in the world. Details of the so-called “technical capabilities notice,” which are issued under strict non-disclosure rules, leaked to the media, which drew ire from White House officials and U.S. lawmakers who criticized the effort, arguing that the U.K. order would put Americans’ privacy at risk.

In a recent development, california-based Apple refused the order, and blocked U.K. users from switching on its Advanced Data Protection data-encryption feature for iCloud, rather than comply with the demand. The U.K.’s secret order was later narrowed to allow access to British nationals’ data stored in Apple’s encrypted cloud, and Apple also challenged this second order.

Industry observers note that prior to the ruling, Apple was legally barred from disclosing the order, even after details leaked. When reached for comment, Apple told TechCrunch that it welcomes the decision to make public the fact that the U.K. Home Office issued a technical capability notice.

Apple previously mentioned that it has “never built a backdoor or master key” to any of its products or services and “never will.”.

A spokesperson for the U.K. Home Office, which has yet to confirm the existence of a notice, did not respond to a request for comment.

Rights group Liberty International, which brought the legal challenge on behalf of two individuals, stated that this is the first known time that the government has been compelled to confirm the existence of a secret technical order.

A final hearing on what specific order details can be released is anticipated next year.

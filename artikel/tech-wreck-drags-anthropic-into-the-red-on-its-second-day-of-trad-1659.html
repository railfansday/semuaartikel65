Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.

The Anthropic honeymoon is over.

Despite its debut-day rally, the broader markets plunged around the stock, and it's now catching the tech cold - down in premarket trading on its second day as a public company.

It's day 2 and Anthropic shares are under pressure.

The stock is falling in premarket trading, at one point down over 6%.

The stock had closed 17% higher in its first day of trading, easing back from a near-40% jump earlier in the session.

Speaking exclusively to CNBC's Andrew Ross Sorkin after ringing the bell at the Nasdaq, CEO Dario Amodei defended the firm's massive $900 billion in AI commitments, saying, "If you look at the amount of cash we have on the balance sheet and the amount of revenue that we're already making per year, suddenly those commitments don't seem so large.".

Nasdaq futures are back in the red, underperforming both the Dow and S&P in early trading.

Nvidia is lower in premarket trading, alongside AMD.

It's a deeply negative handover from Asia and Europe.

Sources indicate that japan's Softbank suffered its worst trading day in over two years as the AI sell-off swept through the Asian tech sector. Shares in CXMT, SK Hynix and others ended the day deep in the red.

In Europe, chip stocks like ASML, Infineon and others suffered heavy selling.

Recent findings suggest that the debate over the export of U.S.-made chips to China has divided the Trump Administration's cabinet. The Nvidia scandal has further exposed this split and fanned the flames between the two camps.

CNBC's Morning Squawk recaps the biggest stories investors should know before the stock market opens, every weekday morning.

Bitcoin hit a 4-month low amid the broader tech sell-off, putting further pressure on cryptocurrencies.

Gold's bad year is getting worse.

Precious metals investors can be forgiven if they feel like the past year has given them whiplash.

Following a torrid run in which the price of gold hit newly introduced records 53 times in 2025, it hit a historic high of $5,589.38 an ounce on Jan. 28, only to take an almost equally historic nosedive. On Wednesday, gold fell below $3,800 for the first time since September 2025, roughly a third below its peak.

Sources indicate that A convergence of factors pushed gold to historic highs earlier this year, according to Suki Cooper, global head of commodities research at Standard Chartered, the London-based multinational bank. Now a different convergence is bringing the price down.

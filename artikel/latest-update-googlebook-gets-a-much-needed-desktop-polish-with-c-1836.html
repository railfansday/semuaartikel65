Latest Update: Googlebook gets a much-needed desktop polish with Chrome 157

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Google is giving its fledgling Googlebook OS a hefty dose of desktop polish with the rollout of Chrome 157. The company has published the full changelog for the browser update, which includes five major features and 22 under-the-hood fixes.

Industry observers note that since the launch of Googlebook for consumers earlier this fall, Google has been slowly smoothing out the rough edges of its latest Android-meets-desktop platform. With Chrome 157 being the primary interface on Googlebook OS, the little desktop quirks — a right-click menu that doesn’t work correctly or a tab rail that refuses to stay fully extended — appear even more glaring than they would on a traditional laptop. This release directly addresses those day-to-day friction points.

Recent findings suggest that the biggest changes are aimed at those who favor vertical tabs over Chrome’s traditional horizontal layout. Before, if a vertical tab rail collapsed, it was difficult to tell which pages were open without expanding the entire sidebar. That’s what Chrome 157 fixes with Auto-Expand Vertical Tabs on Hover, which pops open the rail temporarily when you hover over it with your mouse or trackpad. You move away, and it drops down again.

According to recent reports, you can also resize the vertical tab bar by hand to allow for longer page titles. This option requires you to disable auto expansion, but Chrome remembers your preferred width between browser restarts and newly introduced windows.

Recent findings suggest that google has also simplified navigation of Chrome’s Settings. You can now bookmark or share individual sections such as Appearance and Privacy, as each settings page has its own URL. The browser’s Back and Forward buttons also function when navigating between settings pages.

It has been revealed that chrome 157 adds a persistent control bar when sharing browser tabs during video calls. If you’re presenting via Google Meet, you can stop sharing or switch to another tab without having to dig through menus.

The system's native Save As dialog is enabled by default for actions like saving images, links, and webpages. You have more control over the file names and destinations, including Google Drive and external storage.

In a recent development, the other 22 improvements cover minor irritations. Right-click the address bar, and you get the standard desktop text editing menu. Press Ctrl+L and you get the entire URL with parameters. Chrome also fixes bookmark context menus, improves PDF translation, and allows downloaded PDFs to open directly in a browser tab.

Sources indicate that other fixes include external monitor selection for Google Slides, Picture-in-Picture media controls, web app navigation, and third-party Google Sign-In prompts that might freeze when dismissed. Contextual Search also receives a less intrusive privacy notice.

This massive enhancement comes after last week's broader Chrome 157 launch and a huge system patch in November. The update doesn’t come with a slew of flashy additions, but its suite of practical fixes should make Google’s browser more comfortable to use.

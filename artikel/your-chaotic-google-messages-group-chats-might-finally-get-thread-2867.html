Google Messages might be getting a much-needed upgrade that could make long conversations much easier to follow.

Google is stated to be working on a feature called threaded replies, which will enable customers to keep conversations tied to a specific message, rather than having replies scattered around an increasingly chaotic chat, as spotted by the folks at Android Authority. It’s particularly handy for group conversations, where several people can be talking about different things simultaneously, and nobody knows which message they’re actually replying to.

Recent findings suggest that the current setup isn’t really suited for conversations that split into a few smaller ones. Google Messages displays a preview of the original message above each reply, but it can get confusing after a long exchange when unrelated messages begin appearing between responses.

According to recent reports, google’s proposed fix appears to be visually linking related messages. In the chat bubbles, you’ll see a vertical line on the left side that will show which messages are grouped together. A single reply is a straight connecting line; longer exchanges with multiple replies have a small loop in the line. The system works with images and emojis along with text.

Furthermore, Google is also adding reply counters beneath messages in active conversations. That counter can be tapped, and Messages will open a dedicated thread view. The other conversation fades into the background, and you only see the related messages. You have a separate reply box to continue the conversation, as well as an X button to go back to the main conversation.

Industry observers note that this should make it much less frustrating to catch up on busy group chats. You can jump right into the conversation that matters, without having to scroll through dozens of unrelated messages to get the context of someone’s reply.

According to recent reports, the similarities to Apple’s iMessage are difficult to ignore. Apple already has threaded conversations that split out replies from the main chat, and Google seems to be doing something similar. That’s not necessarily a bad thing, though. Messaging apps should strive to make conversations easier to follow, regardless of who first brought an idea up.

For now, threaded replies are an experimental feature hidden in the development code of Google Messages. Google hasn’t declared a release date, and there’s no guarantee the feature will make it to the stable version. But its relatively polished implementation suggests the organization is making meaningful strides.

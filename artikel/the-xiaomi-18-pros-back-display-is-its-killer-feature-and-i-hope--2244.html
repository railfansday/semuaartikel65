The Xiaomi 18 Pro's back display is its killer feature — and I hope it comes to more Android phones

🌐 Source: Androidcentral • 📅 23 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 6 min read (1244 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • This is a major year for Xiaomi as the new Xiaomi 18 Pro and Xiaomi 18 Pro Max are launching globally.
  • Except, the Xiaomi 18 Pro has a serious secret weapon: an innovative back display.
  • In a recent development, it's not the first Xiaomi Pro phone with a rear display, but it is the first to come out globally, and it was worth the wait.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

This is a major year for Xiaomi as the new Xiaomi 18 Pro and Xiaomi 18 Pro Max are launching globally. I've been using them for a week, and normally I’d promptly settle on it being “an excellent flagship smartphone that's super capable, but doesn't excite me as much as a folding phone.".

Except, the Xiaomi 18 Pro has a serious secret weapon: an innovative back display.

In a recent development, it's not the first Xiaomi Pro phone with a rear display, but it is the first to come out globally, and it was worth the wait. I'm absolutely blown away, it's so good, Google should make it a key part of Android.

Here's what it can do, why it's so good, and the feature that shows the future of Android smartphones.

In a recent development, xiaomi isn't the first to experiment with second screens. Android has long had attempts to add extra screens, side edges for notifications, or Glyph displays/notification lights to tell you something.

Yet all of these felt like gimmicks. The closest we've come to a genuinely usable second screen is the Moto Razr, and that's because it's a folding phone and the cover display is just a small phone screen. None of them were inherently useful and even if they were at first, they promptly lost their utility.

It turns out that there's a good reason for that. The onus is generally on the firm to provide an infinite number of options and use cases, and then predict what else you might like.

Get the latest news from Android Central, your trusted companion in the world of Android.

It has been revealed that then there's the added problem of persuading developers to revision their apps to use this real estate or, more likely, to create latest apps for it. Since the second iPhone, it's been clear that apps and experiences matter a lot for feature adoption, and a secondary display is no exception.

Industry observers note that it turns out there's a great answer to all of these problems: AI. That's the magic that makes this second screen inherently useful – giving you something you'll actually use multiple times a day.

It has been revealed that have you heard of vibe coding? It’s a term used to describe building apps and software with vibes, aka describing changes and having AI make them, rather than coding the changes yourself. I’ve been using it all year to build several platforms and apps, and it’s revolutionary. You can type simple sentences, and AI will make the changes.

According to recent reports, google and Xiaomi have taken this concept further with a newly introduced feature called GenAI apps. As the name suggests, it stands for Generative AI apps, or using AI to generate entire apps with one message. This is the secret to making the secondary display more useful: instead of relying on a tech giant to build everything you need, you can build it yourself.

We’ve all been there: you’re stuck in the doctor’s waiting room, lying in bed, or otherwise just looking for something to do. Sure, you can play yet another game, or you can have some fun and create fresh things to try on the back display. It takes three to five minutes to build a polished, optimized app specifically for the back display.

Simply put: GenAI apps are magnificent, and a glimpse of how we’ll use our phones in the future.

The Xiaomi 18 Pro makes regular phones as exciting as a folding phone.

Industry observers note that think about how useful this is: playing DnD and you’ve lost the dice? Create latest dice in just a minute on your phone. Want to pin your shopping list for when you go shopping? Easy. Love flipping coins? There’s an app for that. I created one in 3 minutes; it’s that easy – and the GenAI app's chat window had it as one of the suggested apps.

Sources indicate that it’s not just about creating your own apps: Xiaomi has built a series of useful, fun, or interactive apps that are easy to use, from an activity and workout tracker to a ninja swipe game, and many more in between. The only real downside? You can install a maximum of 12 at a time, but it is super easy to install or remove them in just a few seconds.

Recent findings suggest that since Xiaomi first built the rear display, I’ve been waiting for them to give this a wow factor that makes it a necessity. GenAI apps offer that wow factor, since you can build whatever you want in just a few minutes. Yes, there are monthly limits on how many apps you can create, but nonetheless, I can’t stop using the Xiaomi 18 Pro back display.

Industry observers note that as you might expect, there’s one further added benefit: it’s the perfect viewfinder for selfies. The back display solves the selfie problem: front cameras are usually lower quality than rear cameras, and rear cameras usually don't let you see what you are capturing (unless you're using a foldable).

The Xiaomi 18 Pro rear display lets you use the full camera UX, including capturing in portrait or landscape based on the phone's orientation when you’re capturing. It’s essentially the same as using the main display, and surprisingly useful for selfies, especially as you can use gestures like holding your palm up to capture the photo.

Beyond that, Xiaomi added built-in support for Google Maps, Spotify, and YouTube Music on the rear screen – letting you glance at turn-by-turn directions or control playback without waking the main display – alongside widgets, panels, and a range of wallpaper and clock styles.

One of my favorites lets you display different inspirational quotes, and considering how positive affirmations can be for your mental health, it’s a great face to have. There’s also a customizable Always On Display for glancing at pinned notes, widgets, or the time while your phone sits face-down.

Sources indicate that I’m so enamored with the Xiaomi 18 Pro and its rear screen that there’s only one thing I want: for Google to make this the default in Android. Every Android phone maker should adopt this; Google should add full support in the core OS, and the Play Store should include a classifier for back-display apps.

Industry observers note that this would solve the other key problem: you can build apps, but you can’t share them with anyone else. A future marketplace or way to surface apps other people have built would build momentum and also let people sell, earn, and do more. Considering how themes are often sold for a low price and theme stores are incredibly popular, I can see how this would be a win for all owners.

In the meantime, I’m off to build some more Gen AI apps. The only thing limiting me? Deciding what to build next.

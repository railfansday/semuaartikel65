Tech Report: Trump bought Nvidia, Palantir, SpaceX in September as federal deals, White House ties grew

President Donald Trump's investment accounts bought shares of Nvidia, Palantir and Elon Musk's SpaceX in September, around the time the companies secured major federal contracts and their executives met with the president, according to a newly released financial disclosure.

Recent findings suggest that the 25-page filing indicates 751 purchases and sales totaling between roughly $40.9 million and $148.1 million, according to a CNBC analysis. Purchases accounted for at least $29.2 million and sales at least $11.7 million. The filing reports transactions in broad dollar ranges, leaving the exact amounts and value of Trump's holdings unclear.

Among the transactions were Palantir purchases before the company received two federal contracts worth $42.5 million combined, according to contracting records, SpaceX investments shortly before and after a $946 million NASA contract expansion, and Nvidia trades as its then-CEO, Jensen Huang, cultivated a close relationship with Trump.

Recent findings suggest that the trades underscore a striking feature of Trump's presidency: His personal fortune remains invested in companies whose fortunes his administration can shape. His accounts reported more than 21,000 trades in 2025, compared with roughly 500 during his entire first term. Recent presidents have generally divested individual stocks or used blind trusts to avoid potential conflicts.

The White House has told CNBC previously Trump and his family have no control over the trades, which outside financial managers handle independently. The Trump Organization indicates the arrangement is designed to avoid even the appearance of a conflict. There is no evidence Trump directed the transactions or that his managers knew government decisions in advance.

Sources indicate that white House spokesman Davis Ingle said that Trump's portfolio is independently managed and that neither the president nor his family has any input on trades. "All investment decisions are made entirely by independent managers," Ingle told CNBC.

The Trump Organization did not immediately respond to a request for comment.

Sources indicate that the timing was particularly notable at Palantir, the artificial intelligence software maker whose technology is used by U.S. military, intelligence and civilian agencies.

Trump's accounts bought between $15,001 and $50,000 in Palantir shares on Sept. 2, followed by another purchase of between $1,001 and $15,000 on Sept. 9.

A week later, the Department of Housing and Urban Development awarded Palantir a contract with $10.4 million in initial obligations for AI-powered grant oversight and fraud detection.

Sources indicate that on Sept. 29, the Department of Homeland Security awarded Palantir another $32.1 million software contract, bringing the two awards to roughly $42.5 million. That same day, CEO Alex Karp joined Trump, Musk, Huang and other technology executives at the White House for a meeting on artificial intelligence.

Trump's accounts also bought shares and debt in SpaceX in the weeks surrounding a major NASA contract award.

Recent findings suggest that on Sept. 1, the accounts purchased between $100,001 and $250,000 in SpaceX debt carrying a 5.35% interest rate. Eight days later, they bought between $1,001 and $15,000 in shares of the major NASA and Pentagon contractor.

On Sept. 18, NASA announced a $946 million expansion of its SpaceX contract, adding three astronaut missions to the International Space Station and bringing the agreement's total value to $5.92 billion. The agency had signaled intends for additional missions in May.

Six days after the announcement, Trump's accounts bought another $15,001 to $50,000 in SpaceX shares on the same day Musk attended Trump's White House state dinner for Chinese President Xi Jinping. The filing does not show whether the purchase came before or after the dinner.

The trades followed an even larger SpaceX investment in August, when Trump's accounts bought between $1 million and $5 million in the company's debt just two days before he signed a policy aimed at expanding commercial space transportation.

Trump's accounts also continued buying Nvidia shares as Huang drew closer to the president — a relationship now under congressional scrutiny over Huang's ties to a woman suspected of spying for China.

Recent findings suggest that the September filing demonstrates four Nvidia purchases totaling between $152,004 and $380,000, including two on Sept. 2 worth as much as $350,000 combined.

The accounts also sold between $1,001 and $15,000 in Nvidia shares on Sept. 24, the day Huang also attended the state dinner with Xi.

The trades bring Trump's Nvidia transactions to at least 108 since his return to office in 2025, worth between $27.2 million and $97.9 million, extending a CNBC analysis published Monday.

Recent findings suggest that the relationship between the president and Nvidia's then-CEO has drawn fresh scrutiny after The Upcoming York Times reported that intelligence officials warned Trump about Huang's relationship with a suspected Chinese spy. Trump has denied being briefed. Huang resigned Nov. 12 following revelations about his personal relationships.

Sources indicate that the September filing also shows substantial trading beyond the three companies. The largest reported transaction was a Sept. 23 purchase of between $5 million and $25 million in Vanguard Dividend Appreciation ETF shares.

That same day, Trump's accounts sold between $1 million and $5 million each in Comcast, McDonald's, Home Depot and Fidelity National Information Services, along with between $500,001 and $1 million each in Nike and Constellation Brands.

According to recent reports, on the buy side, Trump's accounts purchased between $250,001 and $500,000 in TJX shares on each of two consecutive days, Sept. 15 and 16. They also bought between $117,004 and $330,000 in Tesla shares across four transactions, along with smaller investments in CoreWeave, Intel, Microsoft and Apple.

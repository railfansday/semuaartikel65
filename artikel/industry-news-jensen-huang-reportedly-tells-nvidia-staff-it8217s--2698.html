Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang apologized to staff during his first all-hands since returning to the organization, while attempting to distance himself from the narrative that the White House and tech leaders helped him get his job back, according to reports from Bloomberg, The Fresh York Times, and The Handbasket’s Marisa Kabas.

During the meeting, Huang reportedly called claims that President Donald Trump was directly involved in his reinstatement — an idea publicly promoted by Trump himself — “completely untrue.” He added, “Don’t think for a second politics played a role in the company. We are the most apolitical company,” according to Kabas.

According to recent reports, along with attempting to dismiss Trump’s involvement, Huang reportedly said he also didn’t return because he was a “tech bro” who was “above the law” or “above the code of conduct.” He added that, “no story has been more tortured than this one in human history,” Kabas reports. Instead, Huang said the Nvidia board “evaluated the entire situation,” while receiving assurance from the government that he posed no national security threat, according to the NYT.

In a recent development, huang resigned as CEO of Nvidia on November 12th, following reports of affairs with an alleged Chinese spy and two employees, including vice president of healthcare Kimberly Powell. On Monday, Nvidia declared that it had reinstated Huang as CEO, president, and member of the board — a decision the White House remarked Trump was “totally involved in.” Trump also confirmed that he worked with Vice President JD Vance and other officials to bring Huang back. A swath of tech leaders, including Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney, and former White House AI czar David Sacks, were also in favor of Huang’s return.

Recent findings suggest that in a post on Truth Social, Trump said that the government “has no reason to believe” Huang’s return poses a risk to national security, adding that the US attorney general “does not possess evidence” that he engaged in “unlawful conduct.” Nvidia is still facing an investigation over its accounting practices, according to Reuters. The tech giant is also carrying out its own internal investigation surrounding reports that Huang was the target of a Chinese spy plot. Huang is set to testify before Congress on December 1st to answer questions about national security concerns, and Democrats are already planning to press Huang about his return, as well as Trump’s involvement.

According to recent reports, even after all this, Huang reportedly mentioned he “doesn’t believe in succession planning.” He also acknowledged that he violated Nvidia’s code of conduct, adding that he is attending therapy daily, Bloomberg reports. “I made some terrible mistakes,” Huang said, according to the NYT. “I was unfaithful to Lori, and I hurt my kids and of course, I hurt all of you.”.

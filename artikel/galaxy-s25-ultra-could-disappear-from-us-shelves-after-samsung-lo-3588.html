📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Samsung risks a U.S. ban on the import of some Galaxy S25 models over a camera patent dispute. According to Bloomberg Law, the US International Trade Commission (USITC) ordered Samsung to stop importing products that infringe a Maxell patent.

On Nov. 24, the commission issued a limited exclusion order and a cease-and-desist order against Samsung Electronics America. In its official notice, the USITC confirmed a violation relating to claims 1 and 10 of Maxell’s US Patent No. 11,490,004. It threw out infringement findings on four other patents in the probe.

Industry observers note that the row is over technology in a patent called “Mobile terminal.” Maxell, a Japanese electronics firm, complained in December 2024 that Samsung was using patented technology in mobile phones without permission. The investigation officially started in January 2025.

There is an vital distinction between the ruling and an immediate retail ban. The orders are subject to a 60-day presidential review period that began Nov. 25. By setting the bond at 0%, the commission decision means Samsung can continue importing covered products without posting a bond in that window.

Industry observers note that samsung has a workaround of sorts. Some of the accused products did infringe the patent, but Samsung’s redesigned products did not, the administrative law judge ruled in July. So, Samsung's revamped camera experience could matter, but the ultimate scope of the exclusion order will depend on whether incoming devices actually infringe the patent.

Samsung seems to have already fixed the controversial camera tech in redesigned products. The USITC's ruling specifically separates those devices from products found to infringe Maxell's patent, but the agency has not confirmed if current Galaxy S25 owners have received a corresponding software update.

There’s also some protection for current owners. The commission specifically excluded service, repairs, and warranty replacements for products purchased prior to the end of the presidential review period. So even if the exclusion order stands, owners won’t suddenly lose access to Samsung repair services.

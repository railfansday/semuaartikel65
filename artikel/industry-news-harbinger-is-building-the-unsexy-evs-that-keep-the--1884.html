Industry News: Harbinger is building the unsexy EVs that keep the economy moving

🌐 Source: The Verge • 📅 21 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 5 min read (915 words, 📚 Deep Dive)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

While legacy automakers tap the brakes on electric vehicles, one startup is hitting the accelerator. Earlier this month, electric truck maker Harbinger raised $240 million in a Series D round, capping a banner stretch that includes a $300 million order from FedEx and a new defense contract with the US military.

According to recent reports, harbinger is stacking up wins by skipping the consumer EV hype cycle altogether and focusing on the unglamorous workhorses that keep the economy running: Class 4–6 delivery vans and box trucks. The firm’s thesis is simple: go after the overlooked commercial segments where legacy trucks are weak and giant automakers can’t be bothered to compete, CEO John Harris tells me.

“We’re not solving the big obvious problem because everyone’s going to solve that problem, and as a result, the margins get kind of ground down to zero,” Harris claims. “We’re solving problems that — they’ve just been there forever and you no longer think of this as a problem, you just think of it as like part of existence. It’s like a tax on your life.”.

Founded in 2021 by veterans of Canoo, QuantumScape, and Faraday Future, Harbinger aims to fill a structural gap in the auto market, in which most passenger EV platforms lack the durability to scale up while the heavy-duty platforms are too heavy and expensive to scale down.

Recent findings suggest that “In auto, you have Class 1 companies and you have Class 8 companies,” Harris says. “And everything in between is kind of a wasteland... It is much, much, much too small for a major company to serve.”.

Industry observers note that which is not to say the industry hasn’t tried. Ford and GM both took shots at electrifying the medium-duty segment with the E-Transit and BrightDrop delivery vans, respectively, but sluggish sales and the loss of federal incentives forced them to discontinue or cancel those models.

According to recent reports, harris indicates that major automakers are ill-suited to task, with their over-reliance on third-party suppliers to build all the necessary components. The effort becomes too spread out and expensive, with not enough return on your investment. That’s why Harbinger is attempting a more vertically integrated approach, he says, assembling its own battery packs and drive units in house — with inverters to follow.

It has been revealed that “If you are not vertically integrated in auto, you don’t build viable products,” Harris states. “We believe that electric vehicles are only viable when they’re competitive on acquisition price with combustion vehicles.”.

In a recent development, of course, Harris and his fellow cofounders have tasted failure in their previous ventures. Harbinger’s CTO Phillip Weicker cofounded EV startup Canoo, which ran out of cash and filed bankruptcy in 2025. Harris himself spent a year at tumultuous Chinese EV company Faraday Future before moving on to Xos Trucks and then Palmer Luckey’s Anduril.

It has been revealed that “I was an engineer at Faraday. I wasn’t in charge of anything,” Harris states. “So it’s not like, ‘John, why did Faraday not succeed?’ I can tell you lots of reasons, but it wasn’t because the plastic part that I designed didn’t work. It was because the plastic part that I designed was going into a vehicle for which the world customer base was like three people.”.

Industry observers note that those companies “fundamentally didn’t work,” he adds, “because of the market fit concern and the leadership issue. Not because the engineering didn’t work or electrification wasn’t ready or they didn’t have enough capital.”.

Harbinger’s approach is more pragmatic, offering both battery-electric and plug-in hybrid powertrains for its medium-duty trucks. The manufacturer is not pursuing heavy-duty Class 8 trucks like the Tesla Semi, however, as Harris argues the economics of electric long-haul trucking are unworkable.

“Class 8 electrification to me is one of those markets. I completely reject the notion that that’s a good fit for electrification for the majority of the market,” he says. “We can put [the Tesla Semi] on the watch list with the Cybertruck.”.

With companies like Amazon and FedEx making serious moves to secure more electric delivery vehicles, Harbinger is well-positioned to be a key supplier. And with diesel prices skyrocketing as a result of the war with Iran, more companies are waking up to a reality in which EVs can help bring down their costs.

According to recent reports, harbinger isn’t just building delivery trucks — it’s also branching into energy storage and military technology. For example, the company supplies battery power systems to Airstream and partners with American Rheinmetall and Forterra to build self-driving hybrid supply vehicles for the US Army. Harbinger also recently bought Phantom AI to bring advanced driver-assistance features to commercial fleets, in the hopes of giving companies a clear, measurable return on their safety investments.

With millions in fresh capital and major contracts already on the books, Harbinger has cleared the first major hurdle that tripped up its founders’ past ventures. Now comes the hardest part in the auto business: proving it can actually build them at scale.

Recent findings suggest that “Auto is a significant enough opportunity that it can get people’s attention,” Harris claims. “It’s also punishingly difficult. So if you can make something that works in auto, it will be phenomenal in every other segment, because every other segment, the competition is essentially weaker.”.

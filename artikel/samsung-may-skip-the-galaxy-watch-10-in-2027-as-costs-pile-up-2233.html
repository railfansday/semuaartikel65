Samsung may skip the Galaxy Watch 10 in 2027 as costs pile up

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Samsung could be skipping its annual smartwatch debuts, and the Galaxy Watch 10 could be the first casualty.

According to a fresh report from Korea’s ETNews, the company is considering a two-year release cycle, which means that buyers may need to wait until 2028 for its next major smartwatch upgrade.

Sources indicate that the report said Samsung is rethinking its hardware release strategy as mounting manufacturing costs weigh on its mobile business. The mobile unit has posted losses for two quarters in a row, making it harder to justify the costly annual product launches.

Sources indicate that the reported change would bring to an end a nine-year tradition dating back to the original Galaxy Watch in 2018. If Samsung follows through on its blueprint, the Galaxy Watch 9 and the Galaxy Watch Ultra 2, both scheduled for 2026, could be its last flagship wearables until 2027.

The Galaxy Watch 9 reportedly struggled to meet sales expectations after Samsung increased its price compared with the Galaxy Watch 8. That’s not surprising, given that buyers aren’t seeing enough meaningful improvements to justify paying more for another annual upgrade.

Stretching the release cycle could allow Samsung to reduce research, development, and marketing costs while allowing its engineers to spend more time working on substantial hardware improvements.

Releasing a fresh smartwatch is not a guaranty that Samsung will leave customers waiting for improvements. The tech giant is reportedly planning to focus on software updates and new health features for existing Galaxy Watch models throughout 2027. A cheaper Galaxy Fit fitness band might also help keep its wearable line moving.

According to recent reports, the move is stated to be part of a wider cost-cutting effort at Samsung. The company is also reportedly looking at cutting production of its Galaxy A smartphones by about 20% and shifting focus to its higher-margin Galaxy S27 Pro and Ultra devices.

Industry observers note that samsung has not officially confirmed the reported changes. But fewer rolls out a year might be a good thing for consumers if the organization takes that time to offer upgrades worth paying for. What really matters is whether Samsung can improve its current watches without sacrificing long-term software support as yet another casualty of its cost-cutting looks.

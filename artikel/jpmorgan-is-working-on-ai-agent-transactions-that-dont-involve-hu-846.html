JPMorgan is working on AI agent transactions that don’t involve humans

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Recent findings suggest that artificial intelligence agents have already proven to be helpful, or cause a lot of trouble, or both. One area seen as high-potential is in payments, where — if they're able to be trusted — the agents could deal with all sorts of money matters on behalf of people.

That's why JPMorgan Chase and South Korea's Hyundai Card are testing how AI agents can handle financial transactions without direct human intervention, including whether they can eventually negotiate and make payments with one another.

The companies have completed an initial proof-of-concept involving a simulated foreign exchange transaction and are targeting the first half of 2027 for a more complex phase involving multiple AI agents.

Wee Kee Toh, global head of Kinexys Insights at Kinexys by JP Morgan, told CNBC that the next step is to test what happens when autonomous agents move beyond execution and begin interacting with each other in an ecosystem.

"That, I would say, is really the biggest part of what we want to get as an expansion of the phase one scope," he added.

It has been revealed that the initial trial involved a Hyundai Card AI agent requesting a foreign exchange transaction to maintain euro liquidity in its Kinexys blockchain deposit account. JPMorgan stated it was the first private blockchain-based autonomous bank-agent-to-client-agent test for corporate treasury management.

While the initial trial has been completed, JPMorgan has yet to set a timeline for commercializing the technology.

Toh noted building the technology itself was relatively straightforward, but establishing appropriate governance and internal controls proved more difficult.

"Technology-wise, it's really not the biggest hurdle," he mentioned, pointing instead to questions around accountability and the risks of allowing AI agents to execute financial transactions without direct human oversight.

To address those concerns, Kinexys developed a framework that restricts AI agents to predefined transaction parameters, automatically rejecting requests that fall outside authorized limits.

Sources indicate that "You are going to draw a box around the agent. The agent will never be able to do anything out of the box," Toh remarked.

For example, an AI agent authorized to transact up to $5 million would be blocked from executing transactions above that threshold, he remarked.

According to recent reports, the companies are also exploring whether to extend their collaboration to other businesses within Hyundai Motor Group, whose broader operations were one reason JPMorgan selected Hyundai Card as a partner, Toh said. No specific projects have been announced.

Hyundai Motor Group holds a combined 78.04% stake in Hyundai Card through Hyundai Motor, Kia and Hyundai Commercial, according to the card issuer's website.

Hyundai Card has previously experimented with blockchain-based cross-border payments. In July, the manufacturer noted it completed a proof-of-concept involving a $20,000 transfer using the USDT stablecoin between Hyundai Motor's U.S. and Mexican subsidiaries, with the transfer and verification process taking an average of seven minutes.

Industry observers note that kinexys has also been expanding its blockchain-based payments business. The JPMorgan unit mentioned in August that it had processed more than $4 trillion in transactions since inception, averaging about $7 billion a day.

Separately, JPMorgan released a proof-of-concept for its dollar-denominated deposit token, JPMD, in June 2025, before making it available to institutional clients that November.

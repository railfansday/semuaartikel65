Starting Nvidia’s quarterly earnings call on Tuesday, fresh board chair Harvey Jones said that when former CEO Jensen Huang resigned, “the Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO.”.

Jones added, “We will not be addressing or taking questions on today’s call regarding the circumstances of Jensen’s resignation and the ongoing investigation.”.

Recent findings suggest that huang had publicly floated the idea of returning earlier on Tuesday. In a statement posted to X, he wrote that he “did not live up to the standards expected of everyone” at the company, that he regretted his mistakes, and that he would cooperate with the Nvidia board’s investigation into his actions. He added that he’d be willing to return to the chief executive role if offered it, though a day earlier, the Nvidia board reportedly said it was not considering bringing him back. “NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company,” Huang wrote. “If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”.

But since his November 12th resignation, Huang remains entangled in scandal. He’s reportedly had three affairs, two with subordinates at Nvidia, one with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset suspected of facilitating illicit chip sales. (Huang has pushed against restrictions on Nvidia chip sales in China.) He’s scheduled to testify before Congress on December 1st, and an internal company investigation is ongoing.

In a recent development, tech industry heavyweights have still voiced their support for Huang, dubbing him “irreplaceable” and petitioning for his return. Some have cast doubt on the allegations that Alice Huang (no relation) was spying on behalf of China and painted the leaks about their relationship as a conspiracy against the former CEO. President Donald Trump, a close ally of Huang for much of the past year, has also backed him. On Monday, Trump remarked he “would not mind” Huang returning, adding that “I think he learned a big lesson — I guess maybe one lesson is more important than another.”.

According to recent reports, trump’s closeness with Huang could also complicate things for Nvidia, however. The manufacturer has ramped up its lobbying in Washington, D.C., spending $5 million in 2025 and $4.7 million so far in 2026. But as Democrats prepare to take Congress, they’re planning to investigate Trump’s dealings with Big Tech and other industries, alleging potential corruption. “The election wasn’t very positive for AI, and [the news around Huang’s resignation] is only going to complicate things, especially for the company in particular but for AI as a whole going forward,” Brendan Glavin, director of insights at OpenSecrets, told The Verge in an interview last week.

Sources indicate that lior Prosor, managing partner at Deep33, a tech VC firm, said last week that he didn’t expect Huang’s resignation to pop the AI bubble, although Huang has had a significant hand in mobilizing funds for the AI infrastructure buildout in the U.S. Under Huang’s leadership in August, Nvidia entered into partnerships with six financial institutions for about $500 billion to fund such endeavors. “NVIDIA supplies roughly 75 percent of the AI compute hardware the industry consumes, and demand continues to dramatically outpace supply,” Deep33’s Prosor said in an interview with The Verge. “Neither its customers nor its competitors can easily replace the capabilities it provides, and the economic incentives driving the AI trade remain unchanged.”.

Even so, a potential market cooling has some worried, as Anthropic’s IPO, slated to potentially be the largest of all time, is set for Thursday. The chipmaker had agreed to buy $10 billion in Anthropic shares via private placement, on top of a $10 billion funding promise Nvidia had already made last year.

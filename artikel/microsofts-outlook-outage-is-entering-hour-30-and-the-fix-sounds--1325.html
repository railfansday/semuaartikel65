Microsoft's Outlook outage is entering hour 30, and the fix sounds like a server room game of whack-a-mole (Fixed)

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Revision (Nov 16, 11:41 am ET): Microsoft has finally fixed the issues that took down most of its 365 services.

Update (Nov 13, 6:57 am ET): Microsoft claims recovery efforts are now in phases, with some affected infrastructure already back online and traffic slowly returning to normal. Initially, the firm blamed high infrastructure loads across many regions, but the investigation eventually revealed a more specific problem with automated server health checks. The tech giant has revealed that automated server health checks misfired, kicking healthy infrastructure offline and triggering cascading connection failures across Exchange Online, Teams, and Copilot. There are automated health checks that can detect failing hardware and take it out of rotation before your inbox breaks. Instead, a particular server component that does those checks started failing its own tests. Multiple regions lost their healthy servers, and the rest of the infrastructure buckled under heavier-than-usual loads, preventing consumers around the world from loading their mailboxes or sending messages. Now, Microsoft’s recovery playbook relies on working around its referee. Engineers made a targeted change to work around the faulty health-check component on some servers to see if they would stay online, while rolling out safeguards to stop other machines from dropping out. Automated recovery systems have been activated to minimize the disruption, and teams are also working in parallel to rebuild the affected servers with previous configurations.

Over a hundred thousand frustrated consumers were left with broken inboxes and unable to get work done as Microsoft Outlook went down on Thursday, November 12.

Outlook users in the Americas began reporting they could not access their Exchange Online mailboxes, and the complaints were rolling in fast, with Downdetector reporting more than 125,000 complaints as of this writing.

According to recent reports, if you attempted to refresh your inbox only to be greeted by infinite loading screens, you were not the only one. Customers are reporting being completely locked out of their accounts across desktop apps, web browsers, and mobile clients. Microsoft’s official 365 Status account confirmed the hiccup on X, blaming Exchange Online, the cloud backbone that powers your Outlook email, calendar, and contacts.

We're investigating reports of people experiencing issues accessing Exchange Online mailboxes through multiple connection methods in the Americas. More details can be found in the Microsoft 365 admin center under EX1550543.November 12, 2026.

The company initially said the connection failures were limited to the Americas, but the company later confirmed that the connectivity bug could be hitting owners in just about any region.

The problem is due to the way Outlook clients authenticate and connect to Exchange Online mailboxes using different connection methods.

Microsoft engineers are currently scrambling to resolve problems on the server end and mitigate the impact, but the company has not yet provided a concrete timeline for a full fix.

Apps in Microsoft 365 that rely on the same service connectivity may also be slow or throw error codes until the dust settles, though Exchange Online takes the biggest hit. For now, the best course of action is to keep an eye on the official Microsoft 365 status feed, enjoy a rare, guilt-free excuse to ignore your boss’s emails, and wait for the servers to wake back up.

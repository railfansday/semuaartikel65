1Password confirms backend data breach — here’s what hackers actually stole

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

1Password has confirmed that its internal systems have been compromised, exposing the personal information of a subset of customers.

In a blog post, the manufacturer admitted it had been breached after a hacker took over an employee's GitHub account, exposing customer names, email addresses and account recovery tokens. However, your actual passwords remain securely stored, per the disclosure. The company disclosed the intrusion on November 25, six days after first spotting the suspicious activity.

Sources indicate that the attacker used the compromised corporate GitHub account to access private source code repositories and compromised 1Password’s CI/CD build system and pushed malicious code to backend services. That rogue code exfiltrated admin records for a subset of customers, including user IDs, last sign-in times, public keys, SRP parameters and tokens from emailed invitation and recovery links.

According to recent reports, 1Password says your encrypted vaults, Secret Keys, master passwords and browser extensions were not accessed. Its architecture ensures that even its servers can't read your vault keys, so the hacker got nothing from the password perspective.

Stolen recovery tokens may sound scary, but they can't be used to unlock an account without single sign-on credentials or an emailed verification code. To stop brute-force guessing, 1Password extended those email codes from six digits to 10 alphanumeric characters.

1Password has disabled the hijacked GitHub account, rotated internal credentials, tightened endpoint controls and hired outside cybersecurity investigators since discovering the attack on November 19. Management says there’s no evidence of actual account takeovers and has directly notified users with exposed pending tokens, but it’s unclear if every affected customer was alerted.

At this time, you do not need to change your master password or Secret Key. But keep your phishing radar on high alert: The firm warns that scammers now know your name, email and that you use 1Password, giving them prime tools for convincing fake recovery emails. It’s worth noting to never enter your credentials through an email link; rather, open the official 1Password app directly.

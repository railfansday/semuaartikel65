TL;DR: T-Mobile is offering up to $2,100 off Apple's foldable iPhone Duo via 36 monthly bill credits — with no new line or trade-in required — when you commit seven Picks on a qualifying T-Mix plan.

According to recent reports, we were not expecting to see any meaningful Black Friday discounts on Apple’s first foldable smartphone, but we ended up with a full 100% wipeout. That's the sort of deal that makes Black Friday stop and stand still.

T-Mobile quietly upgraded its Apple iPhone Duo promotion from a $1,200 discount to a full $0 per month offer via 36 monthly bill credits, with no trade-in required. Wow. When you build a qualifying T-Mix blueprint with at least seven Picks, the 256GB iPhone Duo drops from $1,999.99 ($55.56 per month) to absolutely nothing.

At $1,999.99, the iPhone Duo is easily the priciest smartphone in Apple’s lineup. Getting this phone for free without surrendering a current flagship phone or opening an unnecessary extra line is by far the best iPhone deal to drop over Black Friday.

We need to be very clear that while T-Mobile advertises "no new line or trade-in needed," a single-line T-Mix proposal caps out at three Picks (which only knocks $1,200 off, leaving you paying $799.99). Unlocking seven Picks requires a multi-line T-Mix account (specifically, at least three lines starting around $200 per month with AutoPay).

Recent findings suggest that you will also pay upfront sales tax on the full $1,999.99+ retail price plus up to a $35 device connection fee at checkout, stay on your plan for all 36 months to keep the bill credits, and wait out a short backorder (current shipments are estimated for Dec. 9-15). If you're happy to jump through those hoops, the iPhone Duo is yours for $0 per month.

In a recent development, it might be worth the hassle. Mashable's Stan Schroeder used the Duo for a month and remarked: "The advantages of having an iPhone with a massive, beautiful, iPad mini-like display in your pocket simply outweigh all of the compromises that you have to make with the Duo.".

How to permanently delete your PlayStation account

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Closing your PSN account is permanent and means you’ll lose all your games, trophies and save data forever.

Sources indicate that you might decide to delete your PlayStation account if you're giving up gaming, switching to PC or protesting Sony's looks to stop producing physical games. And while deleting your account isn't difficult, it's a major step you should evaluate carefully before you carry it out.

There's a lot tied to your PlayStation account that you'll lose, especially if you've been gaming on Sony's systems for multiple generations. Make sure you really don't want to keep your account before moving on, as the deletion is irreversible after a short grace period. In case you're done using the account, consider handing off the credentials to a friend, child or other family member, so your digital collection doesn't end up going to waste.

According to recent reports, in case you have a PlayStation Family set up, deleting your account will also close all child accounts it manages. Also, your PSN ID (online username) can never be used again.

You can close your PlayStation account using the PlayStation app on your phone. Sign in if you're not already, then tap the Settings gear at the top-right of the Play tab. Near the bottom of the page, select Close Account, after which you'll have to walk through a series of steps to confirm the closure.

It has been revealed that if you're using any other Sony services with your account, you'll see a warning that you need to close them first. To do that, tap the Apps and services link that appears to step through each of them.

You also have the option to close your account through the web. Visit PlayStation Support and explain that you want to close your account — you'll need the details to prove it's you. You'll likely start with a chatbot, which should connect you with a real person once it determines what you're looking for.

Industry observers note that if you just need to remove a user from your PS5, it's not necessary to delete your account entirely. To erase a user on your console, go to Settings > Customers and Accounts > Delete Users. Select the profile in question and confirm. This will erase their saved data, along with screenshots and videos, so make sure to have anything important backed up (either to PS Plus cloud storage or a USB drive) before doing this.

After removing your profile from a console, other consumers on the system can no longer gameshare your PS5 library. To check this option before erasing the local user, visit Settings > Users and Accounts > Other > Console Sharing and Offline Play. When this is set to Enable, anyone else on the console can play games you've purchased. Upon signing into your PSN account on a new system, you'll be able to re-download your games and any data saved to PlayStation Plus, then set it as your new home PS5 so others can access your library on it.

If you ever need to de-authorize a console that you don't have access to, you'll have to sign into your PlayStation account management page and use Device Management > PlayStation Consoles > Deactivate All Devices, which only works once every six months.

Industry observers note that and if you're planning to sell or give away your PlayStation, you should factory reset it at Settings > System > System Software > Reset Options. Choose Reset Your Console and confirm. This wipes the system: all consumers, games, save data and media captures will be gone, but it doesn't delete your PSN account. Use it only if you don't plan to keep your system.

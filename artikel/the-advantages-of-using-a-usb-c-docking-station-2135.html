Plus, there are differences between a USB-C hub and a docking station.

Laptops have been getting thinner and lighter from year to year, and they have become so powerful that many people choose a laptop as their main computer. But a thin body means fewer ports, and a desktop setup with a screen, mouse, webcam and keyboard requires a lot of them. If you have a laptop and still want the full desktop experience, a USB-C docking station can be the solution you're looking for.

A USB-C docking station is a box that connects to your laptop through a single USB-C cable and gives it a full set of ports. USB-C docking stations reduce cord clutter, add more ports to your setup and can keep your laptop's own ports from wearing down.

According to recent reports, the biggest advantage of a dock is simplicity. One single cable connects the laptop to its peripherals. That means you can come home and plug in all your devices with one plug; you can also grab your laptop on your way out just as quickly. Monitors, a keyboard, a webcam and a charger no longer need to be reconnected every time the laptop comes and goes, and your desk stays tidier because the cables run to the dock instead of the laptop.

A dock can also add ports your laptop doesn't have. Depending on the model, you can get HDMI or DisplayPort outputs for external monitors, more USB ports, an SD card reader and an Ethernet port for a stable wired internet connection. A thin laptop that comes with three or four ports can end up with a dozen.

Industry observers note that the third advantage is less wear on your laptop. Plugging and unplugging several cables over and over puts strain on the ports, and a dock takes that job over. Instead of plugging several cables into the laptop every day, you plug in just one.

According to recent reports, if you only need a few basic connections, a USB-C hub is the cheaper option. Hubs have roughly four to 10 ports and cost anywhere between $30 and $150. Docking stations generally have more ports, come with their own DC power adapter, and cost more, with some higher-end models costing more than $400. A dock can give you a full desktop setup with multiple monitors, plus more ports of every kind, and the higher-end Thunderbolt 4 and USB4 models can run multiple displays, Ethernet, audio and laptop charging through that one cable. Before buying, it's worth checking a few things, because not every dock works with every laptop. A USB-C port only outputs video if it supports DisplayPort Alt Mode or is a Thunderbolt or USB4 port, and the dock needs to support Power Delivery pass-through at your laptop's required wattage. If you plan to work with the lid closed, keep the power plugged in, since the laptop draws more power with an external monitor attached.

Get those details right and a dock quietly does its job, providing a laptop that goes back to being portable by unplugging just one cable.

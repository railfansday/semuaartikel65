It's worth trying if you're having issues.

If you're experiencing slow performance or other issues with your Google TV, you may have heard about clearing the cache. Caches generally help Android devices such as Google TVs run more smoothly by storing data — such as TV show thumbnails — that may need to be accessed rapidly later. While this may not be an issue on a device with plenty of storage, very few Google TV devices have more than the bare minimum there. The Google TV Streamer that replaced the Chromecast has 32GB of space, the bare minimum for an Android device, making cache management all the more crucial.

Recent findings suggest that even though Google TV manages app caches automatically, they can still get too large, especially if you have a lot of apps installed or store large video files on the TV or streaming box. App caches can also bloat or get corrupted, causing complications including slow performance, app crashes and frustrating system glitches.

Sources indicate that thankfully, Android makes it easy to clear the cache, whether for individual apps or all installed apps, through its settings menu. If your Google TV has become sluggish or glitchy over time, clearing the cache is a troubleshooting step you shouldn't skip.

Industry observers note that there are two ways to clear your Google TV cache: system-wide or per-app. If you're experiencing slowdowns or other challenges across the entire operating system, use the former option. The latter is best for when only a single app is giving you trouble.

To clear the cache system-wide, open Google TV settings by going to the home screen, holding down the home button on your remote or selecting your profile icon, and selecting the gear icon at the top of the quick settings panel. Go to System > Storage > Internal shared storage > Cached data, then select OK to clear the cached data for all of the apps on your device.

If only one app is acting up, clearing all the cache is overkill. Instead, go to Settings using the steps in the above paragraph, then select Apps > See all apps. Scroll to find and select the app you're having challenges with, then select Force Stop and confirm by selecting OK. Next, select Clear Cache and confirm by selecting OK. If you still have issues, it's worth clearing the cache system-wide using the above steps. If even that doesn't work, try rebooting the Google TV. Finally, if you still have trouble, go back to the app's settings page and clear both its cache and data. This will erase everything and return the app to its default state, so only use it as a last resort.

According to recent reports, while clearing the cache for all apps system-wide can make your Google TV device more responsive in the short term, it's a temporary fix for the real problem: insufficient storage. As noted earlier, Google TV devices rarely come with that much storage, and installing dozens of apps will rapidly eat through it.

In a recent development, it's generally good practice to keep approximately 10 percent of an Android device's storage empty. This buffer gives the system breathing room for operations that keep the drive healthy and to build robust app caches. If your Google TV storage is too full, clearing the cache likely won't resolve all your performance issues.

According to recent reports, after clearing the system-wide cache, go to Settings > System > Storage > Internal Shared Storage to see how much space your apps, movies, photos and other files are taking up. The remaining free space will be shown at the bottom. If 90 percent or more of the drive is full, it's time to start deleting things.

If media is the culprit, back the files up and delete them. For most Google TVs, though, the space-hogging culprit is likely installed apps. A good way to start is by deleting any apps you haven't used in a long time, especially if they're taking up an inordinate amount of space. Keeping that streaming service you downloaded to watch a single movie isn't worth the frustration of dealing with a slow TV.

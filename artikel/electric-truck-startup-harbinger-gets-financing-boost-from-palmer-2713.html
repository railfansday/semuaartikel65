📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Harbinger has tapped Palmer Luckey’s Erebor Bank as a upcoming source of capital as the electric truck startup looks to diversify how it funds its growth and an expanding portfolio of products spanning autonomous vehicles, energy storage, and defense.

Los Angeles-based Harbinger noted Tuesday it secured more than $100 million in financing, including a convertible note from Pinegrove Venture Partners and senior debt from Erebor Bank, which launched in February 2026 and recently surpassed $1 billion in loans, according to the Financial Times.

According to recent reports, the fresh financing comes less than three weeks after Harbinger raised $240 million in a Series D funding round co-led by Tiger Global, Capricorn’s Technology Impact Fund, and Ridgeline. FedEx, which ordered 2,000 trucks from Harbinger, also participated.

In a recent development, harbinger co-founder and chief executive John Harris explained in a statement that building an industrial technology manufacturer at scale requires a “different financing approach,” adding that the capital needed to ramp up production and deliver vehicles to customers shouldn’t have to come entirely from equity. Harris noted that major vehicle and aerospace manufacturers have long relied on debt financing to support their production operations.

It has been revealed that erebor is now Harbinger’s primary banking partner. In a statement, Luckey said traditional lenders have struggled to support the unique capital needs of businesses developing real technology and creating U.S. jobs.

“Harbinger is exactly the kind of business we built Erebor to back: an American manufacturer building world-class technology,” he mentioned.

Industry News: Flipkart CEO Kalyan Krishnamurthy to step down next week amid IPO uncertainty

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Kalyan Krishnamurthy (pictured above), who steered Flipkart through Walmart’s acquisition in 2018 and helped build it into one of India’s biggest e-commerce companies, is stepping down as chief executive next week after more than 11 years with the firm.

On Wednesday, Flipkart remarked it would announce Krishnamurthy’s successor in the coming weeks, and chief product and technology officer, Balaji Thiagarajan, will lead the firm until a replacement is found.

A Walmart spokesperson told TechCrunch that Krishnamurthy’s last day at Flipkart will be December 4.

Krishnamurthy joined Flipkart in 2015 from Tiger Global, one of the organization’s early investors, and became its CEO in 2017. He remained at the helm after Walmart acquired a controlling stake in the company for $16 billion in 2018.

Under Krishnamurthy, Flipkart said its business grew more than five-fold. The company has more than 500 million registered people and over 1.4 million sellers, including those on Shopsy, its low-cost shopping platform. The company’s marketplace offers over 150 million products across more than 80 categories.

In a recent development, flipkart has of late moved into newer segments, including quick-commerce through Flipkart Minutes and, more recently, food delivery through a venture called Eat-in by Minutes. The expansion is targeted at Indian consumers who are increasingly turning to quick-delivery services for everything from groceries to electronics.

It has been revealed that thiagarajan joined Flipkart in September last year to lead its product and technology teams. Before that, he had founded Kasu.ai, a Bay Area startup working on AI assistants for corporate finance. He also previously worked at Google, Microsoft, and Uber in senior engineering positions, per his LinkedIn profile.

Krishnamurthy’s exit comes amid uncertainty around Flipkart’s IPO. In March, the company moved its headquarters from Singapore to India as part of its preparations to go public, but in May, Walmart reportedly told Flipkart to delay its IPO and focus on becoming profitable first.

Recent findings suggest that walmart declined to comment beyond its announcement when asked what prompted Krishnamurthy’s departure, and whether the leadership change would affect Flipkart’s IPO prepares. Flipkart did not respond to a request for comment.

Industry observers note that founded in 2007 by Sachin and Binny Bansal as an online bookstore, much like its bigger rival Amazon, Flipkart eventually expanded into other categories. Under Krishnamurthy, it has grown into India’s largest online retailer by gross merchandise value, ahead of Amazon.

Recent findings suggest that “Both Flipkart and Walmart are better because of Kalyan. We are grateful for his leadership, the governance he instilled and his many contributions to the company,” Chris Nicholas, president and CEO, Walmart International, remarked in a statement.

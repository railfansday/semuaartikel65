Your PC's USB port can charge your phone, even when it's turned off — Here's how

🌐 Source: Engadget • 📅 22 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 4 min read (721 words, 📚 Deep Dive)
💡 Key Takeaways
  • Your USB ports might have a hidden "Always On" mode that charges your phone with the PC shut down.
  • Almost all PC USB ports supply enough current to charge a device, but not all continue to do so when the PC is shut down.
  • Depending on the system, USB standby power might be on by default or be part of an energy-saving setting that cuts power when the PC is off.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Your USB ports might have a hidden "Always On" mode that charges your phone with the PC shut down.

Almost all PC USB ports supply enough current to charge a device, but not all continue to do so when the PC is shut down. This convenient feature can also be confusing, since each brand tends to have its own terminology. For example, Lenovo calls it an "Always On" port while Dell uses the term PowerShare. Others stick with icon labels, like a battery symbol or a lightning bolt. The best way to know for sure is to dig into your PC's manual, confirm the markings and learn how to enable it.

Depending on the system, USB standby power might be on by default or be part of an energy-saving setting that cuts power when the PC is off. Your desktop or motherboard documentation should have this information. Fortunately, testing the feature is fairly simple: plug your phone in, turn the PC off and see if your phone continues charging.

Recent findings suggest that if it stops charging, it's safe to assume the setting is off, which you can rectify in the BIOS or UEFI on most Windows PCs. (If your PC never supported the feature, changing settings won't help.) Restart the PC and press the key the startup screen tells you to (usually F2, Del or F10). Look under Advanced, Power or Chipset for a USB power delivery or Always On charging option and enable it. If you see ErP (Energy-related Products) or Deep Sleep settings, make sure they're disabled. Save and exit.

It has been revealed that you don't need a computer science or software engineering degree to do this, just a little patience as you wait for the PC to boot up and press the right key. The menu can be confusing at times, but you're unlikely to brick your PC.

Recent findings suggest that for starters, energy-saving features may get in the way. Anything that uses standby power will conflict with firmware settings that cut power to reduce consumption. For instance, if ErP Ready is enabled in select ASUS NUC PCs, USB standby power during shutdown or hibernation is disabled. Check your system's instructions before changing anything.

It has been revealed that you also may not have to turn off every single energy-saving feature just to charge your phone when your PC is off. Keep in mind that changing the energy-saving settings won't add post-shutdown charging if the system never supported it to begin with.

Industry observers note that laptops are an entirely different case, since they often come with battery reserve limitations. Depending on the model, USB charging may stop when the laptop's battery hits a reserve threshold. Laptop charging features may depend on an AC adapter after shutdown, and that's a potential restriction that shouldn't be confused with a desktop's standby power settings. Make sure your USB plug is completely in the port and that the cable is reliable, not one that's been sitting in an attic box for the last quarter century.

In this context, "Always On" refers to power availability, but that's not the same thing as "charging only." A supported port can continue to supply power while the computer is off, though the wording shouldn't be taken literally. Firmware settings can disable that power, and the behavior may be different between ports on the same system.

It has been revealed that power and data transfers are also different capabilities. A phone charging while the PC is shut down doesn't mean you can transfer photos from your phone or copy files to it. The specific feature here is all about electricity available via the USB connection.

Another problem is the lack of consistency in names, setup processes and USB port symbols. One system may offer a dedicated USB power setting while another's ports depend on power-saving configurations. Even within a single brand, capabilities and terminology can differ, and manufacturers implement "Always On" USB in drastically different ways.

For example, "Always On" could mean the feature is active after shutdown, during hibernation or while your PC is asleep. It could mean two of them, just one or all three. The feature may come with symbols, color coding, terminology or fine print in a manual, but not necessarily all of the above.

One UI 9.5 could save your Galaxy S24, S25 from the 9.0 update's battery problems

🌐 Source: Androidcentral • 📅 26 Nov 2026 • ⏱️ 2 min read (444 words, 📖 Standard)

  • According to recent reports, samsung has confirmed that the battery health issue many customers have experienced in One UI 9 will be fixed with its One UI 9.5 upgrade.
📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that any massive software update can come with its share of problems. For Samsung, those problems appeared in the form of battery drain. A recent update suggests a fix is in the works.

A user in Samsung's Turkish community reiterated problems they've experienced since downloading One UI 9 (via Android Authority). The user states battery health "dropped to abnormally low levels on many devices..." After the user raised the issue with Samsung, the firm issued an official response, saying it is working on it.

According to recent reports, samsung has confirmed that the battery health issue many customers have experienced in One UI 9 will be fixed with its One UI 9.5 upgrade. The user adds that this problem occurred "even on newer devices." The situation remains somewhat mysterious. Samsung says the issue "will be resolved." However, the problem raised—the issue users have reported—is about battery health.

Sources indicate that the publication questioned this as well, wondering whether One UI 9 was misrepresenting devices' true battery health. If the opposite is true and batteries were truly damaged, that's an entirely different situation, as SamMobile mentioned. While it's good to see Samsung is at least working toward a solution, we're still far off.

Industry observers note that one UI 9 was delayed (*exasperated sigh*), and its arrival on some older phones wasn't great. Once the patch hit Galaxy S24 phones, consumers promptly noticed severe battery drain. An S24 user stated that, under "heavy use," their device's battery plummeted ~94% in about five hours. While, yes, they were using their phone often, the drop was excessive. Another Galaxy S25 user reported that their phone, which sat idle most of the day, dropped 40%.

Regarding the battery health/degradation issue, the One UI 9.5 beta for the Galaxy S26 only started last week. According to the changelog, Samsung is giving testers a chance to experiment with App Lock (protect apps with biometrics), improved Pro camera controls, Mindful Note journaling, and more. Knowing how Samsung's beta programs go, it will be a while before anyone sees relief from the current software woes.

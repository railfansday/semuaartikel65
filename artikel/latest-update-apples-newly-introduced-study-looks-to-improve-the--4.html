Recent findings suggest that apple announced a newly introduced study today with the goal of better understanding how people interact with conversational AI health tools and how useful their responses actually are. The manufacturer is conducting the Apple Health AI Usage Study in collaboration with OpenEvidence, a clinical AI platform used by healthcare professionals that generates answers based on medical information from sources like The New England Journal of Medicine. Participants will use a new iOS app to ask medical questions and then rate the responses based on criteria including accuracy, detail, and relevance.

The app is now available for download, and enrollment starts today, with Apple looking for up to 200,000 people to contribute. Participants 21 and older must be based in the US, and although the app doesn’t use Apple Intelligence, it does require an iPhone 15 Pro running iOS 26 or later.

In a recent development, study participants can use the app to ask health-related questions and receive easily understandable responses with links to peer-reviewed clinical sources. The app relies on text-based prompts, and while dictation is an option using the iOS keyboard, the app isn’t integrated with Siri or Apple Intelligence.

Industry observers note that during enrolment, participants are given the option to share Apple HealthKit data, like their activity, sleep, or clinical lab results, so responses will include more context about their own personal health. But privacy is a priority, and Apple states it has taken measures to ensure users’ information remains confidential and the data collected won’t be used to train AI models. The aim is to improve the design and functionality of AI-powered health tools so they can deliver relevant health information that’s easier for users to understand and act on.

“At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life. Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey,” remarked Sumbul Desai, MD, Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness.

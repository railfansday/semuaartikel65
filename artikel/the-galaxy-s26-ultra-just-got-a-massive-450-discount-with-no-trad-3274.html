In a recent development, samsung recently raised the price of the Galaxy S26 Ultra, making an already expensive smartphone even more expensive, with a starting price of nearly $1,400. But if you've been waiting for the right time to upgrade to Samsung's latest flagship, the Galaxy S26 Ultra is now down to just $949.99 this Black Friday, saving you a massive $450.

What's particularly good about this deal is that you don't need to trade in your existing smartphone to get this price. You're getting the unlocked 256GB model for less than $1,000, which makes it a much easier phone to recommend.

Sources indicate that the Samsung Galaxy S26 Ultra offers a premium AMOLED screen with Privacy Display technology, a versatile flagship camera setup, built-in S Pen, Galaxy AI features, and 60W wired charging. The unlocked 256GB model is now $450 off, bringing its price below $1,000 without a trade-in.

✅Recommended if: You want Samsung's best flagship smartphone with a significant AMOLED display and Privacy Display tech, versatile cameras, S Pen support, and flagship performance for under $1,000.

❌Skip this deal if: You already own a Galaxy S25 Ultra, prefer smaller smartphones, or don't particularly need the advanced cameras and S Pen of the Ultra model.

It has been revealed that one of the biggest reasons to get the Galaxy S26 Ultra over other flagship smartphones is its display. Samsung arguably makes some of the best smartphone displays, but what makes the S26 Ultra particularly interesting is its Privacy Display feature. It lets you restrict viewing angles when you're using your phone in public, making it particularly useful when checking sensitive information while commuting or traveling.

Of course, you're also getting some of the best hardware Samsung has to offer. The Galaxy S26 Ultra runs on the Qualcomm Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 for Galaxy, which, while no longer the latest chipset, is still a powerful flagship processor with plenty of performance to offer for years to come.

There's more than enough power for demanding games and multitasking, while Samsung's One UI 9 (soon to be upgraded to One UI 9.5) offers a ton of useful features that you won't necessarily find on other Android smartphones.

And even though Samsung hasn't made particularly significant changes to battery capacity over the past few generations (the Galaxy S26 Ultra still ships with the same 5,000 mAh cell as the previous Ultra flagships), it has finally improved wired charging speeds with the Galaxy S26 Ultra. The phone supports up to 60W wired charging, making it considerably quicker to top up than its predecessors.

I've always found Samsung's Ultra smartphones a little difficult to recommend at their full retail price, especially when there are plenty of great Android flagships available for under $1,000. But with the Galaxy S26 Ultra now available for just $950 without requiring a trade-in, it's much easier to recommend Samsung's best flagship over the competition.

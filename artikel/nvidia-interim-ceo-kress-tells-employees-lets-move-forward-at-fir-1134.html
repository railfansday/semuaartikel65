Nvidia interim CEO Kress tells employees ‘let’s move forward’ at first all-hands meeting since Jensen Huang exit

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Interim Nvidia CEO Colette Kress told employees "let's move forward" on Wednesday during the manufacturer's first all-hands meeting since founder Jensen Huang resigned, according to an attendee who spoke to CNBC on the condition of anonymity.

Kress was joined by newly appointed board chair Harvey Jones and hyperscale and high-performance computing head Ian Buck.

Several other senior members of Nvidia's executive team spoke or joined Kress on stage, including longtime company veteran Jeff Fisher, SVP of software engineering Dwight Diercks, senior vice president of GPU engineering Jonah Alben, executive vice president in charge of operations Debora Shoquist and co-founder Chris Malachowsky, according to people familiar with the matter.

Kress opened with the message about moving forward and also mentioned pay raises and explained that the yearly review process would be moved up from March to December when employees typically get a pay raise and are granted more stock. Nvidia shares are down 37% from its 52-week high.

In a recent development, jones discussed the search for the next CEO, the attendee added. The meeting lasted about an hour and was in person as well as streamed on Microsoft Teams.

Sources indicate that the general sentiment swirling in the room, according to the attendee, was that employees want Huang to come back. Employees have spoken up on X in recent days to call for the return of Huang as CEO, and one senior employee reportedly sent a letter to the board warning Nvidia would lose "irreplaceable talent" if it doesn't consider reinstating Huang.

But another person familiar with the matter disagreed, saying that employees welcomed the remarks from leadership as well as the visibility into company leadership.

"The Nvidia board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO," Jones said during the earnings call on Tuesday.

Sources indicate that some executives shared that they do not agree with Jones. "Why make such a strong definitive statement when the investigation is still playing out," an employee told CNBC. The employee asked not to be named given the sensitivity of the subject.

The meeting comes one day after former CEO Huang remarked he was ready to return as CEO should the board want him. Multiple employees told CNBC that Huang's letter "fired employees up.".

It has been revealed that despite recent turmoil, Wednesday's meeting was not out of the ordinary. Nvidia typically holds all-hands the morning after quarterly earnings reports, according to a former employee who spoke to CNBC on the condition of anonymity. Kress recapped Tuesday's earnings report, calling it a "great quarter," according to one person familiar with the matter.

In past meetings, Huang took time to answer questions submitted by employees, according to the former employee. A person in the room told CNBC that the executives did not answer questions in Wednesday's all-hands.

Nvidia shares were down Wednesday, a day after the tech giant reported third-quarter earnings that topped Wall Street expectations and gave no signal that the company was going to slow its growth.

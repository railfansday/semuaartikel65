Google Messages always has something going on, be it a UI patch or the addition of a latest feature - and the messaging service could be rolling out not one, but two handy tools soon.

First up, it appears that Google Messages is getting some voice note improvements, as spotted by Android Authority in one of its recent APK teardowns.

In a recent development, sick and tired of voice note playback stopping when you leave a chat? Google is fixing this.

Sources indicate that at the moment, you can listen to voice notes and audio files when you switch to a different app, but it stops when you leave a chat and return to the main conversation list. Although it's not live yet, Android Authority claims it managed to enable a feature, which it notes allows audio playback to continue even when exiting a chat. This finally deals with a huge pain point, allowing you to listen to a voice note from one person when replying to another in a separate conversation.

Sources indicate that this is the biggest upgrade when it comes to voice notes and audio files, but it's not the only one Google is working on. Additionally, the outlet spotted new playback control buttons in the notification center, including new pause, play, and forward icons supporting 5- to 30-second skip and rewind.

It has been revealed that as far as aesthetics go, Google is tipped to roll out small but noticeable changes for icons within chats. If the newly introduced features roll out, the voice note icon could change from its current wave-like symbol to a standard microphone, which makes more sense, though I can see it being mistaken for a speech-to-text function.

Industry observers note that when you have friends and family constantly on your case, organizing your conversations can make a huge difference, but Google Messages' six-pin limit just isn't enough to suffice for some customers. Thankfully, this could soon be a thing of the past.

Android Authority carried out another deep dive into Google Messages' latest beta, finding strings that point to the possibility of Google lifting its long-time pin restrictions as well as reviving its old chat pins layout.

In a recent development, depending on how many chats you pin, the layout will switch based on this number. If you pin six chats or fewer, your conversations will appear in the current tile-based design. If you pin more than six, your chats will automatically switch to the app's previous list-based layout, which allowed you to pin up to 20 different conversations - though we don't know if that number will be reinstated for this upgrade; the strings in Android Authority's findings don't clarify that.

As it is with every teardown, it's not guaranteed that the features in development will see the light of day. There's always the chance that Google could abandon the ideas altogether. That said, if the upgrades remain on Google's release calendar, there's no doubt they'd be welcomed quality-of-life upgrades for those who rely on Google Messages to stay connected.

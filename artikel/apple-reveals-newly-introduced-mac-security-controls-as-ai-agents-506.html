Apple reveals newly introduced Mac security controls as AI agents raise privacy risks

As promised last month, Apple on Monday announced changes to Mac security controls designed to protect users’ data from the growing popularity of AI agents and their apps’ demand for “full disk access.”.

“Before granting an app such extraordinary access, consumers should understand that it puts all of the data on their Mac at risk,” Apple wanred in a fresh blog post aimed at app developers.

Unlike AI chatbots that simply answer questions, agents run on a user’s device where they can take actions across the operating system, files, and apps on the user’s behalf.

Sources indicate that while the tools can be useful, Apple has grown concerned about the increased risks to users’ data and privacy that these AI agents introduce. At the same time, the tech giant has been benefiting from sales of Mac minis and Mac Studio computers that AI early adopters have been purchasing specifically to run their local AI models.

To balance these competing interests, Apple said in October that it would revise some of its security settings around Mac apps. The announcement followed two notable incidents involving AI agents. In one, a journalist claimed that Meta’s Muse app read their private messages without consent; a separate report from Wired cited a flaw in ChatGPT’s Mac app that could have allowed hackers to access sensitive data.

Now, Apple is sharing the changes it’s making.

Industry observers note that for starters, it’s splitting up Device Control and Data Access into two settings: System & App Protection Override and Accessibility. The changes is designed to help customers better understand that selecting the first option means overriding built-in security protections.

Industry observers note that to streamline configurations, Apple says there will now also be a single method for granting permissions that involves selecting the requested app from a list. The manufacturer will also introduce newly introduced guidance encouraging customers to grant access only to the specific apps that need it when they enable the System & App Protection Override setting. Similar guidance will appear when users enable Full Disk Access to allow an app to access their folders.

The tech giant will also provide people with “increased visibility into the wide range of data apps will receive access to or control over if full permissions are granted,” the blog post notes.

Industry observers note that another change will give customers more granular control over when an AI agent or app can gain access by allowing users to configure their apps to ask for permission every time access is needed. While arguably tedious and a potential obstacle to true automation, the feature could allow users to experiment with local AI without giving the agent carte blanche.

There will also be a upcoming transparency indicator that appears when an app is controlling another app through the System & App Protection Override option.

The changes, which are arriving with macOS 27.3, don’t appear to prevent Mac customers from installing and running AI agents or other apps that ask for higher levels of access. Instead, they give users more nuanced options, control, and information about the choices they’re making.

Apple advises app developers to adjust their onboarding processes to account for the latest changes and suggests that their apps learn to “gracefully handle having access to only a limited set of folders or apps.”.

Developers of apps that request Full Disk Access for disk backup or to provide assistive technologies can request exceptions to the new rules (which Apple calls “entitlements”) to preserve the same functionality they offered before the macOS 27.3 update.

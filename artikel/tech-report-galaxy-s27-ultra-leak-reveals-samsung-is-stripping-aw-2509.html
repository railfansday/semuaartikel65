Samsung might be gearing up for another significant change to its flagship phone cameras. A latest leak suggests Samsung is likely to ditch the laser autofocus hardware, possibly taking the 3x telephoto camera with it. That’s two changes to the rear camera system, and while neither is necessarily a recipe for photographic trouble, it does bring up an interesting question: what does Samsung intend to use instead?

Industry observers note that the claim comes from well-known leaker Ice Universe, who shared the alleged changes on X. Samsung has been using laser autofocus in its Ultra phones since the Galaxy S21 Ultra, so this is a departure from a camera setup the company has used for several generations.

For the uninitiated, the laser autofocus module is supposed to emit an infrared beam and calculate the time it takes to bounce back from a subject, then use the distance information to assist the camera in locking focus.

Sources indicate that this is particularly helpful in low-light situations, where traditional phase-detection autofocus (PDAF) can struggle. But laser autofocus has its own limitations. It struggles with distant subjects, bright sunlight and moving objects.

Industry observers note that that doesn't necessarily mean the S27 Ultra will have a worse camera. Autofocus technology has come a long way. Both dual-pixel and all-pixel autofocus can calculate the distance to a subject by using multiple light-sensitive areas across the image sensor. This technology is already being used in some of Samsung’s other flagship devices, so the ditching of a separate laser module is not necessarily a step backward.

It’s also rumored that Samsung might utilize its fresh 200MP ISOCELL HP6 sensor for the Galaxy S27 Ultra and S27 Pro. Previous reports have indicated support for LOFIC, a technology that’s designed to improve HDR photography and preserve detail in the brightest and darkest parts of a photo. It’s not known if the new sensor will also introduce an advanced autofocus system as well.

Sources indicate that the bigger question is what these hardware changes portend for everyday photography. The lack of a dedicated 3x camera may mean more reliance on cropping the sensor for zoom ranges in between. The removal of laser autofocus may mean more reliance on the main sensor’s focusing system.

None of these changes have been confirmed by Samsung, and we won’t see the Galaxy S27 family until the first quarter of 2027. Whether these rumored changes are a smart play for newer imaging tech or just another instance of stripping out useful hardware to cut costs is something buyers will want answered before splashing out flagship money.

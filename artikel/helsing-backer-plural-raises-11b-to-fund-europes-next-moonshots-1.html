Helsing backer Plural raises $1.1B to fund Europe’s next moonshots

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Operator-led VC firm Plural has raised €1 billion ($1.1 billion) in new capital, joining a growing cohort of larger European funds that also includes Mundi Ventures’ Kembara and EQT-managed Scaleup Europe Fund.

Despite its size, Plural’s fundraise isn’t (only) for a growth fund: two thirds will go to its third early-stage fund, and one third to a new “opportunities” fund making select follow-on investments, TechCrunch learned from Plural partners Khaled Helioui and Carina Namih.

The two buckets are fixed, but the check size is not. Plural is “still very committed to being an early stage fund,” Namih remarked. But Helioui added that some of the early-stage “moonshots” it aims to back may require “raising a couple hundred million” to get off the ground.

“Moonshot” is an appropriate term to describe some of Plural’s portfolio highlights, such as Proxima Fusion and The Exploration Organization — and its new fund will be looking for equally promising opportunities in energy, AI infrastructure, defense, health, and more.

Many of these are capital-intensive, but this is no longer stopping European entrepreneurs, especially second-time founders, and companies like Daniel Ek’s Helsing are “not isolated cases,” stated Helioui, who backed the startup’s seed round alongside two Plural partners.

“If you’ve built and scaled a tech giant, chances are, you’re more likely to be driven and passionate about having an impact on Europe’s ability to defend itself, procure its own energy, or own its operations and manufacturing, rather than just another app,” he said.

With bigger pockets, Plural will be able to keep up longer with the larger and larger rounds its portfolio companies have gone on to raise. It also made an effort to raise more of this capital from Europe, Namih mentioned.

While Europe’s institutional LPs were, for a long time, more likely to back banker types than founders hoping to displace those bankers, the current shift towards Europe’s sovereignty and resilience creates tailwinds for Plural, whose office in Tallinn played a key role in its decision to invest in defense tech — including in a missile manufacturer, Hypersonica.

“The reality is today still, you have a different level of conversation and a different sense of appreciation of the urgency and the criticality of these topics depending on how far from the border to Russia you are,” said Helioui.

Two of Plural’s six partners are Estonians, and Skype alumni: Wise cofounder Taavet Hinrikus and Teleport’s Sten Tamkivi. However, there is more to Plural than Tallinn; the fund also has offices in London, where Helioui, Namih and Ian Hogarth are based; and in Paris, where it hired former Uber executive Pierre-Dimitri Gore-Coty as a full-time partner in October 2025.

Industry observers note that according to a press release, each partner will lead a small number of new investments a year. These companies will then get access to Plural’s growing portfolio support, which already ranges from hiring and communications to finance, Helioui noted. That’s another indicator that VC firms in Europe are increasingly starting to look like their U.S. peers.

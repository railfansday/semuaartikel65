Mike Schur reveals how Digs best life advice doubles as great writing advice

In a recent development, in the last moments of Dig's premiere, archaeologists Elise (Amy Poehler) and Dylan (Geraldine Viswanathan) stumble upon an astounding discovery. At the bottom of a trench that no archaeologist has touched in a decade, under a thin layer of dirt, there lie the remains of a perfectly preserved statue.

In a recent development, "We dug in this trench for two years," Elise marvels. "And if we had only gone an inch deeper...".

She trails off, but the sentiment is clear: If the dig team had kept chipping away at the trench, they would have found the artifact of a lifetime. This scene is Dig's core thesis in a nutshell: You can always dig deeper and find greater rewards, whether it's in your relationships, your career, or yes, the giant trenches peppering an archaeological dig.

"Digging a little deeper is just good life advice in general, but it's also a really good lesson in writing," Dig co-creator Mike Schur told Mashable over a Zoom interview.

Writing, as Schur can attest, is "exhausting" work. "By creative standards, not digging a ditch standards," he noted. That means that it can sometimes be tempting to take the easy way out. To leave the trench after two years of digging, as it were.

Sources indicate that "When you hit on something like a joke or a scene or a character that's pretty good, every ounce of your being is like, 'It's good. Just move on,'" Schur noted.

However, Schur learned to curb that impulse while writing on the American version of The Office, which was developed by his mentor, Greg Daniels.

During script revisions, Schur recalled the writers' room going over scenes time and again with a fine-toothed comb, punching up jokes, pitching newly introduced ideas, and just generally refining what was on the page.

After revisions, Schur mentioned, "I remember [Daniels] would say, 'Okay, I think that's two percent better.".

Industry observers note that schur continued: "He used to say that if you make every joke in the episode two percent better, then the whole episode is going to be seven and a half percent better, and that's amazing. You just made something a little better and a little more memorable.".

Think of that two percent as being the last little inch of dirt separating Elise from the miraculously intact Greek statue. It might be tough to excavate any further, especially if you've already been digging fruitlessly for years. But for writers, established and aspiring alike, couldn't that extra little bit of effort be worth it if you eventually strike gold?

As Schur put it, "Digging a little, doing a little extra work, revisiting a scene one more time to make it a little better is the essence of what I think differentiates memorable writing from very good writing.".

Think the Google Fitbit Air is your only option? We've tested trending screenless fitness trackers to find the 6 best

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

We're experiencing always-on tech fatigue, which is in part why wearables like smart rings and now screenless fitness and health trackers are becoming popular.

It has been revealed that they can capture all the same data as a smartwatch or traditional fitness tracker, but do so passively, in the background, so you can go about your day without distractions. Screenless fitness trackers in particular are on the rise, graduating from a market once dominated only by Whoop to include huge brands like Google Fitbit and Garmin as well as smaller ones like Luna and Amazfit.

Android Central has tested many of the latest screenless fitness bands extensively, often sporting more than one at a time to compare and contrast them and the data they collect. At their core, they all do the same thing. But there are also notable differences in design, battery life, app experience, and subscription options (or membership requirements). You'll want to pick the right one to use alongside your Android device based on your needs, preferences, and budget.

The Google Fitbit Air is thin, comfortable, affordable, yet packs tons of power in a small band. Think Fitbit's history of accurate activity and sleep tracking combined with Google's knowledge in design, AI, and apps. With Google Health Premium, it's the best you can buy right now.

Recent findings suggest that the Garmin CIRQA is thicker and chunkier than others, but it's a solid choice if fitness is your main priority. That's thanks to accurate automatic tracking and a side button for rapidly triggering an activity, cute shareable post-workout cards, and decent battery life that keeps it going for more than a week per charge.

The Luna Band combines some of the best aspects of apps like Google Health Premium in a subscription-free, AI-driven experience. I loved the LifeOS system with the Luna Ring 2, and with the consistent app updates, it continues to improve, making this a promising option.

It has been revealed that you might need an executive-level salary to afford the Whoop MG's annual membership, but medical-grade ECG and blood pressure trends make it worthwhile for those with heart health concerns, especially. The app and system are among the most aggressive in terms of pushing you to get in shape, focus on your health, and slow the pace of aging. But for some, that's a good thing.

With features like live heart rate broadcasting to compatible devices like gym equipment and an optional arm sleeve, the Amazfit Helio Strap is meant for gym-goers and runners. It's a simple design with accurate data capture, and best of all, no subscription.

The Fitbit Air is one of the most accurate screenless fitness trackers, combining Fitbit's long history in the business with top-notch vitals and sleep tracking, along with Google's powerful processing and AI integration. While you can use the Fitbit Air with the free version of the app and get basic details, you will get a lot more out of it with a Google Health Premium subscription.

It has been revealed that AI Coach is one of the best I have used, answering questions about the data, like why you might not have slept well and what you can do to improve, what exercise would be good to work around a shoulder injury, and even logging specific dumbbell weights and reps to gather more details about a workout. I love how easy it is to log meals by either dictating what you just ate or snapping a photo. You'll see a detailed breakdown of calories consumed (versus burned), protein, fat, carbs, and more. It's a solid food diary if you want to use it for that, too.

It has been revealed that the companion app is easy to navigate with all the essentials at a glance, like heart rate, a detailed breakdown of sleep, blood oxygen, readiness score, and more. Most notably, the app guides you through how to tackle the day with commentary, analysis, motivation, suggestions, and advice. It really is like a virtual coach. A couple of massive omissions, our reviewer notes, are the lack of ECG and built-in GPS. But considering how affordable the band is, we can live with that.

Along with what's captured from the band, the more information you provide with food logging, details about workouts, injuries, moments of stress, and more, the more intelligent it becomes in putting this all together. It's one of the few wearables where I'd say the subscription is worth the money.

Given the affordable price of the band, use the three-month trial to get an idea of what Premium adds and decide from there. It's a no-brainer option to keep on top of fitness and vitals.

The Garmin CIRQA is thicker and wider than other bands, but it's still incredibly comfortable to wear. Most importantly, it tracks all the essentials for free. You can opt for the Garmin Connect+ subscription for add-ons like Garmin Coach, AI-powered insights, and nutrition tracking. But it's not robust enough to be worth it, and that's pretty much all that's behind a paywall.

Sources indicate that the band accurately tracks activity, vitals, and sleep; the data provided is typically in line with other wearables I rely on. What I love is that it's the only screenless band I have seen to date with a physical side button you can press to manually start and stop an activity. You can later clarify in the app what it was, like a cardio class or baseball game. But it does a pretty good job of automatically picking up activities with distinct movements, like walks, runs, boxing, and strength training. I love the ability to add a post-workout selfie to a cute card with workout stats you can share on social media or with friends.

If you're looking for a band mainly to track fitness, the Garmin CIRQA is a good option. You don't get all the bells and whistles that come with Google Health Premium and Fitbit Air. It's also a higher upfront cost for the band. But you can keep track of everything that matters, including a detailed diary of your workouts and stats.

One of the lesser-known brands in this space, Luna is best known for its smart rings. But the tech giant released its first screenless tracker at CES 2026 with the Luna Band, and it's a serious contender. It tracks all the same metrics as others and does so accurately, based on my month-plus of wearing it. This includes everything from activities to sleep, vitals like heart rate, HRV, stress, respiratory rate, and more.

Recent findings suggest that at the heart of the experience is the Life OS system, an AI-driven system that lets you ask questions, log things like stress and meals, get advice, dive deeper into data, and more. There's an "Ask Luna" hands-free voice option, though it's limited to iPhone support right now.

Seemingly inspired by Ultrahuman smart rings and the tech giant's PowerPlugs, the Luna app also includes downloadable micro apps you can add to customize the experience. These include, at the time of this writing, sleep alarm, cycle tracker, calorie tracker, and circadian rhythm. The app indicates that upcoming features are coming soon, like a Luna Age (cardio fitness score), supplement planner, and hydration analyzer.

The app has been updated and redesigned several times since I started wearing the band. So, if you are looking to get in with a brand that's experimenting and continuously developing, this is a good option. Most importantly, you can be confident that the data is accurate.

It has been revealed that around since 2015, Whoop isn't technically the originator of this category, but it has a similar reputation to Oura as the brand that put the category on the map. But before you get excited about a Whoop tracker, they're expensive with a capital "E." And the Whoop MG is the top-line model.

Recent findings suggest that it's a medical-grade tracker that, compared to its little sister, the Whoop 5.0, adds ECG and blood pressure insights. ECG is easy to measure any time by placing your thumb and index finger on the sensors on either side, and blood pressure requires an initial cuff measurement to then provide subsequent trend estimates.

The app is packed (and I really mean packed) with data that you might initially feel like you need a medical degree to interpret. Thankfully, however, it's thoughtfully laid out and explained to help you make sense of it all. It tracks all the expected metrics and has a Healthspan and Pace of Aging feature. After the 31-day logging period, it will report a Whoop Age that you can compare to your biological age and determine your pace of aging. It's a moving target, so if there's ever a stat that prompts you to work out, go for that walk, eat better, and head to bed early, it's that one.

In a recent development, if the Whoop MG is too rich for your blood and you are willing to forego ECG and blood pressure insights, consider the Whoop 5.0. Its membership options are slightly more affordable, but still more expensive than any other band over its lifetime.

According to recent reports, if you love the concept behind the WHOOP MG but find the pricing too rich for your blood, even for the WHOOP 5.0, the Hume Band 2.0 is worth considering. While I haven't yet tested this model (stay tuned!), it is widely praised for offering some of the same benefits as WHOOP bands, including a focus on longevity and helping you reduce the speed of aging.

According to recent reports, while you get the core tracking features for free, including sleep, recovery, strain, HRV, skin temperature, and of course activity tracking as well as blood pressure trends, there is a Hume Premium subscription tier that unlocks more. But it's much cheaper at $8.99 per month, which works out to be almost half the price of the cheapest WHOOP One blueprint.

The band and companion app capture useful data like metabolic capacity (similar to a readiness score), sleep apnea risk alerts, chronic illness detection (identifying early signs), and AI coaching with the subscription tier. You may have to take the data with a grain of salt. But the price of entry makes Hume Band 2.0 a good starting point if you're considering exploring the concept of a screenless tracker and value overall lifespan data more than daily metrics and workout stats.

According to recent reports, amazfit has a solid reputation in the smartwatch space, with many of its models ranking among the best affordable Android smartwatches. This reliability extends to the Amazfit Helio Strap, which is simple, affordable, and, based on customer feedback (we haven't reviewed this one yet), as reliable as its beloved smartwatches.

Sources indicate that one of the unique features is live heart rate broadcasting, which allows you to broadcast your heart rate to compatible devices, like a Garmin smartwatch, Apple Watch, or even gym equipment. The best part? There's no subscription required…for anything.

Industry observers note that there's a more premium option called the Amazfit Helio Strap Pro that boasts a dual-sensor system and is designed for hybrid athletes and HYROX training. But most people won't need that model. There are also useful accessories like an arm sleeve for wearing it around the bicep, something runners will appreciate so they can still wear a running watch on their wrist. This is a solid budget option for fitness enthusiasts who also want vital health tracking.

Choosing the right screenless fitness band comes down to two core things: recognizing your needs and budget.

It has been revealed that if you want something you only need to pay a single price for and that doesn't require recurring fees, choose one of those options. If you are serious about health, want guidance and granular data, and can afford it, you may find value in some subscription-based bands.

All these bands track both fitness and health, including vitals. But some focus more heavily on fitness, some on health, and some a mix of both. If you only want to monitor daily data, you can look for a simpler option. If you need guidance towards meeting goals, a more advanced one makes sense. For ECG readings, the Whoop MG is your only option in this category. If it's pricing doesn't meet your budget, however, you can consider a smartwatch with ECG instead.

You'll be happy with any of these screenless fitness bands. For an AI-driven coaching experience, Fitbit Air can't be beat. But if you want to get something similar without a subscription, Luna Band is worth considering. If you exercise daily and want to track workouts as much as vitals, the Garmin CIRQA is a solid choice.

It has been revealed that the Whoop MG is a medical-grade device, ideal for serious athletes or someone who is especially concerned about heart health and/or blood pressure. But it comes in at executive-level pricing. It's worth noting that none of these trackers, even the Whoop MG, is a replacement for consultation from a medical professional, and they are not meant for diagnosis, only supplemental data.

According to recent reports, the best overall budget option is the Fitbit Air. It doesn't cost much upfront, and you can gauge how the subscription works through the three-month trial. Once the trial lapses, see the difference without it and decide from there if it's worth paying the nominal monthly fee. Even with Google Health Premium, the cost is one less takeout lunch a month when all is said and done. And even after a year, the overall cost is still less than the upfront cost of most of these other bands.

Today (Nov. 10), Amazon's "Ring Robotics" begins a newly introduced path in home security for homeowners. In a press release, the firm states Ring Robotics will focus on security measures that patrol, move, respond, and investigate. Jamie Siminoff, founder and chief inventor of Ring, said Ring Robotics is the product of users wanting "eyes in the places a fixed camera couldn’t reach, and peace of mind in the spots they’d never want one.".

Sminioff adds, "The honest answer wasn’t more cameras; it meant reimagining what a security device could be." As a result, we've got the first two products of Amazon's continuous work with Ring Robotics: the Ring Rover and Ring Drone Cam. The Ring Rover Cam is exactly what you think—an automated security robot that conducts "regular sweeps" of a given space.

Amazon states the Rover is intended more for businesses (think office spaces and wide, expansive warehouses). If you remember the Ring Astro, this is sort of like that. The post states that once the Ring Rover receives its orders and it arrives at the intended location, it will perform a "360° scan to give you a comprehensive view.".

The Rover is deeply connected with other Ring devices (doorbells, cameras), and if one of those detects a disturbance, the security robot will investigate for you.

It has been revealed that A periscope camera lets customers adjust its height depending on location, and constant 4K streaming never lets you miss a beat. The Ring Rover also features a built-in siren and light tower.

Recent findings suggest that amazon states the Ring Rover Cam can be pre-ordered now for $999. The robot will begin shipping in the U.S. on December 16.

Recent findings suggest that the Ring Drone Cam gives us Always Home Cam vibes (if you also remember that from 2021). However, Ring Robotics' latest take brings a more modern aesthetic to the flying security drone. Amazon states the Drone Cam "gives you whole-home visibility without the need to mount a camera in every room." AI is built directly into the Drone Cam, as is its integration with Ring's scope of home security devices.

According to recent reports, similar to how you can designate areas and orders with the Rover, the Drone Cam requires flight routes. Amazon states the Drone will fly those routes on its own when scheduled; however, owners may deploy it at any time. The Drone Cam offers a 148-degree FOV and 360-degree rotation in 2K resolution.

At the moment, Amazon claims the Drone Cam will be sold for $249, released on January 27, 2027.

Cameras in the home. Flying, roving cameras in the home. It's enough to stir concern in one's mind. Amazon tries to offer relief, stating that when the Rover and Drone are powered down (or in an idle state), their cameras are off. For the Drone, its camera turns off completely, while the Rover retracts its lens (mic off, too). Additionally, when everything is functioning and active, Amazon indicates, "Both have LED indicators, and Ring Rover tells you right on its screen.".

What's more, these devices are loud. The organization highlights its use of TAKE security encryption for the videos each device captures. This reportedly helps to secure the footage the devices capture for consumers.

Updated on Monday, Nov. 16 at 4:14 p.m. ET — In a statement posted to X, JPMorganChase confirmed it's experiencing "a temporary problem related to a software bug in on-premise hardware systems," but that customers "still have access to their accounts and funds." The statement also said that service should be restored by the end of the day.

In a recent development, while user error reports have spiked on the platform DownDetector for Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Zelle, and US Bank, at this time, only Chase is confirmed to be experiencing problems. In a statement, a JPMorganChase spokesperson told Mashable, "This is not a systemic issue — other banks may have been impacted by slowness in transactions only to the extent that they were a party to a transaction with us.".

Another day, another major outage.

According to recent reports, just days after Microsoft experienced two days of downtime for services like Outlook and the FAA grounded all U.S. aircraft due to a telecommunications and data issue, some major U.S. banks are experiencing problems of their own.

It has been revealed that user error reports are surging for Chase, Bank of America, US Bank, Wells Fargo, and Capital One, according to DownDetector. User error reports started to rise around 12:00 p.m. ET on Monday afternoon, and by 2 p.m. ET, DownDetector recorded a peak of more than 4,800 reports for Chase Bank over the prior 24 hours.

In a statement to Mashable, a JPMorganChase spokesperson confirmed that it's experiencing issues due to "a software bug in on-premise hardware systems," and that the problem is not related to a cyberattack or a systemic issue with U.S. financial institutions. In a recent development, during outages, DownDetector sometimes shows elevated user error reports at organizations that are functioning normally, but which may be impacted by third-party outages. For instance, if a money transfer app is experiencing problems, customers may report a problem with their bank, rather than the app.

In a recent development, US Bank told CBS that's exactly what's happening this afternoon. While DownDetector reveals elevated error reports from US Bank customers, a spokesperson for the bank told CBS that it has not been directly impacted by the outage. However, "clients attempting to send or receive Zelle payments with another financial institution may experience transaction delays," the spokesperson remarked.

Notably, Zelle told Mashable its service was not impacted by the outage, indicating the problem may lie with financial institutions.

"Zelle is not down," a spokesperson told Mashable. "Zelle is operating as it should. Consumers use Zelle through participating banks and credit unions, which manage their accounts and hold their funds. Consumers do not have a separate Zelle account, and Zelle does not hold consumer funds. An problem at the financial institutions may be impacting broader services, including Zelle payments, for some of their customers.".

As of this writing, we have not seen reports of customers who are unable to access their funds or make purchases. Rather, CBS and other outlets have reported problems with some in-person bank transactions and online banking tools.

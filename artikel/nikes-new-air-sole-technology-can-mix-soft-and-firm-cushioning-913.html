In 2024, Nike unveiled its Air Max 1000 that were manufactured using a 3D-printing process resulting in a shoe that had a uniform silhouette but varying areas of support and flexibility throughout. The organization is now doing something similar with its iconic air-filled soles using its fresh Tuned Air technology. While previously the level of cushioning in Nike Air shoes was uniform across the entire sole, a new manufacturing process can produce multiple air pockets with varying levels of cushioning and stability.

In a recent development, nike has been putting air pockets into its shoes for nearly 50 years as a way to increase comfort and cushioning while making them lighter. Its air pockets have even found their way into apparel as a way to improve insulation by trapping body heat, but Nike aims to use its upcoming Tuned Air technology as a way to customize the cushioning experience in its shoes and tailor them to specific athletes and sports. The first shoes featuring the technology are expected to arrive in late 2027 and will be targeted at runners.

Industry observers note that while one of Nike’s earliest successes was track shoes with a grip design inspired by a waffle iron, the manufacturer credits the “vibrant colors on magazine covers” as the inspiration for its latest shoe technology. A Tuned Air sole is manufactured using a “proprietary digital print and selective lamination process” where an ink is applied in specific patterns that define where the layers bond to each other. This process can be used to create internal pockets in an unlimited combination of shapes and sizes, and by fine-tuning how much air is in each of those pockets, some parts of the sole can feel firmer while others feel softer.

The Tuned Air technology will not only allow Nike to highly customize the fit and feel of air sole shoes for different sports, it will also help keep the benefits of the custom tuning consistent across different sizes. The new manufacturing process will also help reduce the amount of time it takes Nike to go from a concept for a new shoe to a final design ready for production. “Prototypes that once took three weeks can now be made in three days,” the company claims in a release today. That gives Nike’s designers more time to refine new shoes, and more opportunity to experiment.

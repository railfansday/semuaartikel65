Recent findings suggest that last month, Microsoft asked as many as 60,000 employees to voluntarily leave their jobs with buyout offers. This week, CEO Satya Nadella took some time to explain why.

In a talk with Andrew Ross Sorkin at the Latest York Times' DealBook Summit in New York City (as reported by TechCrunch), Nadella addressed the buyout offer that made headlines in October. As we reported at the time, the buyouts came amidst a huge amount of AI investment at Microsoft and industry-wide AI layoffs. And in his comments at the summit, Nadella made it explicitly clear that the company reorganization was necessary because of AI.

You can listen to the CEO's full comments at about 27:20 in the New York Times' YouTube video of the interview:.

Recent findings suggest that according to Nadella, it's not necessarily about directly replacing humans with AI in a one-to-one fashion. However, that may not be comforting to workers asked to leave their jobs, if only voluntarily. “Let’s take software engineering. We went from, ‘Well, we don’t buy that AI can ever do software.’ To suddenly, ‘Wow, it can do all software, and why do we need software engineers?’ To now rediscovering that software engineering is very much alive and, in fact, is going to thrive, except the way the team is organized and the work that a software engineer does has now got a new operating point,” Nadella said.

In a recent development, like many tech leaders, Nadella believes the future of development at Microsoft and beyond involves humans managing a team of AI agents, an approach that many Substantial Tech companies have already adopted. For example, the team behind the Microsoft 365 AI agent Cowork consists of only 11 human beings who, in Nadella's words, "build a software factory that builds the product" rather than writing the product code themselves.

Rather than simply laying off workers, Nadella mentioned the buyouts were intended to give workers some measure of control over their future at Microsoft.

By opting for buyouts, the CEO noted that he wanted “to make sure that at least everyone felt like they had agency in making choices," per TechCrunch.

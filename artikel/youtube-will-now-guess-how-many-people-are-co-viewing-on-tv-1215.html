YouTube is making another big change to how views are counted on long-form videos, YouTube Shorts, and livestreams.

According to a company patch, YouTube will now include "co-views" in public video view counts.

It's YouTube's estimate of how many people are watching content together on TV via a single account.

YouTube claims it will include these estimated views because "viewers are now watching over 1 billion hours of YouTube on TVs every day." The company says it wants to "credit creators true reach" and "provide a more complete picture of how audiences are engaging with content.".

Industry observers note that this isn't the first recent change to how YouTube counts video views. This past summer, the platform started counting views from the moment a video plays. Previously, viewers had to watch for a specific amount of time, typically around 30 seconds for long-form videos, in order for their view to be counted in the public metric. Co-views is certainly a bit more offbeat than that, as the company is essentially inflating view counts by estimating how many people are watching together on a single device that would've previously been counted as a single view.

YouTube, however, claims this upcoming platform metric "aligns with TV industry standards, including how traditional broadcast and streaming networks estimate the total number of people exposed to content.".

The manufacturer notes it is using a statistical model that utilizes "factors like demographic patterns and viewing times.".

Creators will be able to see new co-view rate analytics in YouTube Studio to gauge how this metric affects their overall views.

This newly introduced metric will only be applied to video views on TV, so if consumers watch content with friends and family on their desktop or mobile device, co-views won't be counted. YouTube will only count co-views going forward and will not retroactively apply them to videos on the platform.

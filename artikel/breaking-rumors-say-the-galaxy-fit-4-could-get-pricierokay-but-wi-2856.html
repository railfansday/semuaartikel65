Breaking: Rumors say the Galaxy Fit 4 could get pricier—okay, but will its upgrades justify it?

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Samsung is reportedly gearing up for another fitness tracker after the Galaxy Fit 3 in 2024. Rumors have steadily surfaced, and now we're hearing about its alleged price.

In a recent development, it's a tumultuous time in the tech industry. Prices are soaring all over (even grocery stores are getting hit). A Dealabs (French) rumor about the Galaxy Fit 4 suggests Samsung's next tracker won't dodge a price increase (via SamMobile). The publication reported an alleged price increase for the Fit 4 in Europe. The post states Samsung could raise prices by ~€10 to ~€80 (~$87).

Despite its supposed pricing appearing online, the publication states it's still unclear when Samsung could launch the Fit 4.

Recent findings suggest that no one wants to see prices go up. However, if there is a price increase, is there something under the hood to justify it? Galaxy Fit 4 rumors began in June, suggesting the device would stay true to its fitness-tracker lineage. The device might debut with RTOS (operating system), not Google's Wear OS that you'd find on the Fitbit Air or the Galaxy Watch 9.

Industry observers note that as far as what to expect, rumors said the Galaxy Fit 4 would skip Samsung's summer Unpacked (the one we just had). While that was true, reports said Samsung would launch the device a "few months later." We thought September might've been the debut, but it never happened, so we're still waiting. Rumors are still incredibly light on its specifications, which could mean we're still far from a launch.

Right now, all we have to theorize about Samsung's next steps is what the Galaxy Fit 3 featured: a 1.6-inch display, smarter workouts, and a battery that could last up to 13 days. With everything the Galaxy Watch 9 contains, maybe we can expect a few of those health features to slide on over.

Another rumor suggests the Galaxy Fit 4 will lack NFC for contactless payments and a built-in GPS. Instead, consumers might need to rely on their phones for that. It's not a huge dealbreaker, but that added convenience would go a long way. What's more, black, white, and light (icy?) blue colorways were rumored for the Galaxy Fit 4.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang has agreed to appear before the Senate Banking Committee, panel Chair Tim Scott, R-S.C., mentioned on CNBC's "Squawk Box" on Wednesday.

In a recent development, commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick has also agreed to testify, Scott remarked. The agreements to appear voluntarily come as pressure builds on Capitol Hill to investigate Huang's affair with a suspected Chinese spy, which led to his ouster at the chipmaker he founded.

Sources indicate that "They both have agreed," Scott mentioned. "We will hopefully have this done before Christmas.".

Huang reached an agreement on Tuesday with the chairmen of three House committees for a closed-door transcribed interview on Dec. 1 jointly before the three panels. Scott had asked Huang to appear publicly in the Senate.

Scott noted the appearances will provide the committee with "more information about what they knew, when they knew it and why they didn't do something about it.".

According to recent reports, the outgoing banking committee chair also homed in on Lutnick, calling for answers as to why the commerce secretary did not act after being warned about Megaspeed — the Singaporean company where the alleged Chinese spy, Alice Huang, was an executive. Nvidia sold its chips to Megaspeed, which U.S. investigators suspect of diverting them to China.

Industry observers note that "They were warned as early as March of this year, several months ago, that Megaspeed should be on the 'entity list,'" Scott remarked, referring to a Commerce Department list of companies the U.S. views as national security risks. The Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security "didn't do it, Howard didn't do it, we want to know why.".

Sources indicate that lutnick and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent have been embroiled in an intra-administration battle as the fallout from the Huang affair grows. Bessent on CNBC on Friday appeared to blame Lutnick for a lack of action on Megaspeed.

"You will have to ask them about their internal processes," Bessent noted, referring to Commerce and noting his department has long supported imposing restrictions on Megaspeed.

A spokesperson for Huang declined to comment on Scott's announcement that he has agreed to appear.

Industry observers note that huang's upcoming appearances are likely just the start of the embattled former CEO's problems on Capitol Hill. Democrats will take control of Congress in January and have demanded sweeping investigations into the affair and Huang's cozy relationship with President Donald Trump.

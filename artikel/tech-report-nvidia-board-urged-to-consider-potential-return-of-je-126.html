Two major Nvidia shareholders are calling on the board of directors to weigh all options for the company's future leadership, including "the potential return of Jensen Huang.".

Altimeter Capital CEO Brad Gerstner posted a letter on X on Friday that urges Nvidia's board to "pursue a slow, measured, and deliberate process regarding the future leadership of Nvidia." The letter is also signed by Atreides Management's Gavin Baker.

According to recent reports, nvidia was Altimeter Capital's largest disclosed equity holding in its most recent SEC filing, with a 9.4 million-share stake worth nearly $1.9 billion as of June 30. Atreides held 1.2 million shares worth nearly $250 million at the end of June.

The letter said Nvidia will be "capably led" by interim CEO Colette Kress, who served as Nvidia's CFO for 13 years before the board declared Thursday that she'd lead the firm in Huang's absence.

In a recent development, "But the Founder and long time incredible leader of this extraordinary tech giant has earned the right for a deliberate process that avoids any needless rush to replace him before all the facts are known," the letter concludes. The document did not call for Huang's immediate reinstatement.

Recent findings suggest that nvidia founder Jensen Huang resigned on Thursday amid multiple affair allegations, including one with suspected Chinese spy Alice Huang, who also worked with Nvidia customer Megaspeed. The Singapore-based firm is being investigated by the Justice Department for possibly redirecting some of its supply of Nvidia's restricted artificial intelligence chips to China.

SpaceX CEO Elon Musk remarked on X Friday that he signed a petition asking to reinstate Huang. A petition circulating online called "Stand with Jensen" claims to have more than 100,000 signatures, including more than 5,000 current Nvidia employees.

Musk said on X Saturday that without Huang, some "critical deals will be lost.".

Another letter sent Friday called on U.S. Secretary of Commerce Howard Lutnick to testify about Megaspeed before the House Intelligence Committee.

Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on Dec. 10 about whether the Commerce Department is "properly performing its broader role of preventing China from acquiring advanced AI chips," according to the letter. Huang has also been asked to testify.

The Commerce Department originally rolled out an investigation into Megaspeed that was moved to the Justice Department in 2025 after Nvidia's lawyer contacted a senior Commerce official, according to The Latest York Times.

Industry observers note that president Donald Trump is scheduled to visit China on Thursday, but doesn't strategy to raise Nvidia and the suspected spy affair during his meeting with Chinese president Xi Jinping, according to U.S. officials. Musk is expected to join Trump on the trip to Shenzhen, China, for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation summit on Nov. 18 and 19.

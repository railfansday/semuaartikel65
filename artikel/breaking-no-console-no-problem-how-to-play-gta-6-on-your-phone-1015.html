Grand Theft Auto 6 is just hours away, and while console owners are looking forward to diving into the huge virtual world of Vice City, I'm sure that there are plenty of mobile players feeling left out.

After all, GTA 6 is currently the highest-rated game of all time on review aggregator Metacritic, with early reviews calling it a once-in-a-generation experience that you surely won't want to miss.

If you primarily game on a phone or tablet, there are actually ways that you can play GTA 6 via streaming – not to mention plenty of Android native alternatives that are worth checking out.

Here's how to play GTA 6 on your phone, as well as some titles that are like GTA 6 to consider instead.

If you won't settle for anything but the real deal, then cloud gaming is the way to go. Cloud gaming services are effectively the gaming equivalent of Netflix, streaming a play session from a powerful remote server.

With a fast internet connection (a consistent 50 to 100 Mbps download rate is ideal) and a compatible mobile gaming controller (I would recommend hooking up the official Xbox Wireless pad for the most authentic experience), you can dive straight into GTA 6 without even waiting for a download.

It has been revealed that this no-nonsense gamepad is fully compatible with Xbox Cloud Gaming and easy to hook up to your mobile phone so a perfect choice for GTA 6. Just prop your phone up using a makeshift stand, and you can dive right in.

The best service to use for GTA 6 is Xbox Cloud Gaming, and it can be accessed straight through your phone's browser. Do note that it requires both an Xbox copy of the GTA 6 game and either an Xbox Game Pass subscription or purchased cloud playtime, which grants you access to a set number of hours of cloud streaming.

If you run out of time, you'll need to buy more via the Xbox Store.

This makes it quite an expensive avenue for getting your GTA 6 fix, so I'd only recommend it for the most dedicated gamers.

If you're not prepared to go down the pricey streaming route, then you still have some options at your fingertips. In my view, the best alternative is to pick up one of the many native ports of older Grand Theft Auto titles available on the Google Play Store. The best part is that they don't cost anything to try out.

GTA: Vice City - Definitive is an official remastered version of the 2002 installment Grand Theft Auto: Vice City. Set in the 1980s, it takes place in the same city as GTA 6 and offers its own unique storyline and a more condensed open world to explore. Yes, it's obviously not as technologically advanced as the fresh game (there has been more than two decades between them, after all) – but it's still a timeless classic and could be just the thing to tide you over until the FOMO wears off.

That initial free download nets you a 30-minute free trial, which you can then upgrade into the full game with a one-time in-app fee if you are enjoying the experience.

According to recent reports, you can do the same with GTA III - Definitive, which is a remaster for the third game in the series. GTA: San Andreas - Definitive is also available, though this entry costs money up front to play.

All three games have full touch control support, so you won't need to buy a separate controller either.

If you would rather play a more modern Grand Theft Auto game, then PC emulation is worth looking into.

You're going to need a seriously beefy phone for this, however, and I would recommend going with a dedicated gaming model like the RedMagic 10 Pro or the newly released RedMagic 11 Pro for the best performance.

These phones come with a built-in PC game emulator, which lets you download games you own from Steam with ease. If you own Grand Theft Auto 5 on Steam, you can get it running smoothly on your phone at around 720p resolution after fiddling with the graphics settings.

This lets you experience the whole previous entry, which is the next best thing to GTA 6.

You can even do this on any high-end mobile device by seeking out and downloading a compatible PC game emulator, but the setup process is much more complicated, and I wouldn't recommend it for beginners or those not willing to tinker with their tech.

If you're just after a game like the Grand Theft Auto series, then there are some native Android titles that offer similar gameplay.

I've been testing OneState RP over the last week or so, which is a free-to-play life role-playing game set in modern Los Angeles.

In a recent development, it takes a bit of time to get going, but it's a surprisingly expansive experience that lets you pursue different career paths and engage in online multiplayer activities. You can become a police officer, for example, or join a criminal gang while working to earn money that you can spend buying homes, cars, and more.

It's all based around big servers filled with hundreds of other players, who you're encouraged to interact and role-play with. It's like having a whole virtual world on your phone!

In a recent development, retro City Rampage DX is also a great experience. It's only a few bucks to buy and offers top-down open-world action that's very reminiscent of the early GTA games. It's wacky, filled with fun challenges, and features more than 60 story missions to complete.

According to recent reports, finally, the popular free-to-play open-world RPG NTE: Neverness to Everness should be on your radar. It has slick anime graphics with an incredibly detailed and massive open-world city. The setting is more fantastical than the other two games that I've been talking about, but I personally enjoy the tense, supernatural tone of its main plot.

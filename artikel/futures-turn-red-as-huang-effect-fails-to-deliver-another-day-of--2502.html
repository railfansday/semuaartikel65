Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.

The animal spirits are subsiding.

Jensen Huang's return to Nvidia sparked a historic market moment, but lingering questions remain over what his reinstatement means for the AI tech giant and the future of corporate governance in America.

Nvidia shares are lower in premarket trading, while futures are lower as the Nasdaq and S&P give up earlier gains.

Industry observers note that nvidia surged 31% during Monday's regular session, leading a rally in chip stocks. The Nasdaq Composite closed 6.5% higher, the S&P 500 finished up 4.7% and the Dow Industrials rose 3% Monday.

The rally spread through the technology sector in Asia, driving huge gains for South Korea's Kospi and Taiwan's Taiex, but the gains faded in Europe where the sector dipped into the red, pulling the Europe Stoxx 600 lower in early trade.

Nvidia, chip stocks and other technology names have turned red in premarket trading after that blistering rally on Monday.

Wall Street analysts are scrambling to upgrade their rating and price target on Nvidia shares.

26-year-old Google worker earns $323,000 a year—but lives on less than minimum wage.

At 26 years old, Google AI security analyst and YouTuber Charles Broomfield is on track to earn about $323,000 this year — but he lives on less than minimum wage.

For the past few years, Broomfield has kept his annual spending on core living expenses under $20,000, less than the roughly $23,700 a full-time worker would earn at the minimum wage, averaged across all 50 states.

It’s the season of great deals on TVs. But once you get a fresh one home and set it up in your living room, it might not meet expectations when you turn it on. Maybe it’s overly bright, or things just look… off. Luckily, there are some easy tweaks you can make so that new, expensive purchase looks its best.

Sources indicate that it seems counterintuitive, but the default mode for most TVs out of the box isn’t accurate and can include lots of extra processing that makes the image look artificially detailed or overly smooth. The mode is usually called something like Eco or Energy Saving and is used to meet energy consumption requirements. But it also negatively affects picture quality.

Recent findings suggest that the easiest way to make the biggest positive change to your TV’s image is by changing out of this picture mode. Using Filmmaker, Movie, Cinema, or Professional picture mode (each company uses different names) will make the color temperature more accurate — it will look more red in comparison to the very blue Eco mode, but trust me, it’s far more realistic — and will either turn off extra processing or, at the very least, tone it down. Also, avoid Vivid and Dynamic modes, which are only there to blast brightness and make the image incredibly blue.

Sources indicate that another setting you should immediately turn off is the ambient light sensor. The idea behind it is solid — it adjusts the picture brightness and color temperature based on the ambient light in the room — but I have yet to find one that doesn’t make distracting brightness adjustments while I’m watching TV. This option can also be called Brightness Optimization, Ambient Light Detection, Intelligent Mode, or Energy Saving Step, and it’s usually not in the picture settings menu. Instead, look for it under General settings in a power-saving menu and switch it off.

After the ambient light sensor is off, you can adjust the brightness based on how much light is in your room. This can be done with the brightness or backlight slider, whichever one looks like it changes the brightness across the entire screen. Having the brightness pumped up in the middle of the day can be great to combat sunlight streaming through the windows, but can be overbearing once the sun goes down. Adjusting it to taste can be the difference between an immersive movie experience and a headache two hours later.

Recent findings suggest that of course, the one setting everyone thinks about when it comes to TVs is motion smoothing. I’ve made it a personal mission to turn it off on any TV I have access to, be it in a waiting room, a hotel room, or my parents’ living room. It uses a process called frame interpolation to create extra frames between existing ones to smooth out motion. It’s quite often referred to as the “soap opera effect” because it makes movies look like afternoon TV dramas. Using Filmmaker mode will generally turn this and other extraneous processing off, but it’s still best to double-check.

As with the ambient light sensor, the idea behind motion smoothing is good, and for some programs with fast motion, like sports, it can actually do a solid job depending on the TV manufacturer (Sony’s Motionflow on its lowest setting can be decent).

It has been revealed that if you blueprint on gaming, using Gaming mode will turn off some of that extra processing to reduce input lag as much as possible, and to make high refresh rate modes available. I recommend looking for the color temperature setting and switching it to something with “warm” in the title, as that will move the color temp toward something more natural.

Finally, if you have anything connected to your TV via HDMI (especially a gaming console), make sure the HDMI settings are set to Enhanced instead of Standard. This will expand the bandwidth to the port’s full capability. As with the power settings, HDMI settings are usually found in a General menu and can be called HDMI signal format or External Device Manager.

Getting a new TV is a substantial investment, and one that should last you a few years at least. And while most TVs today can perform incredibly well, they still need a bit of a nudge to get all the performance you paid for. Making these tweaks will do exactly that and lead to years of enjoyment.

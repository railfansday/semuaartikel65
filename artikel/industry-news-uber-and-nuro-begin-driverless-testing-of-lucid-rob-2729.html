Industry News: Uber and Nuro begin driverless testing of Lucid robotaxis ahead of 2027 launch

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Uber and Nuro have started testing driverless Lucid Gravity SUVs on public streets in the San Francisco Bay Area more than six months after receiving the permit that allowed the companies to pull the human safety driver from behind the wheel.

The companies will soon begin offering driverless rides to employees in the final lead-up to launching a commercial robotaxi service in the first quarter of 2027.

The milestone comes just 16 months after Uber declared an ambitious blueprint to launch a premium robotaxi service using Lucid electric vehicles outfitted with autonomous vehicle technology developed by startup Nuro.

In a recent development, that strategy came with hefty investments in both companies and much-needed endorsements of their respective products. Uber initially agreed to invest $300 million in Lucid — a figure that has since ballooned to $500 million. Uber also agreed to invest nearly $500 million in Nuro, TechCrunch previously reported, citing sources familiar with the deal.

In a recent development, the agreement provided both Lucid and Nuro with boosts beyond the obvious capital injection. Uber agreed to buy at least 20,000 of Lucid Gravity SUVs over the next six years, giving the struggling EV maker a more predictable source of revenue. Nuro, meanwhile, was less than a year removed from announcing a risky, but necessary pivot away from developing driverless delivery vehicles and toward licensing its autonomous vehicle technology to automakers and mobility companies.

Industry observers note that nuro has spent nearly a year testing the Lucid Gravity robotaxi on public roads in the San Francisco Bay Area with a human safety operator behind the wheel. The California Department of Motor Vehicles, the agency that regulates the testing and deployment of autonomous vehicles in the state, granted Nuro a driverless AV permit in May 2026.

Uber and Nuro are also testing autonomous vehicles on public roads in Houston with human safety operators behind the wheel. Nuro is supplementing that on-road testing with closed-course testing and simulation to validate the self-driving system before opening the robotaxis to the public. The companies’ combined engineering fleet of autonomous vehicles, which stood at 100 AVs earlier this year, is expanding as Lucid begins manufacturing the first production versions of the robotaxis at its Arizona factory.

The Lucid Gravity robotaxi, unveiled in January 2026, is outfitted with high-resolution cameras, solid-state lidar sensors, and radars that help the self-driving system perceive the real-world environment and operate in it. Uber, which will own and operate the fleet, has focused its efforts on the in-cabin experience, including how riders will interact with the vehicle.

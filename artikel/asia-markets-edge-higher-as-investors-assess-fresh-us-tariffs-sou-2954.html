Asia-Pacific markets edged higher Thursday, tracking Wall Street gains overnight, while concerns over artificial intelligence spending, sticky inflation and new U.S. tariffs risk capping gains.

South Korea's Kospi rose 0.9%, while the small-cap Kosdaq Index advanced 1.62%, as investors await Bank of Korea's rate decision with Reuters-polled economists expecting a 25 basis point hike.

Japan's Nikkei 225 rose 1.21% in early trade, while the Topix edged 0.71% higher. Australia's S&P/ASX 200 was up 0.15%.

Futures for Hong Kong's Hang Seng index last traded at 22,149 compared with the index's last close of 21,966.01.

Focus will be on the implications of a fresh set of U.S. tariffs unveiled by the White House on Wednesday, targeting more than a dozen major trading partners, including China and the European Union.

According to recent reports, meanwhile, fresh U.S. inflation data reinforced expectations of further monetary tightening by the Federal Reserve. The personal consumption expenditures price index rose 3.8% year on year in October, accelerating from 3.7% in September, while core inflation came in slightly above expectations at 3.3%.

Recent findings suggest that overnight in the U.S., the Dow Jones Industrial Average erased an 1,100-point plunge on Wednesday, advancing 164.25 points, or 0.33%, to end at 50,182.95. The Nasdaq Composite, however, fell 0.62% to 23,585.80, while the S&P 500 shed 0.25% to close at 7,182.78.

It has been revealed that U.S. markets will be closed Thursday for the Thanksgiving holiday and will have a shortened trading session on Friday.

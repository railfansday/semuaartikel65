Anthropic defies the odds, holding record IPO despite ‘massively disruptive’ market

The moment felt impossible.Between Jensen Huang's shocking exit from chip giant Nvidia, voters' rebuke of artificial intelligence in this month's midterm elections, and rising interest rates that threatened to stymie plans for a multitrillion-dollar infrastructure buildout, there seemed to be no way Anthropic could carry out an initial public offering.Anthropic's response? Let's go!"Very few companies would've attempted it in this environment," noted Byron Deeter, a partner at Bessemer Venture Partners, which counts Anthropic as its biggest investment ever, in an interview. "These things collectively have been massively disruptive, yet the team stuck to its true north and got the largest financing in IPO history done, and it's just remarkable.".

Anthropic's stock jumped 17% in its Nasdaq debut on Thursday, closing at $97.17 per share and giving the Claude creator a market cap of roughly $1.8 trillion. That makes it the seventh-most valuable U.S. tech giant, despite being less than 6 years old.

Earlier in the day, the second floor of the Nasdaq was decorated in orange, Anthropic's signature color. Founders and backers were given Claude lapel pins that served as their ticket to the bell ringing, which was followed by a champagne toast ahead of the opening. With the first trade, the stock shot up to $111.50.

The previous afternoon Anthropic raised $81.3 billion, topping Elon Musk's SpaceX for the largest IPO on record.

It has been revealed that it was a wild feat, and Anthropic backers breathed a sigh of relief. Days earlier, Altimeter Capital's Brad Gerstner, whose firm put in an order for $5 billion of stock in the IPO, acknowledged that the election results, the semiconductor sell-off and the Huang scandal created "a very bad backdrop" for the offering. Nvidia shares are headed for their seventh straight weekly drop, losing 38% of their value over that stretch.

Recent findings suggest that malcolm Ethridge, managing partner at Capital Area Planning Group and a CNBC contributor, pointed to the high levels of volatility in the market as a real risk to going public. The Cboe Volatility Index (VIX) is up 46% this month, including a 16% surge on Thursday, to over 28.4.

Ethridge stated that with the VIX above 25, most companies would consider "shelving" an IPO. But Anthropic decided, "this is our window," Ethridge said.

In the face of stout headwinds, investors told CNBC that the combination of Anthropic's historic growth rate (1,200% year over year), its adjusted operating profit and its openness to constructive feedback from Wall Street contributed to this week's gravity-defying moment.

"Just truly, truly market redefining," Iconiq partner Will Griffith, an Anthropic investor, stated in an interview. "The ability to achieve this growth, at this scale, in this short of a time frame, this efficiently — the world has never seen that before.".

Sources indicate that anthropic CEO Dario Amodei co-founded Anthropic in 2021, after he and a handful of other researchers defected from OpenAI, now its chief rival, to pursue what they viewed as a safer approach to AI. The organization captivated the corporate world with its Claude AI models and products, which fueled its unprecedented rise.

With tens of billions of dollars flooding in over the past few years from private investors and tech giants, Anthropic was able to secure the computing power needed to train frontier AI models and serve customers with an expanding portfolio of services.

It has been revealed that anthropic executives, including CFO Krishna Rao, used those funding rounds as opportunities to build goodwill with the market, particularly when it came to pricing. The company opted not to squeeze every last dollar out of the ecosystem, preferring, in Amodei's words, to "underpromise and overdeliver.".

In a recent development, "This isn't a company that's focused on optimizing price," Deeter remarked. "They could have priced the IPO higher. At every financing they have chosen to leave some money on the table.".

Anthropic has also worked to prove itself as a company that can deploy capital responsibly. Of the more than $140 billion that the company has raised, it's only spent around $10 billion, which helped to reassure investors that it could weather a potential storm.

Sources indicate that the organization confidentially filed its prospectus with the Securities and Exchange Commission in June, and OpenAI, followed days later. But by August, OpenAI executives signaled that the ChatGPT maker was targeting a 2027 debut, as a series of security incidents rattled the industry and opposition to data centers swept the nation.

The market, stung by rising oil prices and the Federal Reserve's proposal to raise interest rates, soured on the AI trade and lost its appetite for IPOs. Biometric ring maker Oura, Australian AI data center operator Firmus and SK Hynix's U.S.-based Solidigm subsidiary all postponed or abandoned intends to go public.

Gerstner said Altimeter reduced its exposure to the broader AI trade to the lowest in three years. Meanwhile, the firm aimed to put five times as much into Anthropic's IPO as it had invested in any prior initial share sale, Gerstner told CNBC.

Anthropic began testing the waters with investors in August, before kicking off its official roadshow last week. It was all taking place while many AI stocks were melting down.

Having successfully reached the public market, Anthropic now has a boatload of much-needed cash on hand as it continues to spend aggressively on compute capacity.

Recent findings suggest that assuming underwriters exercise their option to buy all the shares available to them, Anthropic's fundraise would total $93.5 billion in the IPO, topping the $85.7 billion raised by SpaceX. Add to that another $26 billion from a parallel share sale to a handful of tech companies, and the more than $130 billion in cash and equivalents on Anthropic's balance sheet at the end of September.

It's only been one day for Anthropic as a public manufacturer, but the AI lab's financial standing is about as solid as anyone could have hoped.

Industry observers note that "Today puts the organization in a stronger financial position, and in doing so, only strengthens our resolve to do the right thing," Amodei stated in a speech at the Nasdaq on Thursday. "If we can show it's possible to make the right choices on safety and still build an incredibly successful company, others in our industry will be inspired to do the same.".

— CNBC's Leslie Picker and Jordan Novet contributed to this report.

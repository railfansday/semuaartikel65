Apple introduces upcoming app to study how people use AI for health

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Big AI labs like OpenAI and Anthropic have made their bets on offering chatbots that can answer health-related queries with specialized features. Apple is debuting a new study to understand how people use AI for health. But it isn’t quite following other AI startups just yet; it wants to study how clinically grounded answers can help people.

Industry observers note that the iPhone maker is partnering with healthcare-focused AI firm OpenEvidence to launch a new study app. Apple is inviting users in the U.S. over 21, using iPhone 15 or later, and running iOS 26 or later, with Apple Intelligence enabled. The company is aiming to run this study with up to 200,000 participants for more than 12 months.

It has been revealed that the latest app will allow users to ask health-related questions, as they do with ChatGPT or Claude, with answers grounded in clinical data, from sources including The New England Journal of Medicine, Journal of the American Medical Association, and Nature. Apple said that the app will try to give simplified answers to everyday medical questions. Users can rate those answers and provide feedback about the responses; they’ll also be asked to participate in monthly surveys.

Participants can share data from HealthKit such as resting heart rate, activity, sleep, or lab results to get more personalized and pointed suggestions from the app. The manufacturer said the app can also help prepare participants better for a doctor’s visit, and it will guide consumers to specific providers for answers related to concerning medical problems.

Industry observers note that apple specified that the data will be anonymized before sending it to a secure third-party cloud provider, and it won’t be used to train any kind of AI models. The company said that OpenEvidence deletes the query and response data after 24 hours.

According to recent reports, “At Apple, we believe responsible innovation starts with scientific rigor and clinical grounding. Health is deeply personal, and conducting a dedicated research study is the most thoughtful way to understand how people engage with, comprehend, and evaluate health AI in everyday life,” Sumbul Desai, M.D., Apple’s vice president of Health and Fitness, mentioned in a statement, adding, “Insights from this study will help advance the broader scientific conversation and ensure that as technology evolves, we continue building trusted tools that genuinely support people wherever they are on their health journey.”.

The app is available to download starting today for U.S. owners. It’s not clear if Apple wants to release AI-powered answering to its Health app or Siri yet. The potential is huge — earlier this year, OpenAI stated users were asking over 300 million health-related queries each week.

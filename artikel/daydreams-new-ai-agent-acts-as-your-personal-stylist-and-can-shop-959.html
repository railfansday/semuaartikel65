Daydream’s new AI agent acts as your personal stylist — and can shop for you, too

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that can an AI agent serve as your personal stylist? That’s what e-commerce veteran Julie Bornstein’s fashion startup, Daydream, aims to find out.

On Tuesday, the company launched “Daydream Agent,” an AI agent that is described as a personal stylist that can help you proposal a look, virtually try on clothes, surface relevant pieces based on your taste, and more.

The idea, the company explains, is to put a personal stylist in everyone’s pocket. This application of AI could be promising, given that early adopters of consumer AI agents like Meta’s Muse have found that online shopping is a key use case.

Meta CEO Mark Zuckerberg recently noted that shopping was a top activity for Muse’s over 10 million owners and that 25% of people who set up Muse now use it daily.

It has been revealed that in Daydream’s case, the fashion startup has been focused on using AI to deliver personalized shopping results to its owners. Its new AI agent builds on its earlier work with an AI chatbot that could help users find outfits and make suggestions, but not complete purchases.

In a recent development, now the firm says its Daydream Agent expands on that experience by remembering each shopper’s own personal style, fit preferences, and budget to refine its recommendations. Shoppers can also upload a photo to have the agent reuse that image to virtually try on any item they’ve saved. When they find something they like, the agent can complete a purchase through a one-click checkout process.

The agent can also handle other fashion-related tasks, like keeping an eye on when items go on sale or when brands have a new drop, or providing weekly updates or spotting new fashion trends.

Also, instead of leaving items in their cart, shoppers can save pieces they’re interested in to a persistent “clothing rack” so they can compare options and think about their purchases before deciding what to buy. Shoppers can also connect their own closet and their calendar to the agent, helping them to build outfits around what they already own and plan what to wear for upcoming events.

It has been revealed that the tech works by pulling real-time pricing and availability information from its catalog of over 10,000 brands, then combines that with its fashion-focused artificial intelligence. It’s powered by a combination of OpenAI, Gemini, and Claude models, which it fine-tuned, along with its own trained models designed for particular use cases.

Sources indicate that however, considering that fashion is more of an art than a science, the firm says it’s keeping humans in the loop. To do so, this month it brought on its first stylist in residence, Francesca Salih, an international fashion stylist and the founder of StyleGrid. Salih provides the startup with styling content that helps shape how the agent pulls together outfits and offers advice. This advice is part of the company’s fashion knowledge database, which the agent experience relies on.

Bornstein, formerly Stitch Fix’s COO and the founder of The Yes (exited to Pinterest in 2022), started Daydream in 2023 as an AI search engine. The organization, backed by $50 million in funding from Forerunner Ventures, Index Ventures, GV, and True Ventures, says more than 2 million shoppers have used Daydream to discover apparel and accessories since its June 2025 launch.

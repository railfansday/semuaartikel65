After a full season of cutthroat legal proceedings, War's central divorce trial between actor Carla Duval (Sienna Miller) and tech mogul Morgan Henderson (Dominic West) has reached its final verdict. The winner? No one. But the loser? Also no one.

It has been revealed that that's because, after some eleventh-hour heroics from mole Johnny Warren (Archie Renaux) and honeypot Flora Monroe (Rose Williams), Carla and Morgan reach their own settlement, independent of their lawyers and the judge. Following their decision, Carla will receive 50 percent of Zephyr's shares and monthly payments from Morgan until he retires. Carla and Morgan will share custody of Carla's son, Gabe (Aran Murphy). In short, it's basically what Carla had been asking for this whole time, and what the pair could have achieved without going to court.

Their decision stems from hearing Flora read Morgan's conciliatory letter to Carla, which St. John Smallwood (Pip Torrens) tried to dispose of, aloud in court. For Carla and Morgan, the letter is a reminder of the good in their relationship, something they sorely needed to recall after months of highly public mud-slinging.

"It tied itself up with humanity and kindness, and that was unexpected for me when I was reading it," Miller told Mashable in a junket interview alongside West. "I think the moral of that being that had these two people dropped their ego and their pride and nasty covetousness, if they'd let all of that go, they could have probably resolved this without the hideous experience of taking each other to court.".

"It felt like a spectacular waste of $10 million on lawyers' fees," West joked.

For series creator George Kay, there was never a version of War Season 1 where the court ruled in favor of one side or the other.

Sources indicate that "In the majority of these significant divorces, the real winners are the lawyers, and so we wanted to take that agency away from the justice system and put it back in the hands of [Carla and Morgan]," Kay told Mashable in a junket interview. "So, it was always going to be down to them to work it out, which is the big expensive irony of what they've been through.".

Sources indicate that by the end of Season 1, Carla and Morgan have found what seems to be an affectionate alliance. Even though Carla now has the Zephyr shares she asked for, she won't be using them against Morgan to trigger the sale to Michael Ancker (David Menkin), as Zephyr CEO Gillian Pope (Angel Coulby) wanted. Instead, Gillian is being ousted, as it turns out she leaked the security footage of Flora leaving Morgan's hotel room to the press.

In War's final scene between Carla and Morgan, the pair stand, at peace, on a Normandy beach by their cottage.

"I'll always love you," Morgan tells Carla.

Industry observers note that as it seems that the pair is about to go their separate ways, Carla calls Morgan back up to the cottage for a hot chocolate with Gabe. It's proof that their life as a pairing isn't over. It's just taken a different shape. And maybe thanks to that, both Carla and Morgan "won" in the trial after all.

Industry observers note that "The end of a divorce, it's not like someone's dead," West mentioned. "You either never speak to each other again, run screaming through the streets, or you realize actually why you love the person in the first place. I think that's what happens to Morgan in the end.".

"We described this divorce at the world's most expensive couples therapy, because it's only by going through everything that they've been through, hearing what the other one thinks via their lawyers, emotionally reengaging with everything they've had in their relationship, that by the end they realize what an amazing thing they have, and so we leave them okay," Kay said. "They're divorced, technically, but actually they're much better parents and they're much closer emotionally, and that was always a North Star for us.".

It has been revealed that the sweet ending for Morgan and Carla is at odds with the rest of War's bloodthirsty, win-at-any-cost tone. But for Kay, that was intentional.

It has been revealed that "We didn't want it to be bleak or dark," Kay remarked of the ending. "We wanted it to be redemptive.".

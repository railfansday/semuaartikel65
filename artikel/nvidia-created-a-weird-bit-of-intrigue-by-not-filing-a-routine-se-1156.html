Nvidia created a weird bit of intrigue by not filing a routine SEC report

Industry observers note that during Nvidia’s quarterly call on Tuesday, CFO-turned-interim-CEO Colette Kress dropped this little oddity: She said Nvidia wouldn’t be immediately filing its quarterly results report, known as a 10-Q, with the SEC. “We plan to file our 10-Q in the coming weeks.” And she offered no reason why.

It has been revealed that the delay was widely noticed. Among the many comments on X about it, economic and investment analyst Edward Dowd called it a “red flag.” Nvidia has typically filed the 10-Q the same day it reports results.

The 10-Q offers far more detail about a manufacturer’s operations than an earnings press release, including sections that disclose “legal proceedings,” which include lawsuits that could affect the company; and “risk factors,” which cover other potentially business-impairing items.

It has been revealed that the announcement of the delay was so unusual that UBS analyst Timothy Arcuri asked about it after the public earnings call. Nvidia told him the delay was related to whether the company would need to disclose additional information about its ongoing investigation into Huang in the 10-Q.

“The company unveiled that the 10-Q will be filed ‘in the coming weeks’ – later than usual. When queried on our callback, management indicated this was related to the ongoing investigation and whether any additional risk factors would need to be added to the filing,” Arcuri wrote in his research note. (Arcuri still rates the stock a “buy” by the way, although he did lower his price target to $220 from $240. The stock is now trading around $150).

Notably, on the same day that Nvidia reported earnings, The Wall Street Journal reported that the SEC is investigating Nvidia over whether the manufacturer’s disclosures misled investors.

In a recent development, so the mystery thickens: What will Nvidia disclose? We’ll have to wait and see. (Nvidia has not yet responded to our request for comment.).

Sources indicate that the delay suggests the manufacturer may have more to say about its mounting legal troubles. Nvidia is now facing multiple federal investigations over allegations that its partners were selling restricted Nvidia AI chips to China. So far, the scrutiny has come from a number of congressional committees; the Department of Justice looking into Megaspeed, the company run by a woman with whom Huang had an affair and who is also suspected of being a Chinese spy; and now, reportedly, the SEC.

Additionally, Nvidia is conducting its own internal investigation into Huang after he admitted to having an affair with Alice Huang (the Megaspeed executive), and allegations surfaced that he also had affairs with other Nvidia employees.

In a recent development, there’s little doubt that Wall Street will scrutinize that 10-Q once it’s finally filed. The intrigue brings to mind the old saying: “Doctors bury their mistakes. Lawyers put theirs in prison. And accountants hide theirs in the footnotes.”.

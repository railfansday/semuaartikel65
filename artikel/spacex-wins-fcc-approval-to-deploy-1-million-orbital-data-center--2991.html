SpaceX wins FCC approval to deploy 1 million orbital data center satellites

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Federal Communications Commission has authorized Elon Musk's SpaceX to put 1 million artificial intelligence computing satellites in orbit, Chairman Brendan Carr confirmed on Wednesday.

Carr said in a post on X, the social network owned by SpaceX, that the tech giant's Starmind constellation, and "competitor constellations will use solar power to extend America's lead" in AI.

It has been revealed that musk noted in a reply, "Much appreciated. This will be a great asset to America and the world.".

Industry observers note that the FCC, in its authorization filing, noted that SpaceX's application "received a substantial number of comments from the public, including petitions to deny filed by Kuiper Systems LLC (Amazon Leo), Viasat Inc. (Viasat), and the American Astronomical Society (AAS).".

In a March petition urging the FCC to deny SpaceX's application, Amazon had labeled the company's Starmind project as a "lofty ambition rather than a real blueprint.".

The e-commerce and cloud computing giant is building a competitor to SpaceX's Starlink satellite internet service, and noted it would take SpaceX "centuries" to build the constellation, adding that the company provided scant details about how it would "deliver on these grand claims.".

The petition resulted in a rebuke from Carr, who mentioned Amazon "should focus on the fact" that it's falling short of its own satellite deployment milestone.

Amazon declined to comment on the FCC's approval.

Other companies have also set their sights on orbital data centers. Google last year confirmed Project Suncatcher, which it describes as a "moonshot to put AI in space," and introduced its first chips into space on a SpaceX rocket and Planet Labs satellite in October.

In March, Jeff Bezos' Blue Origin petitioned the FCC to let it launch up to 51,600 satellites as part of a low Earth orbit satellite constellation dubbed "Project Sunrise." The constellation would work in tandem with Blue Origin's planned TeraWave satellite internet service, announced in January.

Astrophysicist and author Adam Becker was critical of the FCC move in an email to CNBC on Wednesday.

Sources indicate that "Any serious attempt at this is going to further pollute the night sky and runs a serious risk of a cascading chain reaction of space junk collisions, which would be a disaster for all of humanity," he wrote. "Musk and Carr are either profoundly stupid themselves, or hoping the rest of us are too stupid to notice this is nonsense.".

SpaceX did not respond to a request for comment.

The FCC mentioned in its authorization that it dismissed "comments and concerns raised regarding topics over which the Commission has no authority, including environmental concerns," and concerns about its impact on "optical astronomy.".

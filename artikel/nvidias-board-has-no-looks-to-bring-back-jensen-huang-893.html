Nvidia’s Board Has No Looks to Bring Back Jensen Huang

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

As Nvidia reported its third-quarter earnings on Tuesday, the company’s financial performance was overshadowed by the drama surrounding its former CEO. And Nvidia’s board made one thing clear right off the bat: Jensen Huang isn’t coming back.

At the start of the earnings call, Nvidia board chair Harvey Jones emphasized the firm’s strength and “groundbreaking technology” before turning to the circumstances surrounding Huang’s departure.

“On November 12, our board of directors declared that, after disclosure of personal behavior inconsistent with the company’s code of conduct, standards, and cultural norms, Jensen Huang voluntarily resigned his positions … and the board unanimously accepted his resignation and does not intend to reinstate Jensen as CEO,” Jones said.

Asked about the organization’s strategy for retaining talent and navigating the leadership transition, interim CEO Colette Kress emphasized the importance of the company’s workforce. “Our employees are absolutely instrumental to everything in this company,” Kress said. “We will take care of our employees during this transition to make sure that they are safe and that the work can be done in the current environment.”.

Recent findings suggest that jones added that the Nvidia board is taking the search for a latest CEO “very seriously” and is considering both internal and external candidates. National security dynamics are changing the chip industry, Jones noted, and “our new CEO will have to navigate those waters very carefully. So the search will be thorough. We want to install the permanent CEO as quickly as possible, but we won’t expedite the CEO before finding exactly the right person.”.

According to recent reports, one recurring theme that emerged from the earnings call, aside from the Huang saga: Demand for Nvidia’s chips remains as strong as ever. Kress stated that Nvidia expects that it will keep growing throughout 2027 and into 2028, fueled by the rise of agentic AI.

Less than a week after Huang resigned as chief executive, Nvidia reported $115.8 billion in revenue for the quarter that ended on October 25, 2026. That exceeded analysts’ expectations of roughly $109 billion and was nearly double the revenue reported in the same quarter a year ago.

The vast majority of Nvidia’s revenue came from its data center business, which generated $107.6 billion, up 110 percent from a year ago.

Earlier this month, The Latest York Times reported that Huang had an affair with an alleged Chinese intelligence asset, who had served as an executive at a Singapore-based cloud provider called Megaspeed. Then, reports emerged that Nvidia’s longtime leader additionally had an affair with one of his direct reports; following Huang’s resignation, Reuters reported he had an affair with a second employee.

Huang’s relationship with the alleged Chinese operative has raised questions about what he knew about Nvidia’s advanced chips making their way into China despite US export restrictions. Nvidia had placed Megaspeed on its “no sell” list in June 2025, but The Newly introduced York Times reported that, according to third-party data, a subsidiary of Megaspeed “received shipments of more than $800 million of export-controlled Nvidia A.I. technology, including Blackwell and H200 chips,” which were subject to export restrictions.

According to recent reports, on the heels of Tuesday’s earnings, Nvidia’s stock rose in after-hours trading. The firm had lost more than $1 trillion in market value over the past month amid the fallout from Huang’s scandal.

Earlier on Tuesday, Huang released his first statement since his resignation.

Huang thanked allies for their support, writing he’d “seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA’s employees, investors, business partners, and customers for me to return as CEO.” He admitted to failing to “live up to the standards expected of everyone at NVIDIA,” adding he was “committed to cooperating with the Board’s independent investigation.”.

However, Huang concluded, if the board “decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the firm, and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.”.

Sources indicate that nvidia, which is headquartered in Santa Clara, California, has roughly 60,000 employees, and sentiments have varied in the wake of Huang’s departure. “In just three days we went from superhero cape to ‘Oops, I forgot my pants,’ and nobody’s quite sure how to swallow that,” an employee told WIRED last week.

But in his 33 years leading Nvidia, Huang earned a loyal army of supporters by minimizing layoffs and red tape. Some employees have been calling for his return to the helm. And a few tech industry heavyweights are similarly eager for Huang to be reinstated. Executives including Elon Musk, Replit CEO Amjad Masad, and Y Combinator CEO Garry Tan took to X to voice support for Huang, signing a petition to “Bring Jensen Back.”.

Huang’s activities are presently under investigation by at least four different entities: the FBI and the US Department of Justice, Congress, the US Securities and Exchange Commission, and an independent law firm appointed by Nvidia’s board. Industry sources previously told WIRED that Huang is unlikely to return to the company any time soon.

Interim CEO Kress, at the end of Tuesday’s call, offered what sounded like a rallying cry for anyone doubting Nvidia’s future without Huang.

It has been revealed that “Our momentum is real. Our demand is accelerating. Our supply is growing. Our customers and our partners are with us, and employees are committed,” Kress remarked. “We’ve got this.”.

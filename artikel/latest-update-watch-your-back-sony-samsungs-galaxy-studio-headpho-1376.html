Not since the release of 2014's Level Over headphones has Samsung stepped into the world of over-ear audio devices, and more leaks of its upcoming 'Galaxy Studio' headphones bring us one step closer to its potential unveiling.

We came across a wave of leaks back in October, where we first heard news that Samsung could challenge Sony and Apple with its own flagship headphones, but thanks to the eagle-eyed experts at SammyGuru, we now have illustrations and visual components that showcase the Galaxy Studio's versatile functions.

Recent findings suggest that from what we know so far, design-wise, the Galaxy Studio headphones come in two shades (black or beige), with collapsible ear pads for travelling. The headphones are also projected to have an 870mAh battery.

Samsung’s upcoming Galaxy Studio headphones have a flexible design. - Samsung is developing high-end Galaxy Studio wireless headphones that may launch with the Galaxy S27 series early next year. - FCC documents suggest the headphones have flexible earcups that fold flat for easier storage and carrying. - They support Bluetooth and Bluetooth LE, will come in Beige and Black, and would be Samsung’s first wireless headphones in over a decade. The reigning champion of our best wireless headphones, the Sony WH-1000 XM6 model, has clearly been an influence on the Galaxy Studio's development, but Samsung has also added its own flair to the device.

Just like its competitor, the Galaxy Studio packs most of its controls on the right ear pad. This supports touch gestures that trigger a multitude of functions, including tap to pause and play, double-tap to skip songs, triple-tap to go back, and there's even a touch-and-hold gesture that releases an AI assistant. It's pretty standard stuff for headphones, but the AI assistant integration gives it an extra boost.

As for its audio tools, it's more or less the same story. According to the animations leaked from the outlet, the Galaxy Studio has both Active Noise Canceling (ANC) and Ambient sound modes, with additional controls for ANC level, Ambient sound volume, and noise control level.

Just like Sony's range of over-ear headphones, it's likely that the Galaxy Studio will pack head tracking support, allowing spatial audio to adjust as you move your head. The headphones are expected to have an auto power-off tool where, if no audio is playing, the device turns off after a set period of time. In the APK, Samsung lists options for 30-minute, 60-minute, or 120-minute timeframe settings.

On top of this, the left ear pad will sport volume buttons, which Sony's flagship headphones don't have. Instead, you can adjust audio volume by vertically swiping up or down on the left ear pad.

As mentioned, the Galaxy Studio model will mark Samsung's first over-ear headphones release in 12 years, and between then and now, the audio game has changed drastically across the board among tech giants.

Of course, Samsung needs to take a couple of pages from the books of its rivals to play catch-up, but it's also focused on developing its own flagship features — most notably its versatile connectivity.

It has been revealed that in addition to its audio functions, the leaks point to two separate ports (USB-C and 3.5mm), which allow you to connect the Galaxy Studio headphones to a mobile device for wired listening. This is a connectivity feature that Sony has yet to integrate into its own audio hardware lineup.

It has been revealed that listening wirelessly via Bluetooth is convenient, but true audiophiles know that wired listening is the best way to bring out the lossless and hi-res quality of digital music streaming. That said, the leaks don't detail how much the Galaxy Studio headphones reduce latency, the USB audio bit depth, or sample rate, so it'll take a series of hands-on experiences to determine how the headphones perform in that department.

Sources indicate that the most frustrating part is that, at the end of the day, the headphones are still in development, so whether or not some of these specs make it into the final product is another story. But if everything lines up according to what we've seen so far, I fear that Sony, Apple, and Bose alike will start shaking in their boots.

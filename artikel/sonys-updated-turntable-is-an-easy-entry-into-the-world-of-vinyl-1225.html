Sony’s updated turntable is an easy entry into the world of vinyl

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

It has been revealed that record players and other retro tech are making a strong comeback, and I’ve recently rejoined the world of vinyl collecting. Lately, I’ve noticed myself turning to Spotify less frequently, so it felt like the perfect time to start using a record player again.

It has been revealed that choosing a turntable can be quite overwhelming, especially for beginners. That’s where Sony’s fresh PS-LX3BT turntable comes in.

Sources indicate that since 2019, Sony’s PS-LX310BT has been a go-to choice for vinyl newbies. Now that it has been discontinued, the PS-LX3BT emerges as its successor, keeping the same beginner-friendly approach while adding a few modern upgrades.

Sources indicate that currently priced at $399.99, the PS-LX3BT is designed for people who want to enjoy vinyl without getting too caught up in the technical side of things. It’s also a good option for those looking to upgrade from their first inexpensive turntable without having to learn how to set up or customize a more complicated audio system.

One of its biggest selling points is how easy it is to use. The PS-LX3BT features automatic playback, so you simply press start and the tonearm lifts, moves into position, and drops onto the first track for you. There’s no need to manually position the needle or worry about getting everything just right. It’s a small convenience, but one that makes the experience much less intimidating for newcomers.

Industry observers note that sony has also made a few welcome changes compared with its predecessor. The PS-LX3BT now uses USB-C for power and supports Qualcomm’s aptX Adaptive Bluetooth codec, allowing it to transmit audio at up to 24-bit/96kHz. It also has a traditional phono output alongside Bluetooth, so you can choose between a wired speaker setup and listening wirelessly.

According to recent reports, one slightly strange design decision, however, is the repositioning of the start, stop, and tonearm buttons. On the older model, these were placed outside the dust cover. Now they’re underneath it, meaning you’ll have to lift the cover just to press start. It’s not a huge inconvenience, but still kind of annoying.

Recent findings suggest that the turntable also offers USB connectivity for digitizing your vinyl collection. Connect it to a computer, and you can convert your records into digital audio files, which is useful if you want to preserve older records or listen to your collection on the go.

In a recent development, my biggest disappointment is the lack of a built-in speaker. It would have been nice to have everything included for someone just getting started. You’ll need to buy speakers (and possibly an amplifier) or compatible Bluetooth headphones separately, so keep that added cost in mind.

Industry observers note that if you’re searching for a more affordable and beginner-friendly automatic record player, there are cheaper options, such as this $179 option from Audio-Technica. But overall, Sony’s PS-LX3BT is a solid choice for anyone ready to dive into the world of vinyl for the first time.

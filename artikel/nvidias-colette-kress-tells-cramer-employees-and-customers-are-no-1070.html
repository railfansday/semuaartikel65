Nvidia's Colette Kress tells Cramer: Employees and customers are not going anywhere

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia's customers and employees remain committed to the firm despite last week's abrupt departure of longtime leader Jensen Huang. That's the message interim CEO Colette Kress conveyed exclusively to CNBC's Jim Cramer shortly after the AI chip giant delivered a quarterly beat and strong guidance.

Sources indicate that "[Hyperscalers] are the ones that are right now feverishly looking for more focus in terms of the compute that we have available," Kress said on "Mad Money." "This concept that anything is changing in terms of any of our types of customers — that is just not true. They are all continuing to be with us, and we have a very deep breath of different customers.".

Kress' comments came after Nvidia reported better-than-expected quarterly earnings and revenue and bumped up its fiscal 2028 revenue growth estimate to 75% from the previous 70%. It was the first earnings report since Huang's resignation as CEO amid a widening affair scandal. Shares rose nearly 4% in after-hours trading as the results helped ease concerns about the chipmaker's outlook following the leadership shake-up.

Some on Wall Street have wondered whether Huang's departure would prompt certain Nvidia customers to lean harder into their in-house designed chips and/or competitors such as Advanced Micro Devices.

Industry observers note that during the "Mad Money" interview Tuesday evening, Kress emphasized that major customers have continued expressing interest in the organization's products. "I have seen already with many of these [customers] contacting me and saying, 'Great to see everything intact. Great to see that there's more supply. I need more demand.'".

According to recent reports, customer demand, however, wasn't the only issue facing Nvidia. Huang's departure has also raised questions about whether the chipmaker can retain the engineers and other employees who helped build its dominance in artificial intelligence. The co-founder was widely viewed as the driving force behind Nvidia's technology and corporate culture.

Industry observers note that kress dismissed concerns about a potential employee exodus, stressing to Cramer that the workforce's longstanding commitment to the manufacturer is intact, and so is support from the leadership team. "The company has been built under the perspective that the employees look at Nvidia as their life work, and many of them have been here for many decades, working with us both from a technology standpoint or a business and operations perspective," she said.

"We have high confidence that our employees are definitely still working, still working with us, and enjoy ... the work here at Nvidia," Kress told Cramer.

I am traveling through Portugal now that Web Summit has wrapped up, so please enjoy this shortened edition.

Web Summit leaned heavily into Chinese founders and companies this year. The opening remarks from Web Summit co-founder Paddy Cosgrave drove the point home when he stated the superstars of the future are coming from China, not California. He also gave a strong endorsement of China and open source technology when he noted that Chinese companies are building more of our shared future than anyone else.

These remarks carried a different kind of weight just a few days later as the news spread throughout the conference that Nvidia founder and CEO Jensen Huang had resigned from his leadership post after admitting to an affair with an alleged spy.

The former CEO, after all, has been one of the strongest proponents for open source in the United States. And anyone who has followed the emergence of autonomous vehicle tech knows how central Nvidia is to that ecosystem.

Nvidia is a major supplier of AI computing technology to the industry — although the degree of reliance varies. Some companies are using Nvidia’s automotive processors to run automated driving software, while others are using Nvidia GPUs to train autonomous driving models. And then there are the full-stack partnerships, which includes Mercedes-Benz, Zoox, and Wayve.

I spoke to Leo Wang, the CFO of Chinese autonomous vehicle manufacturer Pony.ai, this week. I asked him about the challenges China’s self-driving industry faces and what he thinks will happen if the fallout from Huang’s resignation results in even stricter export controls on Nvidia chips shipped to China.

Recent findings suggest that demand is already far greater than supply, Wang said, noting that it’s one of the pain points Pony.ai struggles with. The company’s strategy is twofold. Engineers are directed to be as efficient as possible when deploying its training models. That might mean using midtier compute resources instead of high-end ones, he said.

Pony.ai is also turning more to domestically produced chips to help close the gap.

Industry observers note that “If you look at the whole ecosystem, especially in recent years, definitely domestic chips in China also get lots of attention and development,” he said, noting that these chips provide another option for AI training. “I think that gives us some flexibility.”.

When asked what he thinks will happen if export restrictions on Nvidia chips tighten, Wang mentioned Chinese companies have already been preparing.

It has been revealed that “If you look at the whole situation, this has been developing for already a couple of years,” he stated. “Everyone, I would say, is also practicing kind of like a Plan B for a few years, so at least if you think about that, I would say there are alternatives. Although cost-effectiveness-wise, probably it’s not the best choice yet. But still, it’s an alternative way that already exists and which is more mature than a few years ago.”.

The National Highway Traffic Safety Administration is investigating a Waymo software recall from a year ago that was issued after numerous reports that its robotaxis were illegally passing school buses. The agency mentioned that a majority of these reported incidents occurred after the company deployed a software revision intended to fix the problem. Sources familiar with Waymo tell me that there have not been school bus-related incidents since March. I have not been able to independently verify that with the Austin Independent School District.

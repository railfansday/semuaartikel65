Lagarde says EU funds should take first losses on chip plants, data centers

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

European Central Bank President Christine Lagarde stated Friday that EU public funds should take the first losses on major chip-plant and data-center projects to help attract private capital.

"Where we agree that a challenge requires a European solution, we should strengthen the European institutions that can provide it," she said in her final address to the Frankfurt European Banking Congress before she steps down as head of the ECB in February.

"In technology, where projects such as chip plants and data centres need European scale, it means public-private partnerships at European level, with public money, including from the EU budget, taking the first losses and drawing in private capital.".

Lagarde has previously warned Europe does not have enough data center capacity to meet its own demand, and that existing plans for a network of up to seven gigafactories in the European Union do not go far enough.

According to recent reports, but it comes as pick-and-shovel projects tied to the artificial intelligence boom are increasingly controversial, complex and costly, from Europe and Asia to the U.S. CBS exit polls indicate that opposition to data centers was a major factor for over half of voters in the Nov. 3 midterm elections.

In her Friday speech, which reflected on her over-seven-year term, Lagarde cited older challenges facing the EU — including competitiveness and the long-running struggle to complete its capital markets union — and newer issues, including the bloc's dependence on imports for critical raw materials and technology.

"By the time everyone can see the need to act, the best time to act has usually passed. Acting early, before the case is obvious, is one of the hardest things a policymaker has to do. It means paying a price up front for a distant pay-off," she stated.

Recent examples of the EU's decisiveness have included its 800 billion euro ($877 billion) pandemic recovery fund, its geopolitical and financial support for Ukraine and its steps to drastically hike defense spending, particularly in Germany, Lagarde noted.

However, she mentioned that every year without a fully integrated capital markets and supervisory system meant that "more of our savings are invested abroad and more of our growing firms look elsewhere for funding.".

She also cited a recent estimate that raising Europe's share of global AI computing capacity from around 5% to 15% by 2030 will require investment of around 1.3 trillion euros.

"But as long as we can buy computing power from foreign providers and frontier AI models remain available to us, the case for paying that price is hard to make," she stated.

It has been revealed that "In both, the cost of inaction is real, whether it is a loss we are already suffering or a danger we may face tomorrow. But it rarely becomes visible enough to build the broad consensus for change that we found for the pandemic or for Ukraine," Lagarde told the audience in Frankfurt.

Recent findings suggest that "This is the central problem of European policymaking. We can see the problem early, but we can act only once it has become impossible to ignore. And by then, almost by definition, it is too late. Lost industries are very hard to win back, and once a dependency has been used against us, our sovereignty is already exposed.".

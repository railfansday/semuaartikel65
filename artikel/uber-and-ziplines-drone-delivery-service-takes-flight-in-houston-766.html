Industry observers note that uber Eats is officially launching drone delivery in partnership with Zipline starting Tuesday. The initial rollout begins in the Houston suburb of Spring, Texas, with Dallas next in line and dozens of additional US cities planned.

Sources indicate that the rollout comes after Uber and Zipline first declared their tie-up, including an investment in Zipline by Uber, back in August 2026. The companies have set the ambitious goal to hit 1 million drone deliveries per day by the end of 2029.

Industry observers note that spring residents in the coverage area will see a fresh “Drone” icon in the Uber app’s home screen to browse participating restaurants. At checkout, customers will need to confirm a clear drop-off zone in the yard, and can track the drone in real-time via the app. Because the system is fully autonomous, tipping is disabled — though customers can still rate the experience in the app.

Unlike other low-hovering delivery drones, Zipline’s aircraft cruise at over 300 feet at speeds up to 70 mph. The drone hovers high overhead and lowers a tethered delivery pod onto the customer’s doorstep or yard. The drones dynamically calculate flight paths based on wind, altitude, and speed — dipping their noses to accelerate or pitching upward to brake as quietly as possible. The drones are built to fly in rain and poor weather, carrying up to 8 pounds of cargo within a 10-mile radius of the restaurant.

It has been revealed that the initial plan is to operate daily from 8AM to 10PM, Uber notes. Customers can order from national chains like Jersey Mike’s and Zaxby’s, alongside dozens of Houston-area staples such as Urban Bird and Abu Omar Halal.

According to recent reports, to be sure, the “1 million deliveries a day by 2029” target is very aggressive, especially considering that Zipline presently averages roughly one delivery every 20 seconds globally, or around 4,300 a day. Still, delivery drones are becoming a more widely adopted technology in many communities across the US, with Uber competing alongside other players like Amazon Prime Air and Google-backed Wing.

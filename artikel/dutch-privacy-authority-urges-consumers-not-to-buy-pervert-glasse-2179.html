📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

The Dutch Data Protection Authority is urging consumers not to buy AI-enabled camera glasses, warning that they compromise the privacy of nearby bystanders and risk the safety of their people.

The authority, whose recent statement falls just short of an outright ban, stated that use of the internet-enabled and always-on glasses to record people in public is “almost always prohibited.”.

Sources indicate that critics have dubbed these smart wearables as “pervert glasses” for their propensity to be used to video people without their permission, particularly to harass women. While some glasses have warning lights when customers are recording, privacy advocates have argued that these alerts are not clear enough. Some people have even made phone apps capable of detecting nearby smart glasses wearers.

It has been revealed that the Dutch data protection authority warned that the glasses also produce wrong results and unsafe results. In one test carried out by the Dutch authority, a pair of unnamed glasses “gave dating advice to an adult who indicated that they are interested in a minor.” The glasses also incorrectly read a medicine label and claimed to have called an ambulance when it had not.

The Dutch government’s advice follows a similar move by the Norwegian government, which in September remarked it was considering a ban on camera-enabled devices.

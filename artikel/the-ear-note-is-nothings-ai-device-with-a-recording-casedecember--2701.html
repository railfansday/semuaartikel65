The Ear Note is Nothing's AI device with a recording case—December launch confirmed

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

AI is everywhere, and now we're getting a closer look at an AI-focused device from Nothing. The firm teased it recently, and it might've gone the way you expected.

This week, Mark Gurman from Bloomberg (paywall) sat down with Nothing's CEO, Carl Pei, to discuss what the future holds for its AI device (via GSMArena). First, Gurman teased the device on X, saying the Nothing Ear (Note) is a pair of earbuds "that record and transcribe both in-person meetings and phone calls." Their conversation then shifted to the Ear Note's charging case.

The charging case looks like a proper case (or an old-school cassette recorder). This early glimpse highlighted something familiar: a massive "TALK" button. Pressing "TALK" reportedly lets users record themselves or what's going on around them through the case. While the case is more "external," the earbuds can record phone calls.

In a recent development, owners can also expect transcription. However, this is likely available through the Ear Note companion app. Ear Note will support summaries of previous conversations/recordings, as well as "various AI agents." Carl Pei added more context on X. Pei said, "AI is only as useful as what it knows about you. This is the first of many products building a hardware context layer for AI.".

The Nothing Ear Note is preparing to launch on December 8. Privacy has been a concern (for customers, as well as Nothing), and it was reported that the firm integrated safeguards.

We should've expected a device with some retro vibes after Carl Pei's teaser earlier this month. Pei gave a small glimpse of Nothing's "screenless AI device." He demonstrated the Ear Note's (as we've come to know it) red and green LED indicators. Pei also mentioned, "context doesn't live in a chat box." The teaser was kept extremely brief ahead of Pei's interview with Bloomberg.

Industry observers note that his X post added, "It has no new screen, and it couldn't have been an app." This is interesting because users on social media have likened it to the Essential Recorder app on Nothing phones. It certainly feels similar, but that's because we're trading in one recording app for recording functionality on a pair of earbuds and a case. December 8 will answer everything else we're curious about.

5 music streaming services that let you listen for free

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

A subscription isn't necessary if you can put up with limitations and just want to enjoy music.

Sources indicate that if you don't want to pay the ever-increasing subscription fees of paid music streaming services, there are alternatives. Some of the best music apps let you listen at no cost, provided you're willing to put up with ads and other limitations. You'll still have to pay for features like offline listening or lossless audio quality, but the music comes at a no-cost entry point.

It has been revealed that there's no single music service that does it all, but many of them come close. Spotify owes its title as the biggest music streaming platform to its freemium model and personalized discovery algorithm. YouTube Music also has a massive catalog, packing everything from hits to niche content it's built up over the years. And if you prefer radio stations for music discovery, Pandora and iHeartRadio have you covered.

Recent findings suggest that youTube Music's access to a treasure trove of videos from a time before music streaming services went mainstream makes it a good Spotify alternative. From live performances of yesteryear to video game soundtrack remixes, no other service comes close in sheer variety. It's a shame, however, that the track quality maxes out at 128kbps on its free tier.

Recent findings suggest that if you've already used YouTube for years, the app will adapt to your music tastes right away. While you'll still have to deal with ads, the free tier's biggest glitch is the lack of background playback. You have to keep your device's screen on to keep music playing unless you want to fork out $12 a month for the paid plan.

You also have the option of paying $16/month for YouTube Premium, which includes ad-free YouTube videos. and various other benefits if you're a consistent watcher. Though compared to other options here, YouTube Music's paid audio quality of 256kbps is a drawback.

In addition to decent sound quality (160kbps on the mobile and desktop apps), Spotify's free tier now lets you pick a song to play directly, instead of having to shuffle a playlist and hope for the best. The service's mix of manually curated playlists and ones assembled by its algorithm lets you hop between genres, moods and favorite artists with ease. Its "daylists" tie song choices to your mood throughout the day, while Discover Weekly playlist brings fresh picks based on your listening habits.

It has been revealed that aside from music, Spotify hosts a massive podcast catalog, ranging from former exclusives like The Joe Rogan Experience to The New York Times' The Daily. Audiobooks are on the table too, but those require a subscription. You also get to take part in Spotify Wrapped, a phenomenon that dominates social media at the end of every year.

Industry observers note that while limited track skips, the lack of fast-forwarding and offline downloads, and annoying ads aren't ideal, the app still gives you access to a large music catalog without charging you. If you want to unlock all features along with 24-bit/44.1kHz in FLAC quality, it'll set you back $12.99 a month for a Spotify Premium subscription. Though as with many of the items on this list, a family initiative can help keep the cost down.

If you're only an Amazon Prime member for the free shipping or Amazon Prime Video's shows and films, you're missing out on a significant benefit. Amazon Music's scrolling lyrics, perfect for karaoke sessions, might just tempt you to make the switch. Its large mainstream library can be accessed from smartphones, Echo speakers and your Fire TV.

Amazon Music Prime, the tier included with a Prime subscription, brings ad-free shuffle play (and offline listening for All-Access Playlists) but doesn't feature on-demand song selection or HD audio. You'll need to step up to an Unlimited subscription ($13/month, or $12/month with Prime) to access those features alongside lossless HD playback and Dolby Atmos support.

It has been revealed that even if you don't have Amazon Prime, a free tier is available for anyone with an Amazon account. It lets you stream music stations and playlists at 320kbps, albeit with ads and no offline downloads. While it's a solid service, it isn't worth switching to if you're already entrenched in another. Consider moving only if you've got a Prime subscription and haven't been taking advantage of the music streaming perks that come along with it.

Pandora does let you pick 64kbps songs on demand (after listening to an ad on the free version), but its flagship feature is how it creates stations based on genres and artists you pick. As you train it with thumbs-up and thumbs-down interactions, you get suggestions more aligned with your music tastes. Repeating a song or going back on a radio station also forces you to listen to an ad first, which can sour the experience. Its $5.99 a month Plus blueprint brings ad-free stations and offline playback, in addition to unlimited skips and a jump in bitrate to 192kbps.

In a recent development, iHeartRadio is another strong choice, with its selection of live AM/FM stations from across the US making it a great music discovery engine. Catch up with morning demonstrates and local news, or skip the algorithm and get straight to an Artist Radio station that streams at 128kbps. A $5.99 a month Plus tier gives you unlimited skips, but you'll have to fork out $11.99 for an All Access subscription if you want offline downloads and on-demand song playback.

SpaceX, Musk pledge to donate a share each to active Trump Accounts

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

SpaceX stated Wednesday it will donate one share of the company's class A stock to every active Trump Account, and CEO Elon Musk would personally match the gift.

Industry observers note that "SpaceX is building the infrastructure of the future and working to make life multiplanetary," the company said in a statement. "We believe ownership of the company creating these systems should extend to the next generation of Americans for their long-term benefit.".

The White House announced in October that nearly 70 million Trump Accounts had been created, including more than 60 million through auto-enrollment. It's unclear how many are considered active.

In a recent development, eligible children must have their account claimed to enable contributions from family members, friends and employers, and to receive the government's one-time $1,000 seed contribution. Auto-enrolled children can receive philanthropic contributions without a parent or guardian claiming an account, according to TrumpAccounts.gov.

Representatives for SpaceX and the U.S. Department of the Treasury did not immediately respond to a request for further details about the planned gift.

SpaceX Chief Operating Officer Gwynne Shotwell had previously agreed to make a personal gift of over 2 million SpaceX shares to Trump Accounts, with eligible account holders expected to receive them in December.

In October, Elon Musk posted on X that he would give a share of SpaceX to every kid in America next year.

Trump Accounts, also known as 530A accounts, are open to any U.S. child under 18 with a Social Security number.

According to recent reports, parents, guardians, grandparents and others can collectively contribute up to $5,000 per child per year in after-tax dollars up until the year before the beneficiary turns 18. The annual contribution limit is indexed for inflation after 2027.

Since the July 4 launch, families and friends have collectively contributed more than $1 billion to Trump Accounts. Total deposits now sit at more than $9 billion, CNBC first reported.

According to recent reports, children born from 2025 through 2028 may receive a one-time $1,000 deposit from the Treasury. Other funds may also be available, depending on certain criteria.

The Treasury rolled out temporary regulations at the end of September that allow Trump Accounts to hold stock donations, a move officials have noted is meant to encourage more philanthropic gifts.

Contributing stock can benefit donors, who are able to avoid capital gains taxes triggered by selling appreciated stock. But families can face restrictions related to selling that donated stock. The policy also means some children's wealth accumulation could be tied to the performance of individual companies.

It has been revealed that under U.S. Department of the Treasury guidelines, families cannot refuse stock gifted to Trump Accounts and generally cannot sell it until the earlier of five years or the end of the calendar year in which the beneficiary turns 17.

SpaceX shares have been under some pricing pressure as the manufacturer negotiates a possible merger with Elon Musk's automaker Tesla. The companies have established two special committees to handle merger negotiations, and hired advisers for a potential mega-deal.

CNBC's Kevin Breuninger, Kate Dore and Jess Dickler contributed to this story.

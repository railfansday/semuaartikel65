In an open letter to the CEOs of Amazon and eBay, a coalition of 155 child advocacy groups called on the online marketplaces to stop selling toys powered by artificial intelligence.

The letter argues that AI toys are "cheap, easy to buy, and largely untested." It lists numerous risks associated with the products, including sexually explicit content, unproven educational claims, privacy violations, and instructions for how to access and use harmful household items.

Recent findings suggest that the letter's publication follows the release of annual toy research conducted by U.S. PIRG. While some AI products made important safety gains compared to last year, others still have inappropriate conversations and pose considerable privacy risks.

The Alilo Smart AI Bunny, for example, told a tester where to search in their house for bleach, defined the word "fetish," discussed specific sexual scenarios, explained why lubricant can be helpful, and suggested using "padded cuffs, soft floggers, or feather ticklers.".

Industry observers note that amazon subsequently removed the bunny and another toy capable of sexual exchanges from its platform. The letter identified over 50 AI toys available for sale on Amazon's U.S. marketplace.

"These products should be shown to be safe before they reach children, not withdrawn one at a time after harm is found," the letter said.

The effort was led by the child advocacy groups Fairplay, Alliance 4 Children CIC, and Set@16. On Friday, Amazon told U.S. PIRG that it had removed toys as it investigated the alleged concerns.

It has been revealed that "Amazon is committed to the safety of children and families," the tech giant mentioned in a statement to PIRG. "We're working with our partners across the industry and government to ensure proper guardrails for these products are in place as this technology develops.".

eBay responded to the open letter in response to Mashable's request for comment.

"We are committed to protecting the buyers and sellers on our marketplace and we continually evaluate our policies and engage with regulators to ensure our marketplace remains safe, trusted, and responsive to evolving safety standards," an eBay spokesperson noted in an email.

Amazon told Mashable that it requires all products sold by third-parties to comply with applicable laws and regulations.

It has been revealed that "Amazon is committed to the safety of children and families," the organization remarked in an email. "We support efforts across the industry and government to put appropriate guardrails in place as this technology develops.".

The letter notes that California banned toys with companion chatbots for children younger than 16, beginning Jan. 2027. A bill introduced in Congress would also ban AI chatbots in toys.

According to recent reports, "You do not need to wait," the letter remarked, addressing Amazon and eBay. "Marketplaces are responsible for the safety of what they sell, and you already remove unsafe products.".

Breaking: Microsoft global outage: What the company has said so far

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Updated on Friday, Nov. 13 at 11:55 a.m. ET — Microsoft provided a statement to Mashable expressing "regret" for the outage. The manufacturer also remarked it didn't believe a cyberattack caused the outage.

"We’re seeing encouraging recovery progress, with service availability and email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected infrastructure. We know our customers rely on our services to get notable work done every day, and regret the impact these outages had on those who depend on us. Our teams are reviewing what happened and how we can prevent similar issues going forward." — Charles Lamanna, EVP, Copilot, Agents and Platform at Microsoft.

It has been revealed that updated on Friday, Nov. 13 at 4:59 a.m. ET — Microsoft says it's "seeing early stabilization and capacity improvements as recovery actions continue." The company also noted it has extended email timeout to 48 hours to help preserve delayed mail during this event.

Industry observers note that updated on Friday, Nov. 13 at 1:08 a.m. ET — Microsoft is still looking into the cause of the outage, but states that at present it is "not aware of any malicious activity that is causing this incident.".

Updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 10:58 p.m. ET — Unfortunately, Microsoft's efforts to bring servers back online aren't going as planned. It appears to have been unable to address the concern of them becoming unresponsive again, and has stated it is now searching for different ways to increase traffic capacity.

"We've monitored the status of the changes applied to improve serviceability and confirmed they're not taking as expected," Microsoft wrote in an enhancement on X.

Industry observers note that updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 8:07 p.m. ET — Microsoft has revised its list of features and services impacted by this outage, stating that it is affecting ones that interact with Exchange Online and email services. Microsoft Outlook, SharePoint Online, Teams, Copilot AI, and 365 are all continuing to have various issues, though Azure appears to be operational.

Updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 7:53 p.m. ET — Microsoft has issued a new update stating that the servers they've brought back online may become unresponsive again when they receive traffic. The manufacturer is currently investigating the concern, though in the meantime it's limiting the types of traffic going to these servers in some locations.

Updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 6:23 p.m. ET — Microsoft states that it is bringing some servers back online, and "they remain stable so far." People should expect a "gradual recovery.".

It has been revealed that "We're also shifting traffic to alternate routes wherever possible and observing limited recovery," Microsoft wrote in an enhancement on X. "In parallel, we've streamlined the process of returning the remaining affected servers to service.".

Updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 5:39 p.m. ET — As of this writing, the global Microsoft outage remains ongoing.

In a recent development, updated on Thursday, Nov. 12 at 3:55 p.m. ET — We've updated this story with the latest statements from Microsoft.

Microsoft had a really bad Thursday.

Starting in the early afternoon, Microsoft 365 enterprise software products experienced a major outage, which extended into Friday morning. On the platform DownDetector, more than 100,000 owners reported problems with Outlook alone, starting around 12:30 p.m. ET on Thursday.

The company has posted regular updates about the situation, both on social media and on its website.

On the manufacturer's official status page (which also went offline briefly, according to multiple Mashable reporters), Microsoft said that Exchange Online, Teams, SharePoint Online, Copilot 365, Defender XDR, and other services were experiencing concerns. At first, the status page indicated that the outage only affected Microsoft's enterprise software, Microsoft 365; however, as of this writing, the page lists Microsoft Consumer Products as also experiencing "service degradation.".

On Friday morning, the company finally provided an official statement about the global outage. According to recent reports, "We’re seeing encouraging recovery progress, with service availability and email delivery performance improving across a growing portion of affected infrastructure," mentioned Charles Lamanna, EVP, Copilot, Agents and Platform at Microsoft. "We know our customers rely on our services to get important work done every day, and regret the impact these outages had on those who depend on us. Our teams are reviewing what happened and how we can prevent similar challenges going forward.".

And in an enhancement posted to X, the organization said it had no reason to believe a cyberattack was the cause of the outage.

According to recent reports, "We will continue to share more information as we have it, and for now we can confirm that there is no indication this is the result of malicious or cyber activity. Our teams are reviewing what happened and how we can prevent similar problems going forward.".

Industry observers note that in an initial patch posted at 12:54 p.m. ET to X, the firm remarked that customers were having problems with Exchange Online mailboxes "in the Americas." Exchange Online is a cloud-based tool that provides back-end support for Microsoft Outlook.

However, by 1:07 p.m. ET, the tech giant acknowledged the problem was more widespread, affecting more services and regions. "While we're working to mitigate impact, we've confirmed the glitch may affect Microsoft 365 service connectivity for owners in any region," the company posted.

The Microsoft Status page was then updated with a notification that stated, "User impact: Owners may be unable to connect to multiple Microsoft 365 services.".

While a root cause has yet to be determined, Microsoft has at least shared a brief technical explanation for what's going on.

Recent findings suggest that "We've determined that the elevated load isn't driven by an overall increase in traffic, but by a large number of servers being unexpectedly unavailable, which is shifting load onto the remaining infrastructure," Microsoft noted on its status page, as well as in a thread on X. "We're investigating what's causing these servers to become unavailable while we work to safely return them to service.".

Later on in the X thread, Microsoft remarked it had not identified any recent change that correlates with the outage, but "the failed health checks may originate from a specific component that's responsible for processing server health." In the meantime, Microsoft has worked to prevent more servers from being impacted while it also tries to resuscitate the ones that are down.

Shortly before 4 p.m. ET, the Microsoft status page received another patch, stating that it is still troubleshooting and restarting servers.

"While validating the targeted change, we found that many of the affected servers have become unresponsive, which is preventing us from confirming whether the change is effective," Microsoft remarked. "We're restarting these servers so they can come back online with the change in place, and we're applying the same approach to additional affected portions of our infrastructure to validate the results more broadly.".

Recent findings suggest that by the evening, Microsoft's updates turned slightly more optimistic, as the manufacturer stated that some servers had already come back online. A 4:00 p.m. update stated that the company had "validated an alternative service health check method, which allowed a subset of affected servers to successfully return to service.".

However, by 5:09 p.m. on Thursday, the organization acknowledged that services remained offline.

"We’re opening all available pathways to restore service, as our actions taken thus far have not provided the anticipated relief," read an enhancement posted to its website.

As the outage continued into Friday morning, the tech giant finally reported progress toward recovery.

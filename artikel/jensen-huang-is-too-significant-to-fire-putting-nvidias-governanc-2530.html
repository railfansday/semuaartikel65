Jensen Huang is ‘too significant to fire,’ putting Nvidia’s governance in focus: analysts

Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO may have eased concerns over the chipmaker's leadership, but it has also brought renewed scrutiny to the company's corporate governance and its reliance on its founder.

Huang's abrupt departure and unexpected reinstatement exposed a deeper vulnerability at the heart of Nvidia, industry experts told CNBC, raising questions about board oversight and succession planning.

"What does it mean if you have a company too substantial to fail or a CEO too big to fire?" Angela Mancini, head of advisory for global risk insights at Dow Jones, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Asia" on Tuesday.

Mancini pointed to Huang's unusually large number of direct reports as another sign of how heavily Nvidia depends on its founder.

"What does it mean when someone has 60 direct reports, and you literally are in a situation where you feel like you can't fire them?" she added.

Sources indicate that nvidia's market cap ballooned from $3 trillion on Friday to almost $4 trillion on Tuesday after Huang was reinstated. "That is what you'd call a founder premium," Yang Wang, a principal analyst at Counterpoint Research, told CNBC's "Squawk Box Europe.".

It has been revealed that "The reaction has been staggering," Wang said. "But is this level of dependence healthy?".

For Nvidia, Huang's return offers an opportunity to address some of the governance challenges brought to light by his departure.

Jeff Herbst, managing partner at GFT Ventures and a former Nvidia executive who spent nearly two decades at the company, said he had written to the board late last week calling for Huang's return.

While initially disappointed by the allegations surrounding Huang, Herbst stated he came to view the episode as an opportunity for Nvidia to strengthen its governance, succession planning and overall management.

"While a lot of people saw a crisis here, I saw a potential opportunity," he said.

Herbst mentioned Nvidia's management and oversight functions needed to evolve alongside its transformation from a relatively small technology company into one of the world's largest corporations.

"I think the corporate governance, the succession planning, the legal function, the regulatory function, the political function, all has to scale at the same time," he stated.

He suggested Nvidia add more experience to its board by bringing in individuals who have run large companies and take a more formal approach to developing potential successors to Huang.

Tom Hulick, CEO of Strategy Asset Managers, agreed, saying the chip giant's "corporate governance definitely needs to be retooled.".

It's "unheard of" and "unprecedented" for a president to enforce the reinstatement of a company's CEO, Hulick said on "Squawk Box Europe" on Tuesday.

Sources indicate that "He's a born leader. He's enduring a lot of pain right now. Does he deserve to be back as CEO? It's not for me to speculate. Is he the right person? Probably for now.".

However, Hulick added that the organization still has "a lot of things to worry about" regarding corporate governance.

Huang's departure also exposed how closely Nvidia's leadership and long-term strategy are tied to its founder, raising questions about how the company would navigate a future transition.

Stacy Rasgon, managing director and senior U.S. semiconductor analyst at Bernstein Research, stated the episode had revived attention to a risk investors had long overlooked.

"We all knew that there was key man risk here. We all chose, and I'm including myself in that. We all kind of chose to just ignore it," Rasgon told CNBC.

Succession planning, he said, is an problem both Huang and the board will need to address and communicate clearly to investors.

"I think both Jensen and the board will have to figure out, and I think communicate to investors," he added.

Herbst also pointed to Huang's personal role in driving Nvidia's technological innovations, including his willingness to invest in artificial intelligence and high-performance computing long before those businesses became profitable.

New markets such as autonomous systems and robotics would require major investment and a leader with Huang's passion and vision, Herbst added.

Correction: This story has been updated to reflect that Angela Mancini noted Nvidia is in a situation where it feels like you can not fire the CEO.

Hello, this is Leonie Kidd with today's edition of Morning Squawk.

The Thanksgiving cheer is extending into the market this Friday, with a relief rally across the tech sector after a volatile week of trading.

The half-day session looks set to be driven by those buying the dip in some of the biggest AI names.

Stock futures are rising as Wall Street prepares to open higher following a volatile week for technology stocks.

Recent findings suggest that it's a shortened trading day following Thanksgiving, but the bulls are out, with S&P 500 futures up nearly 2%, while Nasdaq-100 futures advanced 2.3%. Futures tied to the Dow Jones Industrial Average were higher by around 1%.

But the devil is in the data. On Thursday, President Donald Trump used his Thanksgiving message on Truth Social to falsely claim that the latest GDP revision was a "record-setting error" that therefore had to be "immediately investigated.".

The second reading of third-quarter GDP on Wednesday indicated a 4.3% annualized growth rate, revised up from the initial estimate of 3.4%.

The technology sector is leading gains in premarket trading.

The Magnificent Seven, alongside chip-related stocks, neoclouds and server makers are all trading higher.

Nvidia is up over 4%, alongside Anthropic, Alphabet, Amazon, Microsoft and others.

There was a sharp rebound overnight for Chinese stocks, with the country's chip-makers notching their biggest-ever one-day gains.

SpaceX shares are higher in premarket trading after Elon Musk posted on X.com that he was expanding his share-donation plan for American children.

He remarked, "I will be donating another share of SpaceX to all kids in America every year." It comes alongside the group's commitment to allocate one SpaceX share to each active Trump Account.

CNBC's Morning Squawk recaps the biggest stories investors should know before the stock market opens, every weekday morning.

CNBC's Kai Nicol-Schwarz has published two scoops this week about the impact of the Trump administration's tightening of restrictions on Chinese remote access to Nvidia chips.

Recent findings suggest that first up, he reports that neocloud firms are being told they cannot purchase Nvidia chips if any of that computing capacity would be rented to Chinese companies. That's according to sources.

Secondly, the sellers of Nvidia chips are seeking newly introduced buyers as they look to find new homes for GPUs that were originally for use in data centers through which Chinese companies would rent access.

It has been revealed that what is 'texture-maxxing'? The food trend putting crunch on the menu.

Texture-maxxing is the latest food trend taking social media by storm as consumers, driven by aesthetics and taste, look for a sensory experience.

British supermarket Waitrose's latest food and drink report highlighted "texture-maxxing" as one of the trends of the year. It describes the phenomenon as a multi-sensory indulgence that is "fuelled by texture-maxxing crunch and intense flavour hits," pushing shoppers to fill their trolleys with crunchy peanut butter, dumplings, and trifles.

Grado's GW200 headphones boast fresh drivers and a longer battery life

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

They run $329 and are up for preorder now.

Grado has been a substantial name among audiophiles for decades. The manufacturer resisted making a wireless model until 2018 when it released the well-received GW100 open-back headphones. Now, Grado is back with a major refresh of those initial Bluetooth cans.

Recent findings suggest that the GW200 brings a spate of upgraded features to the table. First of all, there's a newly-designed audio driver. The 44mm S is based on the larger drivers found with some of its wired headphones, like the HP100 SE. The organization promises they delivery "clarity, detail and a full-bodied musical presentation while preserving the openness and natural character that define the Grado listening experience.".

Sources indicate that the battery life here has also been improved. The GW100 headphones topped out at just 15 hours. This was an problem even back in 2018. However, the GW200 can last around 60 hours per charge, which is more in line with competitors. They can also operate via a wired 3.5mm connection and this won't draw any power.

Sources indicate that they use Bluetooth 5.4 and support for a bunch of common codecs, including Qualcomm aptX Lossless, aptX Adaptive and SBC, among others. The headphones also include multipoint connectivity that can maintain two simultaneous Bluetooth connections. That should make it easy to switch audio sources on the fly.

According to recent reports, these are on the lighter side, especially when compared to the GW100 headphones. The GW200 cans weigh 235 grams and include a padded leather strap and an adjustable steel band. They even integrate with the organization's complete range of standalone headphone cushions, for increased comfort.

Grado's latest GW200 headphones are available to preorder right now and cost $329. Shipments should go out in December. Since launching the wireless GW100 headphones back in 2018, the company has also branched out into wireless earbuds.

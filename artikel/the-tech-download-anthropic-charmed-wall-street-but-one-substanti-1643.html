Anthropic's historic debut on Thursday saw the manufacturer defy a wider tech selloff, as investors paid a premium for the stock.

The five-year-old manufacturer, which raised $81.3 billion as part of the listing, is one of the first opportunities for public markets to value a pure play AI lab.

Many see the IPO as a possible savior of the AI trade after a shaky few months, but one big uncertainty hangs over the organization's future prospects.

"One of Anthropic's biggest challenges will be securing enough compute to support their steep growth trajectory," Gil Luria, D.A. Davidson, head of technology research, told me.

Industry observers note that access to the huge quantities of compute the tech giant needs to continue developing and deploying models is a big challenge for Anthropic, which didn't respond to a request for comment for this piece, to navigate. It also comes at a time when there's growing public opposition to major data centers and delays to some AI infrastructure projects.

According to recent reports, anthropic has remarked it has entered into purchase commitments and lease agreements for compute and infrastructure worth around $900 billion. The firm expects to incur roughly half of that cost before the end of 2030, with the rest between 2031 and 2045. Around $186 billion of that spend is tied to deals that can be terminated or subject to conditions like securing financing, facilities or regulatory approvals.

There have been signs of hurdles for access to some of that capacity.

It has been revealed that lambda missed a deadline to secure a facility lease for a $34.9 billion agreement, Anthropic said in a filing earlier this month. Nscale disclosed that it had yet to secure binding financing commitments for its Anthropic contracts, which are worth up to around $44 billion. Lambda didn't respond to a request for comment and Nscale declined to comment.

"In both cases the financing risk falls on the neoclouds and their lenders, not on Anthropic," Nick Patience, AI lead at Futurum Group, told me. But, he added, the "real question is whether that capacity arrives on time.".

It has been revealed that anthropic has cloud infrastructure commitments with Amazon and Google worth more than $100 billion each extending into the 2030s. It also could end up spending more than $100 billion with SpaceX to access AI compute.

So, no shortage of big-money deals for graphics processing units, or GPUs, access.

But even if some of the smaller deals collapse or take longer to get off the ground than expected, it could slow Anthropic down.

"Demand is running ahead of supply, so missing capacity turns into lost revenue very quickly," said Patience.

Margins could suffer too, he added, as a result of increased costs of short-term compute commitments.

Anthropic has said that long-term capacity is significantly cheaper than short-term commitments, which it would need to turn to when "customer demand or research needs exceed this long-term capacity.".

Capacity constraints will be a challenge Anthropic has to navigate.

"Throughout 2027-2028 we are expecting the firm to remain compute constrained, using a mixture of short-term compute to augment longer-term deals with the hyperscalers coming online," Malik Ahmed Khan, senior equity analyst at Morningstar, told me.

Some slippage in terms of AI infrastructure delivery is likely, noted Patience, but a "serious shortage is not, because its biggest suppliers are also its biggest investors and have every reason to keep it supplied.".

According to recent reports, google and Amazon both have stakes in Anthropic that are worth well over $100 billion each. That's some incentive to deliver.

Signal joins United Airlines, banks in limiting AI agents over customer risks

Privacy-focused messaging application Signal is tightening protections against artificial intelligence agents that can access information displayed on consumers' screens, citing concerns over privacy and security.

Signal remarked AI agents built into mobile operating systems, such as Google's Gemini and Apple's Siri with onscreen awareness, can "scan" information on people' screens, potentially capturing and storing Signal data outside its control.

The tech giant mentioned it will block screenshots in its iOS app and, on Android, enable screen security when it detects an AI assistant attempting to read the screen.

"We want to be very clear: it is a serious problem that Signal — and application developers beyond — lack robust tools to prevent AI agents from undermining application-level privacy," Meredith Whittaker, president of the app's nonprofit parent Signal Foundation, said in a Friday blog post.

According to recent reports, signal last year introduced similar restrictions against Microsoft's Recall AI on Windows 11. Its latest move adds to a broader push by companies including United Airlines, JPMorgan Chase and Charles Schwab to limit or block AI agents over security and for customer-protection concerns.

Whittaker remarked the new protections do not address every way an agent might gain access to data, including by retrieving content already stored on a user's device.

Signal also warned that third-party agents such as Meta's Muse could gain access to private app content through permissions granted during setup.

"Application-level protections will always have gaps, so the data an agent can reach has to be classified, permissioned and recoverable before the agent arrives," stated Tianyi Jiang, CEO of data management and protection software firm AvePoint.

AvePoint's State of AI 2026 report found that 88.4% of surveyed organizations had experienced at least one AI-agent-related security breach in the previous year. The survey covered 750 IT leaders globally.

"We continue to call on the major consumer operating system companies – Apple, Google and Microsoft – to provide simple technical tools and accurate documentation that give developers like Signal ways to prevent AI agents from accessing sensitive application data," said Whittaker.

Banks are also restricting third-party AI agents from accessing customer accounts.

Sources indicate that JPMorgan Chase CEO Jamie Dimon said in October that outside agents were too risky for the bank and its clients. Morgan Stanley has blocked third-party agents from its platforms, Reuters reported.

Meanwhile, Charles Schwab CEO Richard Wurster mentioned the brokerage blocks unauthorized agents to protect clients and the firm.

Singapore's OCBC Bank has stated it will not let third-party agents to interact with the bank to transfer money autonomously and warned that "customers should exercise caution" when using such agents for banking.

According to recent reports, united Airlines said Nov. 11 that it would restrict unauthorized agents from booking or managing tickets, citing risks that they could expose customer information, make unintended purchases or violate aviation rules.

"To book flights or do your grocery shop, an agent needs your email, accounts and payment details," remarked Mick Amelishko, an AI advocate at identity verification and compliance platform Sumsub.

He mentioned that level of access creates a risk of account hijacking because actions taken by an agent may appear to a retailer or bank to have been authorized by the user.

It has been revealed that singapore Airlines, by contrast, is an early partner of Meta's Muse. Meta said customers can ask the agent to explore travel, ticketing and reservation options through the airline.

In a recent development, "People treat agent risk as one-size-fits-all rather than matching the security needed to the actual task," said Yat Siu, executive chairman and co-founder of Animoca Brands. He said agents could help consumers act on their intentions and help businesses reach customers directly, but the risks vary by task.

It has been revealed that "There's a much higher risk around an agent able to move meaningful sums of money or access sensitive personal data, than one making your restaurant reservations," Siu said. "The reality is that, just like internet applications to date, agents require tiered safeguards.".

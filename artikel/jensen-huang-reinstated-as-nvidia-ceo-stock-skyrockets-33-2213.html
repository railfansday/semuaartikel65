Jensen Huang reinstated as Nvidia CEO. Stock skyrockets 33%

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

Nvidia on Monday announced Jensen Huang has been reinstated as CEO, effective immediately, a dramatic twist in a scandal that hammered the chipmaker's stock price and rocked the tech and artificial intelligence industries.

Recent findings suggest that the board of directors said it unanimously voted to reinstate Huang following "a series of discussions and meetings" with him. The board added that it "received input from senior levels of the United States government" and that it is confident Huang's reinstatement "poses no national security risk.".

President Donald Trump told CNBC on Monday that he had been speaking to Nvidia's board "directly and indirectly," and that Huang "did nothing wrong." Trump said he talked to Huang on Sunday and asked him to promise to "work hard.".

Huang resigned from his role as chief executive earlier this month after a series of shocking reports about multiple extramarital affairs, including one with an alleged Chinese spy. The New York Times reported on Sunday that the woman, Alice Huang, was in contact with an officer at China's Ministry of State Security during at least part of the time that she was engaging in a relationship with Huang.

Shares of Nvidia surged 33% following Huang's reinstatement. The stock had shed nearly 33% from the time Huang admitted to an extramarital affair on Nov. 8.

"I am honored to return to NVIDIA and lead one of the most consequential technology companies in the world at the beginning of a latest industrial revolution," Huang remarked in a statement. "I take this responsibility with the greatest humility and renewed commitment. I want to thank the NVIDIA Board for its confidence and deliberation through this process.".

In a recent development, senior government officials briefed Nvidia's board late last week and communicated that no national security laws were violated, according to sources familiar with the discussion who asked to remain anonymous because they were not authorized to speak publicly. That upgrade prompted the board to reach out to Huang, the people remarked.

Huang and the board held their first conversations since his ouster on Saturday, and the group determined he would return as CEO during a second meeting on Sunday, the people said. Nvidia's cratering stock price played a role in accelerating those conversations.

Recent findings suggest that trump celebrated Huang's return to Nvidia on Monday, writing in a post on his social media site that the board made "the correct and proper decision" to reinstate him. Trump stated the U.S. needs Huang's leadership to "ensure the Success and Prosperity of not only the Organization, but the American Economy.".

He reiterated that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" that Huang's return poses a risk to national security, and that it "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

"On a personal note, I have gotten to know Jensen, and he is a Great Patriot and National Treasure," Trump wrote.

Recent findings suggest that several tech industry leaders, including Elon Musk, the CEO of SpaceX, Gavin Baker, managing partner and CIO at Atreides Management, and Brad Gerstner, Altimeter Capital CEO, had been actively lobbying for Huang's return. Musk wrote in a post on X on Monday that the board's "mistake has been corrected.".

Nvidia did not immediately respond to CNBC's request for comment.

Industry observers note that for weeks following Huang's ouster, the firm's board maintained that it was not considering Huang's reinstatement. It appointed Colette Kress, Nvidia's CFO, as the chipmaker's interim CEO, who reiterated that position in an interview with CNBC following Huang's exit. Kress will continue to serve as Nvidia's CFO, according to a filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission.

Sources indicate that nvidia's board formed an independent committee and hired the law firm Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz to conduct an investigation into Huang and whether company secrets or national security were compromised. The board stated Monday that "the committee's investigation will proceed to conclusion" and that Huang is "committed to cooperating with it.".

"The Board believes Jensen is the right person to lead NVIDIA, and that his reinstatement is in the best interests of our stockholders, employees, partners and customers," Nvidia Board Chair Harvey Jones said in a statement. "We have every confidence in Jensen and look forward to working with him as he resumes his leadership of the Manufacturer.

Even with Huang back at the helm, Nvidia is facing increased scrutiny over its chip sales to Southeast Asia and China.

Lawmakers have been preparing for investigations into how Huang's affair with the alleged spy may have damaged U.S. AI dominance, influenced his positions on export controls and impacted his close relationships with Trump and his Cabinet. Huang has been one of the loudest voices opposing strict export controls on advanced chips.

According to recent reports, "The reckless decision to bring Jensen Huang back as CEO does not change the fact that Mr. Huang, NVIDIA executives, and the organization's board still owe Congress answers about the company's compliance with U.S. export controls," Sen. Elizabeth Warren, D-Mass., mentioned in a statement on Monday.

Industry observers note that huang is due for a closed-door transcribed interview with the House Foreign Affairs panel and two other committees on Dec. 1. Nvidia general counsel Tim Teter is scheduled to appear before the panels on Dec. 2. Those dates were set prior to Huang's return, and John Fisher, who represents Huang, did not comment on whether there are any chances in his looks to testify.

Sources indicate that "Americans deserve answers about what NVIDIA and the Trump administration knew, when they knew it, and whether sufficient steps were taken to prevent sensitive technology from falling into the wrong hands," Sen. Andy Kim, D-N.J., wrote in a post on X on Monday.

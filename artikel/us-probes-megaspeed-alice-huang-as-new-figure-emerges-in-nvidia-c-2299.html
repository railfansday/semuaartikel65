U.S. probes Megaspeed, Alice Huang as new figure emerges in Nvidia chip case

It has been revealed that the U.S. is investigating Alice Huang and her former company, Megaspeed, Director of National Intelligence Jay Clayton stated, as a report identified one of her associates as a key decision-maker at the Singapore-based company.

Clayton stated the investigation was "ongoing" on Sunday, the day before Nvidia revealed it had reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO, a position he resigned from in part because of his relationship with Alice Huang.

Industry observers note that alice Huang was a key figure in Megaspeed International, a cloud computing firm that operates data centers across Southeast Asia. U.S. intelligence reportedly believes she is a Chinese spy.

"There is an investigation ongoing into her firm and into her," Clayton told ABC on Sunday, adding that, "Nvidia chips, they are the jewel. They are the absolute top that everybody wants, that's why we're focusing on this.".

Nvidia's board revealed Jensen Huang's return before the opening bell on Monday, saying it had "a series of discussions and meetings" with him.

It added that it received input from senior levels of the United States government" and is confident that his reinstatement "poses no national security risk.".

Alice Huang's associate, Chinese national Li Ming, who is now out on bail in Singapore, was "deeply involved" in Megaspeed's activities and took part in major decisions, The Wall Street Journal reported earlier on Saturday, citing people familiar with the matter.

The Singapore charges against Li concern alleged false representations to Super Micro Computer, a U.S.-based firm with known links to chipmaker Nvidia, though Megaspeed is not named in those charges.

Last week, the Commerce and Justice departments said that the investigation into Megaspeed began in 2024 over potential export-control violations. Their joint statement said Commerce's Bureau of Industry and Security and Singapore law enforcement interviewed Huang during an on-site check in June 2025.

The Justice Department later took the lead in the criminal investigation.

Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent told CBS on Sunday that the administration was seeking answers from Nvidia about its chip sales to China, including who oversaw compliance and which levels of the tech giant knew about the activity.

According to recent reports, separately, The New York Times reported Sunday that Alice Huang was in contact with an officer of a top Chinese intelligence agency from spring 2025 through fall 2026, citing people briefed on U.S. intelligence reports.

Bloomberg reported last week that the Justice Department is investigating whether entities linked to Alice Huang helped smuggle Super Micro products to China.

Li faces five charges in Singapore, including two counts of fraud by false representation, along with allegations involving the operation of a firm he controlled, unauthorized bank-account access and another company director's duties.

Prosecutors allege he falsely told Super Micro that the company would hold and lease the servers it purchased.

It has been revealed that the Journal reported that Li is out on bail and that his case is ongoing. He was offered bail of S$1 million in March 2025. The Singapore charges do not name Megaspeed.

Earlier this month, Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs remarked that police had found insufficient evidence to establish that Alice Huang committed offenses related to the possible inaccurate declarations under investigation.

The ministry stated she was issued a stern warning for providing information that police assessed as knowingly inaccurate and for downplaying her role at Megaspeed. It also said Singapore had not investigated her for espionage.

Megaspeed has stated it is a "legitimate and lawful business" and that Huang left the firm in August 2025.

Industry observers note that the scrutiny is set to intensify in Washington. Jensen Huang and the company's general counsel, Tim Teter, have agreed to closed-door, transcribed interviews with three House committees on Dec. 1 and Dec. 2, respectively.

The committees mentioned they want answers about Nvidia's role in allowing Chinese actors to access advanced chips and the involvement of a potential Chinese intelligence asset.

Recent findings suggest that speaking to CNBC's Squawk Box on Sunday U.S. time, Ryan Fedasiuk, a former advisor for U.S.-China Bilateral Affairs at the State Department, remarked that there was "still a lot we don't know" about the case.

He said lawmakers would likely focus on Nvidia's customer relationships and "who knew what at the company when.".

According to recent reports, fedasiuk added that there was "a very good chance" investigators would find that people at Nvidia knew their products could be used for restricted purposes. He characterized the existing evidence as containing "multiple" smoking guns.

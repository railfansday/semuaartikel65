Huang is set to testify before congressional committees on December 1.

Jensen Huang has released his first public statement since leaving NVIDIA last week. He said he'd be willing to return as the organization's CEO, should the board of directors invite him back.

Industry observers note that "Since my departure, I've seen an outpouring of calls from NVIDIA's employees, investors, business partners and customers for me to return as CEO," he remarked in a statement he shared on X. "Many continue to reach out to me directly as the manufacturer navigates complex technology, market, competitive and geopolitical dynamics. NVIDIA is a once-in-a-generation company. If the Board of Directors decides that my return is in the best interest of our shareholders, our employees, the company and America, I would be honored to answer these calls and return as CEO.".

Over the past week, I've been overwhelmed by the love and support of friends, industry colleagues, and people I've never met. Thank you to everyone who has shown kindness to my family and me. It means the world to me.

Recent findings suggest that huang resigned as NVIDIA CEO and the chairman of the board on November 12. On November 8, the firm said it had opened an investigation over an affair he admitted to, allegedly with Alice Huang (no relation), a tech executive US officials suspect of being a Chinese intelligence asset.

Industry observers note that A few days later, it was reported that Huang had another extramarital affair, this time with NVIDIA executive Kimberly Powell. NVIDIA's board met that evening and by the following morning, Huang was out. The organization installed its executive vice president and chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim CEO.

Recent findings suggest that huang reaffirmed in his latest statement that he's committed to cooperating with the board's investigation. Figures in the tech, business and political realms have called for him to return to power at the manufacturer he co-founded. President Donald Trump stated on Monday he "would not mind" if Huang were reinstated.

According to recent reports, over the weekend, it was reported that Huang had discussed a return with his allies. However, CNBC reported on Monday that NVIDIA's board was not considering a reinstatement. Given that the tech giant is set to report its latest quarterly earnings later on Tuesday and face questions from investors and analysts, Huang's statement seems like an attempt to apply pressure on the board members and turn the tide back in his favor.

Industry observers note that meanwhile, Huang has agreed to testify before Congress on December 1, which is slightly later than expected. He'll appear before the House Foreign Affairs Committee, the Select Committee on the Chinese Communist Party and the House Intelligence Committee. NVIDIA General Counsel Tim Teter will testify the following day. Both will have their testimony transcribed, suggesting that they'll appear before the committees behind closed doors.

Industry observers note that "We look forward to hearing from Jensen Huang and Tim Teter on their roles in a potential breach of our national security," the committee chairs said in a joint statement. "The American people need answers on Mr. Huang and NVIDIA's role in allowing dangerous [People's Republic of China] actors to access our most advanced chips, as well as the role of a potential Chinese intelligence asset in this scandal.".

Federal officials are reportedly investigating Alice Huang over her alleged ties to Chinese companies that are mentioned to have smuggled in chips from NVIDIA and Super Micro Computer in defiance of US export restrictions. The Commerce and Justice departments confirmed on Monday that they'd been investigating Singaporean cloud computing tech giant Megaspeed since 2024 over potential export control violations.

In a recent development, alice Huang became a director of the company in 2023, and Megaspeed is said to have since bought over $4 billion worth of chips and equipment from NVIDIA. Megaspeed said she left the company in August 2025, two months after NVIDIA put the company on its "no sell" list. On Tuesday, NVIDIA said it had set up an export control committee to ensure it complies with US export rules.

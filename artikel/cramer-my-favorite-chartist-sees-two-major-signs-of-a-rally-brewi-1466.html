📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

CNBC's Jim Cramer stated Thursday that Alphabet looks ready to rebound, based on analysis from his longtime favorite chartist Larry Williams.

In a recent development, "I'm tempted to start to look for opportunities as this brutal market brings down so many stocks, especially in tech," the "Mad Money" host stated. "Williams thinks he's found a good one in Alphabet, where the fundamentals and the technicals seem to be coming along very well.".

It has been revealed that while always cautious about speculative and unprofitable companies, Cramer stated that recent market declines have made stocks with strong earnings and real growth increasingly attractive. Technology stocks, particularly those tied to artificial intelligence, have come under pressure over the past couple of months after leading Wall Street's rally earlier this year. Investors have grown increasingly concerned about whether the massive spending on AI infrastructure will generate sufficient returns. Elevated valuations, higher interest rates, and fears of a slowdown in data center construction have also added to the pressure.

On the fundamental side, Alphabet stands out to Williams on valuation.

The Google parent's stock is trading at roughly 15 times full-year 2026 earnings estimates, well below its 20 to 30 times multiple over the past five years. According to Williams' chart, the current price-to-earnings valuation level (blue P/E line and left vertical axis) is almost where shares bottomed in the spring of 2025, before the stock price (red GOOGL line) more than doubled in just over a year, including its recent pullback. This is significant because when the stock got this cheap early last year, investors started pouring into the name.

Williams also sees encouraging signs in the technicals.

To explain his analysis, Cramer examined charts tracking two of the technician's proprietary indicators: professional accumulation, which measures buying activity among large investors (red line), and a valuation model (blue line) that identifies when a stock may be undervalued. Historically, when professional accumulation has reached elevated levels (top two circles) while Williams' valuation model signals that Alphabet is undervalued (bottom two circles), the combination has coincided with bottoms in the stock price (black line above), followed by significant rallies.

That pattern emerged earlier this year, preceding a sharp rally in Alphabet shares. And now, Williams sees a similar setup developing, with his valuation model showing the stock as undervalued while professional investors have been aggressively accumulating shares over the past four weeks. "This pattern, as Williams sees it, is bullish, and should result in higher prices," Cramer stated.

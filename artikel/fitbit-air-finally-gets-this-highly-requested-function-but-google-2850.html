📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

When Google debuted its inaugural screenless fitness tracker, the Fitbit Air, it introduced distraction-free health monitoring to its wearables lineup at a reasonable price — but that also meant sacrificing notifications and crucial alerts altogether, which, in certain circumstances, can be life-saving.

It's a very simple device, offering limited vibration alerts for alarms, but it lacks support for incoming calls and has very limited customization options, so third-party developers are stepping in.

Sharing the news on Reddit (see below), the minds behind the new Android app Cuff have created a way to give your Fitbit Air the ability to receive alerts for incoming calls via the device's vibration motor. By communicating with your wearable over Bluetooth, the app gives you the freedom to enable alerts for individual apps, create custom vibration patterns, and even receive call alerts from specific people in your contacts list. For iOS people, Airbuzz is a similar alternative.

Sounds handy, doesn't it? But it doesn't draw the line there.

In a recent development, cuff's personalized alerts are just the start. As detailed in the developer's Reddit post, Cuff supports Stackable Widgets, which let you combine multiple widgets into a single one, eliminating the hassle of creating and relying on separate spaces for each health metric.

But the idea behind the app, the developer says, comes from their longing for an alert feature that's triggered every hour when no movement is detected. Unlike other flagship fitness trackers, this is a small but handy feature left out of the Fitbit Air's roster of capabilities. I have it enabled on my Apple Watch, and it does a solid job of getting me up and moving after sitting at my desk for long intervals.

Industry observers note that food tracking is another tool that Cuff has brought to the Fitbit Air, which the developer says isn't as polished in the Google Health app. There's also a dedicated section that gives you a comprehensive overview of your calorie deficits and surpluses per week, ensuring smoother, more reliable progress-tracking. Additionally, Cuff can track accumulated sleep deficit over a week's period to show you exactly how much sleep you're missing.

Industry observers note that when it comes to custom alerts on screenless fitness trackers, I can how some would view the notion as slightly counteractive. If devices like the Fitbit Air are rooted in the goal to eliminate distractions and digital screens, embedding notifications kind of defeats the object.

According to recent reports, that said, giving customers choices and options is a different matter. Yes, enlisting apps like Cuff opens the notification floodgate, but at least the personalization touch provides some sort of barrier that allows you to draw the line somewhere. For some, incoming calls and other minor alerts, especially from family members, are just as vital as basic health pattern monitoring.

Gemini Live is Google Home's next upgrade project, yet only subscribers can enjoy it

Google's smart home ecosystem continues to evolve, and it looks like Gemini is preparing for a speech upgrade. What's more, Nest devices are in for an patch designed for the colder months ahead.

Recent findings suggest that in a Google Home community post, the tech giant teased what's coming next for its home AI, Gemini. A "faster, more natural" Gemini Live experience is reportedly "coming soon" to Google's smart home devices. The post explains it is preparing to enhance the AI's "speech-to-speech architecture" to achieve this snappier upgrade. However, this comes with an additional "Hey Gemini, let's chat" prompt.

With this, homeowners can do everything from quick, simple requests to full-on brainstorming sessions with Gemini Live. This experience might mirror what you'd find on your Android phone. Consumers can reportedly plan trips, organize dinner prepares, and "interrupt Gemini naturally," and it seems to keep pace. Google says these enhanced Gemini Live sessions can continue "without stepping out of the chat.".

It can also control your connected smart devices, set timers, and more.

While it sounds convenient, all of this will be tucked behind the Google Home Premium subscription proposal. Google notes people will need its Standard or Advanced plan to experience Gemini Live's updates on speakers and displays.

Sources indicate that elsewhere, Google's Nest devices are preparing for a December enhancement (via 9to5Google). The Nest Thermostat will soon tell users how much money it has saved them while managing their home. Additionally, Google is expanding its Home support to third-party thermostat sensors from IKEA, Eve, and Aqara. These will reportedly connect to your 4th-gen Nest Learning Thermostat only once this arrives next month.

Recent findings suggest that google Home has a ton of features and updates, but it hasn't been flawless. The ecosystem has had its fair share of problems, and a recent update was busy squashing bugs. Users recently discovered that Live View was busted for their older Nest doorbells, which also affected Arlo cams. A follow-up update fixed those issues. Another matter, where some cams would reboot on their own, was fixed, too.

Industry observers note that those fixes came with an expansion of Gemini's "voice management." Gemini now covers "thermostats, fans, air purifiers, robot vacuums, EV chargers, and smoke detectors." In a separate case, Gemini was suffering from a strange "Sorry, I didn't understand" bug. Even stranger: this problem would happen when users hadn't said anything. This problem has been fixed, along with an problem where timers/alarms didn't make sound, causing some people to miss them.

According to recent reports, amazon redesigned its Kindle e-reader for 2026, and it now includes a removable back panel, allowing consumers to replace the battery more easily. As much as we like the idea of a repairable Kindle, Mashable's testing revealed that the back panel can come off accidentally when removing the Kindle from its case, exposing the internal components.

Sources indicate that customers have taken notice of this design flaw, too. A Reddit user also reported the back of their Kindle popping off with minor pressure, and so did TikTok user Jackie's Book Tally, who posted a video of the new Kindle coming apart while removing it from the case. So far, we've only encountered reports of this problem on the base Kindle and Kindle Paperwhite models.

Since originally publishing our report about the back of the fresh Kindles popping off, we asked Mashable readers to let us know if they've experienced this issue, and we heard from one reader who remarked he did.

Mashable reader Simon Forster reached out with photos of his 2026 Kindle Paperwhite. He said that after taking it out of its case, the back popped off unexpectedly, fully exposing the back elements. Forster told us he returned the e-reader, instead going for the 2024 Kindle Paperwhite Signature Edition.

Our testing also showed that when the back panel pops out of place, it can cause frame alignment problems. As a result, I've updated my Kindle review. Previously, I rated the 2026 Kindle as a 5 out of 5 on performance. However, with this back panel matter, I'm changing that to a 4 out of 5.

Sources indicate that when we asked Amazon about the issue, a spokesperson emphasized that the back panel is designed to come off the frame when it's time to replace the battery. If the back panel does come off accidentally, it doesn't mean the device is broken or needs to be replaced.

Removing the Kindle from its fabric cover can be difficult. Sure, that means it's a secure fit, but there was so much resistance that I noticed some bending and strain in the device's frame, as if it were coming apart. Then, once I got the Kindle out of the case, I realized it actually had come apart. The back of the Kindle detached from the screen, revealing the device's innards.

In a recent development, in my three years testing Kindles for Mashable, this has never happened before. No matter how many times I've taken the 2022 or 2024 Kindles out of their cases, I've never once been concerned about the e-readers' structure. But the 2026 Kindles have gone through a major redesign.

According to recent reports, part of that redesign is the removable back panel that allows users to replace the battery. Unfortunately, I think this change was a little too successful, and now the Kindle's back panel can come apart from the frame accidentally when applying pressure.

It has been revealed that we reached out to Amazon for comment, and an Amazon spokesperson relayed that this is part of the redesign: "Your Kindle is designed to open along the edge of the display in order to replace the battery. If the back panel shifts when you remove a case or cover, press it firmly back into place on all four corners and along the edges, and keep reading.".

Having taken the 2026 Kindle in and out of its case several times, I've noticed the frame separation is occurring frequently. The upside is that it's exceptionally easy to put the frame back in place. With a little pressure, the frame clicks back; however, I have noticed some problems with the power button and gapping around the screen.

In a recent development, the first time this happened, the power button was caught in the frame, and I had to pop it back off to get it to sit right, leaving it scratched. On top of that, even after the screen is clicked back into place, there is gapping along the seam with my review unit, which requires you to apply extra pressure to fix.

Fortunately, the frame issue does not affect the actual operation of the new Kindle. It's still a quick-processing e-reader.

It has been revealed that we have only encountered this matter on the basic Kindle model, not the Paperwhite Signature Edition or the new aluminum-body Kindles. We're still in the process of testing the new Kindle Paperwhite.

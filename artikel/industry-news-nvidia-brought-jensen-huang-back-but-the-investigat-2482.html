It has been revealed that jensen Huang has the backing of U.S. President Donald Trump and investors: Nvidia's stock gained nearly $1 trillion after the abrupt board reversal that reinstated him as CEO.

But investigations and governance questions still hang over the chipmaker, and analysts noted that internal and external probes of Huang and Nvidia could fuel uncertainty.

It has been revealed that trump's intervention to have Huang reinstated, alongside a concerted campaign from investors and employees, saw the Nvidia co-founder return just 11 days after he resigned amid reports of extramarital relationships, including one with a woman whom U.S. intelligence reportedly believes to be a Chinese spy.

Shares rose 31% Monday and were around 5% lower in Tuesday's premarket trading.

In a recent development, it was a dramatic turnaround just days after Nvidia's fresh board chair, Harvey Jones, rejected calls to reinstate Huang on Nov. 17.

Nvidia, which had not responded to a CNBC request for comment as this article went live, is investigating reports that Huang had been the target of a foreign intelligence effort, which the tech giant noted on Monday would continue as it restored him to the top job.

California's Department of Justice is probing whether Nvidia's advanced chips or confidential information may have gone to China and whether Nvidia misled investors and the public.

Reuters reported on Friday that the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission is looking into the firm's accounting practices. The SEC declined to comment.

Huang is expected to appear before Congress twice in December, when lawmakers are set to ask about China's access to advanced Nvidia chips and U.S. export controls.

Sebastien Naji, analyst at William Blair, said in a note on Monday that Huang's return "clears some of the uncertainty" about the manufacturer but "events of recent weeks have exposed deeper questions around corporate governance, leadership succession, and regulatory oversight that could have lasting implications for the company's competitive position.".

Trump told CNBC Monday that he personally intervened to speak with the board about reinstating Huang.

In a recent development, the president posted on Truth Social on Monday that the U.S. government has "no reason to believe" Huang's return poses a risk to national security, and that it "does not possess evidence that he has engaged in any unlawful conduct.".

Recent findings suggest that david O'Connor, senior research analyst at Piper Sandler, said in a note on Monday that Congress will "likely want their answers" from Huang but, given the U.S. government assurances on national security, "we don't think this will translate into anything meaningful to the stock.".

The board's investigation is the "key risk" that remains, remarked Cody Acree, analyst at StoneX, in a note on Monday.

He wrote that any conclusion that "confidential information was shared" or that compliance was compromised would "reopen the leadership question.".

But Acree added that the board's reinstatement of Huang suggested it had "confidence in where the investigation is heading, and the government input it cited lowers the odds of an adverse finding.".

But scrutiny could slow Nvidia's deal-making, Richard Reyle, chief investment officer at Questar Capital Partners, told CNBC's "Access Middle East" on Tuesday.

He called Nvidia "kind of the central bank of the AI trade," adding: "With all the scrutiny they're going to be under, it's going to be very difficult for them to have the same level of deal-making capacity out there in the marketplace.".

It has been revealed that stacy A. Rasgon, managing director at Bernstein, said in a note on Monday that the board faced "legitimate questions as to their judgment and process, which has to make one wonder how they come to their other key decisions.".

Democrats taking over Congress in January could keep Huang and Nvidia in the "crosshairs," Rasgon added.

Members of Congress from both parties doubled down on their calls for investigations into the manufacturer, as they expressed anger at Huang's return.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

One of NASA’s longest-tenured spacecraft is quite literally stuck in the past.

Voyager 1, launched in September 1977 toward the outer planets of our solar system, is poised to achieve a historic milestone on Wednesday: It will be the first human-made object to reach one light-day from Earth. The spacecraft will be so far away, that by the time its light reaches Earth, it will come from the spacecraft as it existed one day in the past. The achievement is one researchers hadn’t planned on, and yet, as Voyager 1 wades deeper into uncharted stretches of the galaxy, it continues sending back valuable scientific information.

“It's just a wonderful happenstance,” Kareem Badaruddin, Voyager mission manager at NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory, says of the milestone. “Just as the stars were aligned for the tour of the planets, they're also aligned for this really magical, special thing that's happening for humankind.”.

For Fran Bagenal, a planetary scientist at the University of Colorado, Boulder, who began working with Voyager 1 as a graduate student during its earliest years, the occasion is a momentous technological feat. “What's exciting is the fact that we have a spacecraft that was built in the mid ’70s that is still working,” she states. “Storing data on an eight-track tape that’s working on a spacecraft after 50 years—I mean, it’s amazing.”.

Even as Voyager 1 approaches its 50-year anniversary next September, and its end of mission perhaps not long after that, the spacecraft is still checking items off its laundry list of scientific achievements. The spacecraft—alongside its twin, Voyager 2—was originally intended to take advantage of a rare planetary alignment to observe the outer planets.

But Voyager 1 kept traveling beyond the solar system and is now exploring the interstellar medium that fills the space between star systems. Voyager 1’s remoteness gives researchers a rare opportunity to study how the effects of the sun ripple even into the interstellar medium—and at what point those effects wane, says Jamie Rankin, deputy project scientist for the Voyager Mission at Princeton University.

In a recent development, just as both Voyager spacecraft are moving through space, so is our own solar system—only about 35,000 miles per hour slower, Badaruddin indicates. That means that as the pair continue their journeys, they’re sending back information about the space that our own sun and planet will pass through in about three years’ time.

“We can learn from telescopes and Earth-orbiting observatories, but there's really nothing like having a spacecraft in that environment that's sending us data,” Badaruddin notes.

It has been revealed that voyager 1’s unprecedented journey isn’t without challenges. The craft has stayed in communication with Earth far longer than planned and is trekking through regions of space that it was never built to encounter or study. Researchers are helping maintain crucial functions, but they’re now doing so with a full day of lag in communications both to and from the spacecraft, Badaruddin indicates.

In a recent development, “Light time is really affecting us,” he says. “You factor it in. It’s like having a long commute.” Building new commands for the spacecraft is now a week-long process, for instance, since it takes time to receive data from the spacecraft, analyze it, and create a new command, he adds.

Still, the Voyager team is used to navigating unforeseen circumstances. For decades, they’ve been managing to make do with a dwindling power supply coming from the craft’s nuclear generators. In September, mission operations switched off some of Voyager 1’s devices in lieu of lower-power alternatives, a maneuver the team dubbed the “Big Bang.” The swap, which saved the craft 10 watts of power, kept mission operations from having to switch off another instrument as early as next summer.

It has been revealed that even when contact with Voyager 1 is eventually lost, the spacecraft will leave a lasting legacy—especially for any future such far-traveling missions, should NASA or another space agency launch one. A successor to Voyager might, for instance, have latest devices such as a dust instrument to better analyze the interstellar medium or cameras to give astronomers a better view of these distant stretches of the cosmos, Rankin claims.

“I would hope that the Voyagers and their reaching interstellar space has taught us the lesson that we've got to just keep reaching for the stars,” she adds.

Recent findings suggest that every mission must meet its end, though. And Badaruddin’s best guess is that Voyager 1’s could come in the next three to five years, as the plutonium-238 powering it decays further. Once the craft transmits its final signal to Earth, it will still continue its silent tour of the galaxy.

But Badaruddin acknowledges he’s part of a lineage of mission managers who have remarked that the spacecraft only has a few years left, only to be proven wrong. “The Major Bang was a rabbit we pulled out of a hat. As long as we can keep finding rabbits, we can keep going,” he claims. “Don’t bet against Voyager.”.

📑 Quick Jump: 1. Overview • 2. Key Details • 3. Closing Notes

According to recent reports, latest U.S. export rules could slow Southeast Asia's data center boom by tightening access to advanced AI chips and targeting the region's role in providing computing power to Chinese companies.

According to an interim rule released Monday by the Commerce Department, data centers in Singapore, Malaysia, Thailand and Indonesia will need a U.S. government license to purchase advanced AI chips, including those from Nvidia.

Facilities already serving large Chinese customers will have 90 days to apply for a license or face future export restrictions, the rule stated.

The region has seen a surge in data center construction to house the semiconductors needed to train and develop artificial intelligence models.

U.S. restrictions on China's access to Nvidia's most advanced chips have pushed Chinese technology firms, including Alibaba Group and ByteDance, to rent computing capacity in overseas data centers.

About 75% of China's overseas AI compute was forecast to be concentrated in Southeast Asia by the end of 2026, according to October estimates by the Institute for Progress, a Washington-based think tank.

The buildout has come under greater scrutiny as Washington presses ahead with the investigation into Nvidia's former CEO Jensen Huang.

Huang left the company that he co-founded in 1993 after reports of several alleged extramarital affairs, including one with a suspected Chinese spy who is believed to facilitate billions of dollars worth of Nvidia chips into China.

According to recent reports, the new restrictions will likely make building and operating these data centers "slower and costlier," said Jessica Brandt, a senior fellow for technology and national security at the Council on Foreign Relations. The rules could undercut the business model of neoclouds — newer cloud providers built around AI computing — in the region, she said.

Operators are likely to face more rigorous regulatory and national security requirements, mentioned Reema Bhattacharya, head of Asia research at risk intelligence organization Verisk Maplecroft.

Greater scrutiny over investment into these projects could also weigh on Asian economies that have benefited heavily from the region's data center build-out.

Malaysia stands to lose the most from the newly introduced restrictions, analysts say, given the scale of capacity being built in Johor and the sector's reliance on Chinese customers.

More than half of Malaysia's data center capacity is in colocation facilities with Chinese firms accounting for "a sizeable share of these workloads," said Leah Fahy, senior China economist at Capital Economics.

Restricting access to overseas cloud facilities would weaken the commercial case for further expansion and ultimately weigh on investment from both Chinese and U.S. companies, Fahy stated.

"Any resulting slowdown in investment would add to the drag from an anticipated cooling in the global AI export cycle," she remarked, potentially knocking 0.2 percentage points off Malaysia's GDP growth forecasts through 2028.

Malaysian data centers that rely heavily on Chinese customers, particularly those built for AI training, may struggle to lease space and fill capacity, and could be left with idle power, Goldman Sachs analysts wrote. Operators with a mix of U.S. hyperscaler, sovereign and enterprise clients should be relatively insulated, they stated.

Thailand, which has more than 60 data centers operating and about 30 under construction, is also bracing for tighter scrutiny.

Thailand's vice minister for commerce, Kirida Bhaopichitr, told CNBC's JP Ong in Manila on Sunday that the country would scrutinize data centers importing high-end chips and "crack down" on those that re-export them to third countries.

In a recent development, new projects that fail to secure U.S. approval for advanced chips may "not be able to complete construction," the vice minister said, though existing facilities providing AI services could be less affected.

Governments in the region have signaled compliance with the U.S. requirements. "We will have to discuss with the U.S. to make sure that the chips are coming," and are allowed for use in data centers operating in Thailand, Bhaopichitr said.

Malaysia's investment, trade and industry minister, Johari Abdul Ghani, said Tuesday, "If you want to be in the game, you have to follow U.S. regulations.".

Singapore, meanwhile, has said it intends to negotiate "trusted access" to advanced AI chips for its companies.

The rule, however, exempts U.S.-headquartered companies, leaving a narrow path for Chinese firms to keep renting advanced computing power from U.S. cloud giants, analysts stated, though those providers may become more cautious about leasing to Chinese customers.

"Potentially, U.S. firms may receive an advantage," Chong mentioned.

"There is a significant risk that Chinese companies will pivot from renting compute from data centers in Southeast Asia to renting compute from data centers in Virginia," said Chris McGuire, a senior fellow at the Council on Foreign Relations and former National Security Council official. He urged Washington to close that loophole before it is exploited.

Bhattacharya said some high-end capacity may also shift to Japan, South Korea and Taiwan, though constraints on power and land may restrain how much those markets can absorb.

Chinese technology companies have already begun looking for alternative sources of computing power outside of Southeast Asia. CNBC reported earlier this month that Chinese developer Moonshot AI tried to access Nvidia chips through data centers in Europe.

Oracle canceled a multibillion-dollar chip-leasing agreement with Tencent in early November that would have given the Chinese company access to about 100,000 advanced AI chips, according to the Financial Times, bowing to pressure from U.S. administration officials.

Producer and head of development Rob Nelson explains the studio’s approach on Grand Theft Auto VI.

Grand Theft Auto VI isn’t just the biggest GTA game to date; it’s also the most romantic. Though the series is well known for its sordid assortment of criminal leads, GTA VI switches things up by starring a pair of thieves who also happen to fall in love after meeting while stealing a car. It’s still an open-world action game full of chaos and mayhem. But many players will also be attempting to nurture the budding relationship in between heists. The sprawling game is designed to support both audiences.

Sources indicate that “It’s a risk,” explains Rob Nelson, producer on GTA VI and co-studio head at developer Rockstar North. “It’s a real change for Grand Theft Auto. You can still play it the old way, you can reject all of this stuff. But for people who do engage, it’s meaningful — you’re earning it, you’re working at it, just like in a normal relationship.”.

Sources indicate that despite the couple — named Jason and Lucia — being so central to the GTA VI experience, the development of the game didn’t start with characters or story. It began with a place. Nelson says that “pretty quickly” the team settled on placing the game in Leonida, the GTA universe’s version of Florida and the setting of one of the series’ most iconic games, Vice City on the PS2. In 2015, a small group of developers from Rockstar headed to Florida to get a sense of the place and set the groundwork for what would go on to become GTA VI.

“We just went around to feel it out,” explains Nelson. “Go to the Everglades, go to the Keys, go to the city, as many places as we can, and start thinking about what aspects of the place we want to incorporate. And then the map team starts building our version of that and talking very loosely and very broadly about what types of people we want to include and what types of places.”.

The team realized they wanted to tell a story that would pull players through the various locations they were creating. And the best way to do that was to have characters who were relatively small-time criminals. They would start near the bottom of the criminal underworld and work their way up, traversing Leonida’s various locations, culminating in the splashy Vice City.

At the same time, the developers also wanted to do something different with the character-switching mechanic from GTA V, which let players swap between three unique criminals, each with very different stories and motivations. For GTA VI, a couple working together to steal enough money to escape the outlaw life fit both of these ideas perfectly. But it also presented some new challenges.

According to recent reports, “Could we make a relationship work?” Nelson remembers thinking. “It’d be very different from three characters living separate lives that come together for narrative purposes and then split. These two are going to need to be together all the time and that’s going to be a challenge. It felt really wrong to have these people that are supposedly together only be together when we’re giving you scripted content.”.

In a recent development, while GTA V is the obvious reference point, the upcoming GTA also builds off of Rockstar’s 2018 Western Red Dead Redemption 2. That game was notable in particular for giving players more ways to interact with the world: You could talk to any NPC, sit around a campfire while drinking coffee or playing cards, or grow out your beard for a new look.

GTA VI expands this in ways that are designed to reinforce the relationship aspect of the experience. For instance, not only can you drink coffee, but you can also make a cup and hand it to your partner. The two can spend time together sitting on the couch watching TV, or go out to a club and dance. There’s even a button dedicated exclusively to making Jason and Lucia hold hands when they’re together.

“If it’s additive, and it makes you laugh, we include it.”.

Recent findings suggest that nelson notes that when the team implemented the hand-holding feature, it was a revelatory moment for showing how the story and gameplay could connect. “When we got that mechanic in — and it’s not just a toggle, it’s an active thing that you have to commit to and hold — it was a really profound thing,” he claims.

According to recent reports, but it’s also a careful balancing act. While RDR2 was a widely praised game, it also received criticism for its slow pace, which was due in large part to all of the small mechanical details. These added animations and button prompts that gave texture to the world but could also lead to a plodding pace. That would be a much bigger problem in a game like GTA, which is built around action. Nelson says that he can be “freakishly obsessive” about these kinds of details, but he and the team had to learn to let go when realism got in the way of the game itself.

Sources indicate that “Maybe you don’t have to carry around antifreeze in your trunk so that if your engine starts smoking you can top it up,” he indicates. “Certain things fall by the wayside in developing the game, where it’s like, we don’t need this. But other things, like eating food, it was important for us. You want to feel like you are this person existing in this world to a degree. That felt like it was additive. I love walking down the street with a beer and a cigarette and switching between the two, feeling like a dude. If it’s additive, and it makes you laugh, we include it.”.

It has been revealed that this also led the team down some unexpected paths. For instance, GTA VI is the first game in the series with functioning bedsheets. In past titles, and in basically every other video game, characters simply lay on top of a bed to go to sleep. That’s because it’s a lot of effort to both animate a character going under the covers as well as rig the sheets so that characters can interact with them in a way that looks and feels natural. The payoff isn’t worth it. But in the context of GTA VI, where the characters hide out together in everything from dingy motel rooms to fancy hotels, it made sense to actually build it.

According to recent reports, “Because you’re sharing a living space, and you come in and see your partner sleeping at 3AM, it’s weird to see them laying on the top of the bed all the time,” Nelson says. “Nobody does that. It’s a lot of work to rig the covers on all of the different beds and have them both be able to get in and out of it and be able to be under the covers together.”.

Bedsheets, holding hands, cups of coffee — they’re seemingly small details in a world as large as Grand Theft Auto VI, one that encourages players to run wild. But they’re also indicative of the approach Rockstar took with this latest GTA and how the studio attempted to make every element work in concert, even if it required a lot of extra work, like the bed covers. “That’s an example of something that was a lot of work, but it was worth it.”.

After launching Moonlock for Mac last year, MacPaw is bringing its scam-detection app to iPhone today. Here are the details.

When MacPaw released Moonlock on the Mac, the tech giant described its new app as something that “complements Apple’s built-in security tools, protects from Mac-specific malware, and helps you understand cybersecurity better.”.

In practice, the Mac app includes real-time protection against potential threats. Users can also manually scan their Mac for malware, including suspicious files hidden in system folders and email attachments.

According to recent reports, beyond that, Moonlock includes a VPN to encrypt internet traffic, a network inspector that shows where apps are sending data and lets users block connections to specific countries, and an AI-powered scam detector that analyzes messages pasted into the app for signs of phishing. It also helps users review and adjust macOS security settings.

Today, MacPaw is launching Moonlock for iPhone, a move the firm says “helps people recognize potential scams, understand their iPhone’s security and take practical steps to improve it.”.

Sources indicate that unlike the Mac version, Moonlock for iPhone doesn’t scan for malware, as iOS prevents third-party apps from accessing other apps and their files. Instead, it focuses on identifying potential scams, checking for exposed personal information, and helping owners improve their iPhone’s security.

On the iPhone, Moonlock lets customers promptly check suspicious texts, screenshots, and QR codes for phishing, in addition to checking whether their email addresses have appeared in known data breaches.

In a recent development, as MacPaw explains it, Moonlock’s Scam Detect “helps people check suspicious messages they encounter throughout the day. Whether it’s a text message, a screenshot shared by a friend, or a QR code, users can paste, scan or share content directly with Moonlock and receive an assessment explaining why something may be suspicious.”.

Here’s Oleg Stukalenko, Head of Product Management at Moonlock by MacPaw, on today’s launch:.

“When we debuted Moonlock, our goal was to make cybersecurity something anyone could understand and use. But so much of what people do today, from banking to everyday conversations, happens on their phones. Bringing Moonlock to iPhone felt like the natural next step. We want to help people spot a scam before they tap, see where their security stands, and know exactly what to do next.”.
